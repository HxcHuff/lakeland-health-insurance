What to know first

  • The plan pays a set amount for each covered service, not the full medical bill.
  • It includes limited benefits for certain hospital, doctor, prescription, and imaging services.
  • Preferred 4000 is the middle scheduled-benefit option in the Health ProtectorGuard Core series.
  • Health questions, limits, exclusions, and pre-existing-condition rules apply.

The “4000” refers to a $4,000 fixed benefit for each covered day of hospital confinement. It is not a deductible or a promise to pay your full bill.

Reviews and what to verify before you enroll

Preferred 4000 may be worth comparing if you already have major medical coverage and want supplemental help with certain expenses. It is also one of three Core options with different scheduled-benefit levels.

A Florida review should confirm the current premium, ZIP-code availability, medical underwriting, benefit limits, and exclusions before you apply. It should not replace ACA, Medicare, Medicaid, employer, or other comprehensive health coverage. The issued policy controls.

Preferred 4000 vs Select 2000 and Premier 5000

Preferred 4000 is the middle scheduled-benefit Core option. Select 2000 provides lower scheduled benefits for some covered services, while Premier 5000 provides higher scheduled benefits for some covered services. Premiums and limits may also differ.

The right option depends on the care you expect to use, the coverage you already have, current rates, and what you could still owe.

Frequently asked questions

Is Health ProtectorGuard real insurance?

Yes. Health ProtectorGuard is fixed-indemnity insurance underwritten by Golden Rule Insurance Company, a UnitedHealthcare company. It pays limited, scheduled amounts for covered services. It is not major medical insurance and is not Minimum Essential Coverage under the ACA.

Does it cover pre-existing conditions?

Current materials describe a 12-month lookback and a pre-existing-condition limitation that can apply for up to 12 months after coverage begins. The issued policy controls.

Is Preferred 4000 major medical insurance?

No. Preferred 4000 is fixed-indemnity insurance that pays limited, set benefits for covered services. It is not Minimum Essential Coverage under the ACA and should not replace comprehensive health insurance.

Will Preferred 4000 pay my full medical bill?

Not necessarily. If the provider's charge is more than the plan's scheduled benefit, you may owe the difference.

Do health questions and pre-existing-condition rules apply?

Yes. The plan is subject to medical underwriting. Current materials describe a 12-month lookback and a pre-existing-condition limitation that can apply for up to 12 months after coverage begins. The issued policy controls.
