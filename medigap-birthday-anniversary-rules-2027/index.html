Frequently Asked Questions

Do Medigap Plan G benefits change when I switch insurance carriers under a Birthday or Anniversary Rule?

No. Standardized Medigap benefits are set by federal law by plan letter. A standardized Plan G from one insurer covers the exact same Medicare benefits, including the 2027 Part A inpatient hospital deductible of $1,776 per benefit period and 20% Part B coinsurance after the annual Part B deductible, as Plan G from another insurer. Only the monthly premium and rate-increase block differ.

Which licensed states allow switching Medigap carriers without health underwriting in 2027?

Across David Huff's 23 licensed states, seven states offer guaranteed-issue Medigap carrier switching rules for existing policyholders: Washington (year-round switching for Plans B through N), Indiana (60-day Birthday Rule), Virginia (60-day Birthday Rule), Missouri (60-day Policy Anniversary Rule), Maryland (30-day Birthday Rule), Oklahoma (60-day Birthday Rule), and Louisiana (annual Birthday Rule window per carrier filing).

Does switching Medigap carriers also update my 2027 Part D prescription drug plan?

No. Medigap policies do not include retail prescription drug coverage. Reviewing your Medigap rate is an ideal time to check your standalone 2027 Part D drug plan against the $2,400 annual out-of-pocket cap and up to $700 defined-standard deductible during your applicable Part D enrollment window.

Does Florida have a Medigap Birthday Rule in 2027?

No. Florida does not have an annual Medigap Birthday Rule or Policy Anniversary Rule. Outside your initial 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period or a federal guaranteed-issue trigger (such as a Medicare Advantage plan non-renewal), switching Medigap carriers in Florida generally requires medical underwriting.
