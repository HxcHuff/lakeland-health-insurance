Stand-alone drug plan or drug coverage inside Medicare Advantage?

Standalone Part D with Original Medicare / Medigap

If you have Original Medicare, you join a stand-alone Part D plan for drug coverage unless you have other creditable drug coverage. Medigap does not replace Part D. You can keep or change that stand-alone plan during Annual Enrollment.

Drug coverage inside Medicare Advantage

Most Medicare Advantage plans include Part D. You still verify the formulary, tiers, prior authorization, quantity limits, and pharmacy network for the exact 2027 plan. Switching from a stand-alone Part D plan to a Medicare Advantage plan (or the reverse) is a medical-coverage decision as well as a drug decision. See the Medicare hub and the Polk County Annual Enrollment checklist.

The $2,400 out-of-pocket cap: what it does and doesn’t cover

The $2,400 figure is a cap on covered Part D drug costs for 2027. It is not a cap on the monthly premium. It does not make every drug cheap before you reach the cap, and it does not apply to drugs the plan does not cover. Confirm excluded drugs, insulin rules, and what counts toward the threshold in the plan documents and on Medicare.gov.

How to check each drug: tier, prior authorization, quantity limits

Use Medicare Plan Finder or the plan’s 2027 formulary. For each medication, confirm the drug name, strength, and form (tablet, capsule, inhaler, injection). Then check:

  • Whether it is on the formulary, and on which tier.
  • Prior authorization, step therapy, or quantity limits.
  • The deductible that applies to that tier, if any.
  • The pharmacy you actually use.

This page does not list copays. Those amounts belong on the current plan documents. Work through the prescription check before you enroll, 2027 prescription-cost checklist, and the provider and prescription workflow.

Pharmacy matters: preferred vs. standard in Polk

Two pharmacies a mile apart can price the same drug differently on the same plan. Preferred pharmacies usually cost less than standard network pharmacies. Mail-order and specialty pharmacies are separate checks. Look up the exact store name and ZIP for Lakeland, Winter Haven, or elsewhere in Polk on the 2027 plan. If your pharmacy is out of network, ask whether a 30-day transition fill exists and how long it lasts. Do not assume last year’s preferred pharmacy stays preferred.

Late-enrollment penalty and creditable coverage

Medicare.gov says that as long as you have creditable prescription drug coverage, you can wait to join a Medicare drug plan or a Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage. You will not pay the Part D late enrollment penalty as long as you do not go more than 63 days without creditable drug coverage. The plan that provides the coverage has to tell you, and it sends that information every year. Keep the letter.

Employer, union, and some VA-style coverage may be creditable. Confirm the definition on Medicare.gov; this page does not invent extra exceptions. If you are still working at 65, start with working past 65 in Lakeland. For late Part A or Part B sign-up, see the late Medicare sign-up penalty.

Extra Help changes the math

Extra Help can reduce or eliminate the Part D premium and deductible and lower what you pay at the pharmacy. CMS notes that people in a Medicare Savings Program get Extra Help automatically. Without Extra Help, KFF’s 2027 finding applies: there is no $0 stand-alone drug plan. Medicare.gov also says people with Extra Help or Medicaid may change drug coverage once per calendar month.

Check current Extra Help and Medicare Savings Program limits on Medicare.gov and apply through Social Security or Florida DCF / ACCESS Florida. For local Medicare help, return to the Medicare hub.

AEP and other windows in Florida

Annual Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs October 15 through December 7, 2026. A change made in that window generally starts January 1. Outside that window, you need a Special Enrollment Period. Common examples include Extra Help or Medicaid (once per calendar month), moving out of the plan’s service area, or a plan that is not renewing. See when you can switch Medicare plans in Florida. Confirm the exact event on Medicare.gov.

Your Drug Moved to a Higher Tier or Off the List for 2027: How to Ask for an Exception

Direct answer: If your 2027 Annual Notice of Change puts a drug on a higher tier or off the formulary, you can ask the plan for a formulary or tiering exception. Your doctor sends clinical support. After the plan receives that statement, it must decide within 72 hours (standard) or 24 hours (expedited when waiting would seriously harm your health). Ask the plan if you qualify for expedited review. If the answer is no, follow the appeal steps on the denial notice or compare other 2027 plans during Annual Enrollment through December 7.

Tiering exception vs. formulary exception

A formulary exception asks the plan to cover a drug that is not on its list or to waive a restriction when alternatives on the list would not work as well for you. A tiering exception asks for lower cost sharing when the drug is covered but placed on a higher tier than similar options. CMS and Medicare.gov describe how these requests fit Part D rules. Plans may not offer tiering exceptions for specialty-tier drugs; check your plan’s notice.

What your doctor sends

Your prescriber provides a statement that formulary alternatives would not be as effective or would cause harm, or that the drug you need should be covered despite the list change. Include diagnosis and why listed options are inadequate for you. Submit through the plan’s exception process using the form, phone number, or portal in your Evidence of Coverage or Annual Notice of Change. Keep copies of what you send.

How fast the plan must answer

After the plan receives your prescriber's supporting statement, it must decide within 72 hours under standard review or within 24 hours under expedited review when waiting for a decision would seriously harm your health. Ask the plan if you qualify for expedited review. If you already paid at the pharmacy and the exception is approved, the plan must decide your reimbursement request within 14 calendar days.

If the plan says no

If the plan denies the exception, you may request a redetermination as the first level of appeal. The denial notice should explain how to start that request and what deadline applies—follow the steps and dates on that notice. You may still work with your prescriber on appeals or use official Medicare help channels on Medicare.gov, 1-800-MEDICARE, or your local State Health Insurance Program (SHIP).

Or compare plans during AEP

Annual Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs October 15 through December 7, 2026. If another stand-alone Part D plan or Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage lists your medications more favorably, switching may fit better than relying on an exception alone. Re-read what changes for Part D next year on this page, use the Polk County Annual Enrollment checklist, and confirm every drug, dose, and pharmacy on Medicare Plan Finder before you enroll.

Common questions

Do I need a new Part D plan if my premium went up?

Not automatically. A higher premium is a reason to compare, not a reason to stay or leave by itself. KFF reported that some 2027 stand-alone drug plan premiums rise $50 or more a month if the enrollee does not switch. Check every drug, dose, and pharmacy on Medicare Plan Finder, then compare total yearly cost.

Does the $2,400 cap include my premium?

No. CMS set the 2027 Part D out-of-pocket threshold at $2,400 for covered drug costs. The monthly plan premium is separate and does not count toward that cap. Confirm the current rule on Medicare.gov and in the plan’s Evidence of Coverage.

What if my pharmacy isn't preferred on the new plan?

You can still fill many prescriptions at a standard network pharmacy, but the cost is often higher than at a preferred pharmacy. Mail-order and specialty pharmacies follow separate rules. Check your exact pharmacy name and ZIP on the 2027 plan before you enroll.

What is the Part D late-enrollment penalty?

Medicare.gov says you can wait to join a Medicare drug plan if you have creditable prescription drug coverage, and you will not pay the late enrollment penalty as long as you do not go more than 63 days without creditable coverage. The penalty is added to your premium and can last as long as you have Part D. Confirm the current calculation on Medicare.gov.

Can I change drug plans after December 7?

Annual Enrollment ends December 7. After that, you need a Special Enrollment Period. Medicare.gov says people with Extra Help or Medicaid may change drug coverage once per calendar month. Other events, such as a move or a plan that is not renewing, have their own windows. Confirm the event that applies to you on Medicare.gov.

Will my drug cost less on Medicare in 2027, and does that change which plan I pick this fall?

Starting January 1, 2027, Medicare’s negotiated prices take effect for 15 more Part D drugs, including Ozempic/Wegovy, Trelegy, Xtandi and Janumet. A Milliman analysis estimates about 3% of non-EGWP Part D enrollees (people not in employer group Part D plans) could see lower 2027 out-of-pocket costs from the negotiated prices, and actual savers may be fewer. Plan choice still depends on each plan’s formulary and pharmacy network, so compare during Annual Enrollment.

What is a Medicare Part D formulary exception?

A formulary exception asks your drug plan to cover a medication that is not on its drug list or to waive a plan restriction, such as prior authorization or quantity limits, when alternatives on the list would not work as well. CMS describes the process on its prescription drug exceptions page. Approval is not guaranteed; the plan decides using Medicare rules and your doctor’s supporting statement.

What is a tiering exception on a Part D plan?

A tiering exception asks the plan to charge you a lower cost-sharing tier for a drug that is already on the formulary but placed on a higher tier than similar options. It is different from a formulary exception, which deals with coverage or restrictions on the list itself. Plans may not offer tiering exceptions for specialty-tier drugs; check your plan’s notice.

Who submits a Part D coverage exception request?

You or someone you authorize can ask the plan to start the request. Your doctor or other prescriber must send a statement explaining why covered alternatives would not be as effective, would cause harm, or why the drug you need should be covered. Use the exception form or contact method in your Evidence of Coverage or Annual Notice of Change.

How quickly must a Part D plan respond to an exception request?

After the plan receives your prescriber's supporting statement, it must decide within 72 hours under standard review or within 24 hours under expedited review when waiting for a decision would seriously harm your health. Ask the plan if you qualify for expedited review. If you already paid at the pharmacy and the exception is approved, the plan must decide your reimbursement request within 14 calendar days.

Should I request an exception or switch Part D plans during AEP?

Both paths can make sense. An exception may help if you want to stay on the same plan and your doctor supports medical need. During Annual Enrollment, October 15 through December 7, 2026, you can also compare 2027 stand-alone Part D and Medicare Advantage plans on Medicare Plan Finder to see whether another plan lists your drugs more favorably. If the plan denies an exception, the denial notice explains appeal steps and any deadline on that notice.
