When short-term medical may help—and when another option may be better.

Short-Term Medical and TriTerm Medical are designed for temporary coverage needs. They may work for someone who can pass underwriting and needs a non-ACA option. Comprehensive ACA coverage may be more appropriate when you need guaranteed-issue coverage for ongoing medical needs.

Short-term coverage may work for you if...

  • You are between employer plans, waiting for benefits to start, or outside ACA Open Enrollment without a Special Enrollment Period.
  • You have compared ACA Marketplace coverage, including any financial help available through your application, and still want to consider a medically underwritten alternative.
  • You want coverage that can continue beyond one term and understand how TriTerm's three separate terms work.
  • You understand that approval depends on health questions and that exclusions, benefit limits, and network rules apply.

Another type of coverage may be better if...

  • You need ACA-style coverage for pre-existing conditions, maternity, mental health, substance use treatment, or unlimited major medical claims.
  • You have recent diagnoses, treatments, tests, prescriptions, or follow-up visits that could affect approval or claims.
  • You use specialists, scheduled procedures, expensive prescriptions, or non-network providers.
  • You need to compare more than the monthly premium, including exclusions and out-of-pocket costs.

The plan is not ACA

Frequently Asked Questions
Short-Term Medical: straight answers

What to know before applying.

Is TriTerm Medical ACA-compliant major medical insurance?

No. It is short-term medical insurance. It is not Minimum Essential Coverage, is medically underwritten, and does not have to cover everything ACA Marketplace plans must cover.

How long can TriTerm Medical last?

The Florida TriTerm Medical brochure describes three terms: 364 days, then 365 days, then 365 days. That can provide one day less than three years of coverage when the plan is issued and kept in force through all three terms.

Does TriTerm Medical cover pre-existing conditions?

The brochure states that no benefits are paid during the first 12 months for a health condition that existed before the coverage effective date. After 12 months on the plan, eligible expenses related to pre-existing conditions may be covered, subject to the policy.

Which plan options should I compare?

David can help you compare Value, Copay Select Max, Plan 80 Max, and Plan 100 Max where available. The right comparison depends on doctors, prescriptions, deductible comfort, office visit needs, and whether you want a lower premium or stronger cost control after the deductible.

How is Health ProtectorGuard different from short-term medical?

Health ProtectorGuard is fixed-indemnity coverage, not major medical. It pays scheduled benefits for certain covered services and is separate from short-term medical. It does not replace comprehensive coverage or remove a short-term plan's exclusions and limits.
