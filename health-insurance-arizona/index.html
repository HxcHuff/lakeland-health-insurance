Questions Arizona shoppers ask

Where do Arizona residents sign up for Marketplace coverage in 2027?

Arizona uses HealthCare.gov, the federal Marketplace. You can apply on HealthCare.gov, with help from a licensed agent registered for the plan year, or through an authorized enrollment site. Premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions apply only to plans bought through the Marketplace.

What are the 2027 open enrollment dates in Arizona?

HealthCare.gov Open Enrollment for 2027 runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. December 15, 2026 is the deadline for coverage starting January 1, 2027; selections from December 16 through January 15 generally begin February 1.

Can an agent in Lakeland, Florida help me with an Arizona plan?

Yes, if the agent holds an active Arizona insurance license and current HealthCare.gov registration. I hold an Arizona nonresident license #18213932. Reviews happen by phone and screen share from Lakeland, Florida. You keep your HealthCare.gov login and authorize every change.

What does a plan review cost?

There is no separate agent fee for the plan-review assistance described on this site. If you enroll in a plan, the insurer may pay me a commission. I compare plans from the insurers I'm appointed with in Arizona, which may not include every insurer on HealthCare.gov.

How do AHCCCS Medicaid expansion and HealthCare.gov fit together in Arizona?

Arizona expanded Medicaid through the Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System (AHCCCS), covering eligible low-income adults ages 19 to 64 up to 133% FPL (effectively 138% FPL with the standard 5% MAGI disregard). You can apply year-round at azahcccs.gov, and your state Medicaid agency makes the final eligibility decision. Above that cutoff up to 400% FPL, HealthCare.gov determines Marketplace tax credit eligibility.

Why verify Banner Health, HonorHealth, and Tucson networks on your 2027 plan ID?

Arizona ACA Marketplace plans vary widely between Phoenix/Scottsdale/Mesa and Tucson county networks. Auto-renewal does not guarantee a hospital or specialist stays in-network for 2027, and excess advance premium tax credits no longer have a federal repayment cap after 2025.
