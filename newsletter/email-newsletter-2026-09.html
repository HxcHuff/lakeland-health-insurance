Needs Review
Start with who would experience the financial impact
A responsible review begins with the people and organizations affected by the insured’s death. It then compares continuing responsibilities with the resources already available.
 People and services: Consider anyone relying on earnings, childcare, household work, caregiving, or business responsibilities.
 Continuing responsibilities: Review income needs, housing obligations, debts, childcare, education, final expenses, ongoing care, and business commitments.
 Existing resources: Account for savings, survivor income, individual policies, workplace coverage, and relevant business arrangements.
 A rule-of-thumb multiplier cannot determine an individual need. Coverage type, duration, and amount should follow a review of the specific responsibilities and resources involved.
Current Coverage Inventory
Workplace coverage is one part of the review
Employer-provided life insurance can be a meaningful benefit. Its role should be evaluated against the responsibilities identified above and the possibility that employment or eligibility may change.
Coverage: employer-paid and employee-paid amounts, effective dates, and current eligibility
Records: primary and contingent beneficiaries recorded by the plan
Employment changes: when coverage ends after leave, retirement, termination, or another eligibility change
Continuation paths: portability or conversion provisions, application deadlines, available policy forms, and cost
 Do not assume workplace coverage follows you. Federal COBRA continuation coverage applies to group health plans; it does not cover plans that provide only life insurance or disability benefits. Separate portability or conversion options may exist, but eligibility, policy forms, cost, and deadlines depend on the plan and applicable law. Review the certificate and contact the plan administrator or insurer before employment or eligibility changes.
Policy Structure
Term and permanent insurance address different timelines
The relevant comparison is not which category is universally better. It is how long the need may last, whether premiums are sustainable, what the policy guarantees, and which contract provisions apply.
Term life
Coverage for a stated period
Generally does not build cash value
Renewal and conversion provisions vary
Termination rules and future premiums vary by policy
  
Permanent life
Designed for longer-duration coverage
May include cash value
Premiums, charges, and funding requirements vary
Guaranteed and nonguaranteed elements must be reviewed separately
The issued policy controls. Product design, availability, underwriting, exclusions, limitations, and guarantees vary by insurer and state.
September Review List
Events that should trigger another look
Review coverage and insurer records at least annually and whenever a material family, employment, financial, business, or policy change occurs.
Family: marriage, divorce, remarriage, birth, adoption, or a change in dependents
Housing and debt: purchase, refinance, payoff, or another material obligation change
Work and income: job change, retirement, benefit change, or meaningful income change
Care and education: new childcare, education, caregiving, or continuing-support responsibility
Business: starting, selling, funding, or restructuring a business or partnership
Policy: approaching term expiration, conversion deadline, premium change, loan, withdrawal, or lapse concern
Policy Records
Confirm the insurer records—not just your intentions
A review should distinguish the policy owner, the insured, and each beneficiary. Requested changes should be completed through the insurer or employer plan and retained with the policy records.
Confirm primary and contingent beneficiaries
Confirm allocation percentages, legal names, and current contact information
Verify ownership and the person authorized to make policy changes
Keep the insurer name and policy location accessible to the appropriate people
Retain confirmation that requested beneficiary or ownership changes were accepted
 Minors, trusts, estates, divorce orders, business entities, and irrevocable beneficiary arrangements can create legal and tax consequences. Coordinate those decisions with qualified legal and tax professionals.
A Note from David
A responsible review does not start with a product or preset amount.
I start with the people and obligations that would be affected. Then I review current individual and workplace coverage, how long each need may last, whether premiums are sustainable, and whether beneficiary and ownership records match your intent. If new coverage is considered, the review should also address underwriting, policy definitions, exclusions, renewability or conversion provisions, and what is guaranteed versus not guaranteed.
Existing coverage should not be canceled or replaced until any new policy has been issued, accepted, reviewed, and placed in force.
