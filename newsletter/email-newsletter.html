Tax Document
Review your 1095-A before filing
If anyone in your tax household had Marketplace coverage, Form 1095-A supplies information used to reconcile advance premium tax credits on Form 8962.
 1095-A: Marketplace coverage. Review the household members, coverage months, premiums, and advance credit amounts before filing.
 1095-B and 1095-C: Coverage statements from insurers, government programs, or employers. Keep them with your tax records and ask your tax professional how they apply.
Before you file:
1. Download every 1095-A associated with your Marketplace account.
2. Compare the form with the people and coverage months on your application.
3. Contact the Marketplace for a corrected form if the information is inaccurate.
 Important: Premium tax-credit reconciliation is based on your final tax information. Consult a qualified tax professional for tax advice.
Marketplace Account
Keep household information current
Advance premium tax credits are calculated from estimated household information. Income, household, address, or access-to-coverage changes can affect eligibility and should be reported promptly.
Income changed: Update the Marketplace estimate using the household income information requested in the application.
Household changed: Report marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, or other household changes as applicable.
Other coverage became available: Employer coverage, Medicare, Medicaid, or other eligibility changes may affect Marketplace assistance.
 Eligibility and advance premium tax-credit amounts depend on the Marketplace application and final federal tax filing. No specific premium or tax outcome is guaranteed.

Medicare Briefing
Turning 65 or reviewing Medicare coverage?
Start with timing, eligibility, current providers, prescriptions, and the differences between coverage paths. Plan availability, benefits, costs, networks, and rules vary.
Initial Enrollment Period
For most people, the Medicare Initial Enrollment Period is a seven-month window: three months before the month you turn 65, your birthday month, and three months after. Other coverage can affect when you should enroll.
 Still working at 65? Employer size, active employment, coverage type, and account-based benefits can affect enrollment timing. Verify the rules before delaying Part A or Part B.

Annual review windows
