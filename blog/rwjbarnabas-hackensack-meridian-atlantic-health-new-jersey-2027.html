Navigating North, Central & South Jersey Hospital Tiers in 2027

Across New Jersey's 21 counties, hospital and specialty care centers on five flagship systems: RWJBarnabas Health (operating Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital in New Brunswick and Somerset, Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center in Livingston, Newark Beth Israel, Jersey City Medical Center, Monmouth Medical Center, Community Medical Center in Toms River, and Rutgers Cancer Institute / Jack & Sheryl Morris Cancer Center), Hackensack Meridian Health (operating Hackensack University Medical Center, John Theurer Cancer Center, JFK University Medical Center in Edison, Jersey Shore University Medical Center in Neptune, Ocean University Medical Center in Brick, and Riverview in Red Bank), Atlantic Health System (Morristown Medical Center, Overlook Medical Center in Summit, Chilton, Newton, Hackettstown, and CentraState in Freehold), and South Jersey's two anchors: Cooper University Health Care (Camden and MD Anderson Cancer Center at Cooper) and Virtua Health (Virtua Voorhees, Marlton, Mount Holly, and Our Lady of Lourdes).

On Get Covered New Jersey (nj.gov/getcoverednj, where Open Enrollment runs November 1 through January 31), individual ACA plans are state-regulated EPOs (Exclusive Provider Organizations) that rely heavily on Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 hospital cost-sharing and exclude non-emergency out-of-state care across the Hudson or Delaware rivers.

3 Steps for New Jersey Residents Before Open Enrollment Ends

  1. Check Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 Hospital Placement on Horizon OMNIA & AmeriHealth: Even when two New Jersey hospitals are both in-network, an inpatient admission or outpatient surgery at a Tier 1 hospital carries significantly lower deductibles and co-insurance than a Tier 2 facility.
  2. Verify NYC & Philadelphia Satellite Clinics by NJ Street Address: Because Get Covered NJ individual EPOs do not cover routine care in Manhattan or Philadelphia, verify whether in-state satellites — such as Memorial Sloan Kettering (MSK) in Basking Ridge, Monmouth, or Bergen, or Penn Medicine in Plainsboro, Cherry Hill, and Voorhees — participate in your specific NJ EPO network.
  3. Confirm Large Multi-Specialty Groups (Summit Health & Rutgers Health): Always verify the 10-digit NPI for Summit Health, RWJBarnabas Health Medical Group, Hackensack Meridian Medical Group, or Atlantic Medical Group inside your 2027 plan directory.

Emergency Room Protection (No Surprises Act): Under the federal No Surprises Act and Medicare emergency rules, emergency department evaluation and stabilization at RWJBarnabas, Hackensack Meridian, Atlantic Health (Morristown/Overlook), Cooper, Virtua, and Valley Hospital ERs are covered at in-network cost-sharing regardless of network tier; network restrictions apply to non-emergency surgery, scheduled admissions, and clinic visits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 hospital networks work on 2027 Get Covered New Jersey ACA plans?

On Get Covered New Jersey (nj.gov/getcoverednj), carriers including Horizon Blue Cross Blue Shield of New Jersey (OMNIA Health Plans), AmeriHealth New Jersey, Oscar Health, Ambetter from WellCare of New Jersey, and UnitedHealthcare use tiered EPO designs where Tier 1 partner hospitals carry substantially lower deductibles and co-pays than Tier 2 facilities.

Do 2027 Get Covered New Jersey individual plans cover New York City or Philadelphia hospitals?

Individual ACA plans sold on Get Covered New Jersey are state-restricted EPOs (Exclusive Provider Organizations) that limit non-emergency care to participating New Jersey providers and contracted NJ satellite clinics (such as MSK Basking Ridge/Monmouth/Bergen or Penn Medicine Princeton/Cherry Hill) unless an out-of-state exception is approved in advance.

How do Cooper University Health Care, Virtua Health, and Inspira compare in South Jersey for 2027?

In Camden, Burlington, Gloucester, and Atlantic counties, Cooper University Health Care (including MD Anderson Cancer Center at Cooper), Virtua Health, and Inspira Health anchor regional care, with each carrier assigning specific Tier 1 or Tier 2 status by hospital campus.
