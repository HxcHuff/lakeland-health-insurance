What Is Form 1095-A and Why Do I Need Form 8962?

Every tax season, thousands of Marketplace enrollees have their refunds delayed because of a missing Form 1095-A or an unexpected Form 8962 repayment. Starting with 2026 tax returns filed in early 2027, federal repayment caps are gone, making accurate reconciliation more important than ever.

Direct answer: Form 1095-A is your official HealthCare.gov tax statement showing each month's plan premium (Column A), benchmark SLCSP rate (Column B), and advance subsidy paid to your insurer (Column C). You must enter those numbers onto IRS Form 8962 with your tax return; skipping Form 8962 causes automatic IRS e-file rejection (Code F8962-070) and can trigger a Failure to File and Reconcile (FTR) shutoff of future ACA subsidies.

Form 1095-A Columns Explained (And Where They Go on Form 8962)

Form 1095-A ColumnWhat It RepresentsCommon Error to Check Before Filing
Column A: Monthly Enrollment PremiumsFull monthly premium of your Marketplace plan (not your out-of-pocket net bill)Check that terminated months show $0; request a corrected 1095-A if cancelled months still appear.
Column B: Monthly SLCSP PremiumSecond-Lowest-Cost Silver Plan benchmark for your county &amp; householdSometimes prints $0 if you took $0 APTC up front; look up the exact SLCSP on HealthCare.gov's tax tool.
Column C: Monthly APTC PaidAdvance Premium Tax Credit sent directly to your insurance carrier each monthEven 1 month of $1+ APTC requires filing Form 8962; omitting it triggers IRS Reject Code F8962-070.

2026 & 2027 Rule Alert: Uncapped Repayment & FTR Subsidy Shutoff

  • No Repayment Caps Starting Tax Year 2026 (Filed in 2027): Federal legislation eliminated the historical statutory repayment caps for tax years beginning January 1, 2026 and later. If your final MAGI exceeds your projection, you must repay 100% of excess APTC on Form 8962 - and crossing the restored 400% FPL cliff triggers repayment of the entire year's advance credit.
  • Failure to File and Reconcile (FTR): CMS and the IRS cross-check prior-year returns every year. Missing one year generates a 1-year FTR warning notice; failing to file and attach Form 8962 for two consecutive tax years causes HealthCare.gov to shut off your monthly financial help entirely until you file and attest.

How to Fix IRS Reject Code F8962-070 or a Missing 1095-A

  1. Download Your PDF Directly From HealthCare.gov: Forms are posted by January 31 (and mailed by mid-February) under "Tax Forms" inside your HealthCare.gov application.
  2. Check for Multiple or Voided Forms: If you switched plans mid-year, got married/divorced, or had a carrier correction, you may have two 1095-A forms or a "CORRECTED" version - always use the latest corrected form.
  3. Never Zero Out Column B If Claiming PTC: If Column B is blank or $0, use HealthCare.gov's official SLCSP lookup tool before completing Form 8962 with your tax software or CPA.
  4. Update Your 2027 MAGI Now to Protect Next Year's Return: If your actual income ended up higher than expected, update your 2027 Marketplace application immediately so you do not build up an uncapped repayment balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why was my tax return rejected with IRS code F8962-070?

IRS Reject Code F8962-070 means IRS records show someone on your tax return had HealthCare.gov Marketplace coverage with advance subsidies (Form 1095-A), but your return did not include Form 8962. Add your 1095-A data to Form 8962 and resubmit.

What if I had Marketplace coverage for only one month?

You still receive a Form 1095-A and must attach Form 8962 to your federal tax return if any Advance Premium Tax Credit was paid for that month.

Can I lose my 2027 Marketplace subsidy if I forgot to file Form 8962?

Yes. Under CMS Failure to File and Reconcile (FTR) rules, failing to file your federal tax return with Form 8962 triggers a 1-year warning and stops future APTC after two consecutive unreconciled years.

Are ACA subsidy repayment caps still available when filing 2026 or 2027 taxes?

No. Starting with tax year 2026 returns filed in 2027, federal law removed the sliding-scale repayment caps, meaning taxpayers must repay 100% of any excess advance premium tax credits.
