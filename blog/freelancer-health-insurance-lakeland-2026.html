Freelancer Coverage Paths for 2026

ACA Marketplace Plans

HealthCare.gov says self-employed applicants estimate net self-employment income for the year they want coverage. That estimate affects Marketplace savings, so it should be based on realistic revenue, expenses, and household income rather than last year's return alone.

  • Best starting point for most independent contractors.
  • Potential subsidies based on projected household income.
  • Comprehensive coverage and pre-existing condition protections.

Short-Term or Alternative Paths

  • Can be considered for temporary gaps, not as long-term replacement coverage.
  • Review exclusions and benefit limitations carefully.

Income and Tax Records Matter

For Marketplace applications, HealthCare.gov uses modified adjusted gross income, and self-employment income is generally reported after business expenses. If your business income changes during the year, update the Marketplace application instead of waiting until tax filing.

For taxes, the IRS uses Form 7206 to determine any self-employed health insurance deduction you may be able to claim. A broker can help compare health plan options, but deduction eligibility should be confirmed with your tax professional.

HSA-Compatible Plans in 2026

HealthCare.gov states that all 2026 Bronze and Catastrophic Marketplace plans are eligible for Health Savings Accounts. HSA-compatible plans can make sense for some freelancers, but they usually trade a lower premium for a higher deductible. Compare the premium, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, expected care, and whether you can fund the HSA.

Checklist Before You Enroll

  1. Project realistic annual net income for subsidy planning.
  2. Confirm your doctors and hospitals in-network.
  3. Verify prescription coverage tier and pharmacy costs.
  4. Compare annual risk: premium + deductible + out-of-pocket maximum.
  5. Keep income and expense records available in case the Marketplace asks for documentation.
