What Happens to My Health Insurance If I Get Divorced or Separated?

Divorce and separation change your health insurance, tax household, and subsidy eligibility all at once. Knowing the exact timing rules between a temporary separation, a final divorce decree, COBRA, ACA subsidies, and Medicare IRMAA prevents expensive coverage gaps.

Direct answer: When a final divorce decree ends your coverage on an ex-spouse's employer plan, you trigger a 60-day ACA Marketplace Special Enrollment Period and up to 36 months of COBRA (double the usual 18 months). Watch out for one major tax trap if you are still legally married on December 31: filing Married Filing Separately (MFS) generally disqualifies you from ACA subsidies unless you qualify for the domestic abuse or spousal abandonment exception.

Why Married Filing Separately Matters So Much During a Pending Divorce

If your divorce is not final by December 31, the IRS still treats you as married for that tax year. Under IRC Section 36B, spouses who file Married Filing Separately are generally barred from receiving ACA Premium Tax Credits - meaning any advance subsidies received during the year would have to be repaid in full (uncapped for 2026 and 2027) on Form 8962.

There are two important legal exceptions: (1) taxpayers who live apart from their spouse for the last 6 months of the year, pay over half the home's upkeep, and have a qualifying dependent child may qualify to file as Head of Household (eligible for APTC); and (2) HealthCare.gov provides a confidential domestic abuse or spousal abandonment exception allowing MFS filers to keep subsidies. Confirm your filing status with your CPA or divorce attorney before accepting APTC.

4 Action Steps Before Your Divorce Decree Is Signed

  1. Check Your Exact Coverage Termination Date: Most employer plans drop an ex-spouse on the day the divorce is finalized or at the end of that month (spouses usually cannot legally stay on a job plan post-divorce).
  2. Compare Marketplace Subsidies vs. 36-Month COBRA Side by Side: Because COBRA charges 102% of the full unsubsidized employer rate, an ACA plan on your single post-divorce income is often $300 to $700/month less - unless you already met a huge deductible this year.
  3. Coordinate Who Claims the Kids on Taxes: Align health insurance enrollment with the tax-dependent clause in your parenting plan so Form 1095-A and Form 8962 match cleanly.
  4. If on Medicare, Submit Form SSA-44 Right Away: Attach your certified divorce decree to lower 2027 Part B/Part D IRMAA surcharges based on your new single income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does COBRA last after a divorce?

Divorce or legal separation is a 36-month COBRA qualifying event for the former spouse and dependent children, provided the employer's plan administrator is notified within 60 days of the divorce decree.

Does getting divorced qualify me for a Special Enrollment Period on HealthCare.gov?

Losing health coverage because of a divorce triggers a 60-day ACA Special Enrollment Period. Getting divorced without losing coverage does not by itself trigger an SEP.

Can I get ACA subsidies if I am separated and filing Married Filing Separately?

Usually no, because Married Filing Separately generally disqualifies taxpayers from Premium Tax Credits - unless you meet the IRS Head of Household criteria or the HealthCare.gov domestic abuse or spousal abandonment exception.

Can divorce lower my Medicare IRMAA surcharge?

Yes. Divorce or annulment is one of the 8 official life-changing events on Social Security Form SSA-44, allowing SSA to recalculate your 2027 Part B and Part D IRMAA using your lower post-divorce income.
