Three 2026 ACA Changes for Florida Consumers

Published May 2, 2026 | Updated August 12, 2026 | By David Huff, Licensed FL Agent #W371813
Home > Blog > Three 2026 ACA Changes for Florida Consumers

Three federal rules materially affect 2026 Marketplace comparisons: the return of the pre-2021 premium-tax-credit income boundary, expanded HSA compatibility, and event-specific Special Enrollment rules.

This update separates those verified rules from household-specific pricing and plan availability. The Marketplace application, exact plan documents, and current provider and drug tools control an individual decision.

1. Premium Tax Credit Rules Changed for 2026

The temporary enhanced premium tax credit rules ended after 2025. IRS guidance states that, for 2026, premium tax credit eligibility is generally limited to households with income from 100 through 400 percent of the federal poverty line, subject to the other tax, Marketplace enrollment, and minimum-essential-coverage requirements.

The IRS also published a 2026 applicable-percentage table used in the premium tax credit calculation. That table is not a household quote. The result depends on the tax household, projected annual income, benchmark plan, residence, eligibility for other coverage, and the plan selected.

What to verify

Update the Marketplace application when income or household information changes. Compare the resulting premium together with the deductible, other cost sharing, provider network, prescriptions, and annual out-of-pocket limit. Do not project a household result from a statewide average.

2. Bronze and Catastrophic Plans Have Expanded HSA Compatibility

IRS Notice 2026-05 states that Bronze and Catastrophic plans are treated as HSA-compatible beginning January 1, 2026. The rule expands the plan types that can support HSA eligibility, but enrollment in one of those plans does not by itself establish that a person may contribute.

For calendar year 2026, the HSA annual contribution limit is $4,400 for an eligible individual with self-only coverage and $8,750 for an eligible individual with family coverage. The permitted contribution can depend on coverage type, age, other coverage, and the months during which the person is HSA-eligible.

What to verify

Confirm HSA eligibility under the federal tax rules before contributing. Then compare the plan's premium, deductible, prescriptions, provider network, cost sharing, and maximum out-of-pocket exposure. HSA compatibility is a tax feature, not a finding that a Bronze or Catastrophic plan is the best fit.

3. Special Enrollment Timing Depends on the Event

Outside Open Enrollment, a person may be able to enroll or change plans through a Special Enrollment Period. HealthCare.gov lists loss of qualifying coverage, specified household changes, and qualifying residence changes among the events that may create an enrollment opportunity.

Timing, prior-coverage conditions, available plan categories, effective dates, and documentation vary by event. The Marketplace application determines eligibility. Apply promptly after a life change and follow the eligibility notice rather than assuming every event uses the same deadline.

What to verify

Keep the document showing the event and effective date, complete the Marketplace application accurately, review the eligibility notice, select an available plan within the stated window, and pay the first premium to the insurer by its deadline.

The 2026 Review List

  1. Confirm projected annual household income and update the Marketplace application when it changes.
  2. Use the exact plan documents to compare premium, cost sharing, providers, prescriptions, and service area.
  3. Verify HSA eligibility under the federal rules before making a contribution.
  4. Keep life-event documentation and follow the timing shown in the Marketplace eligibility notice.
  5. Recheck each benefit year. Plan availability, networks, formularies, and costs can change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changed for ACA premium tax credits in 2026?

The temporary enhanced premium tax credit rules ended after 2025. For 2026, eligibility is generally limited to households from 100 through 400 percent of the federal poverty line, subject to the other tax and coverage requirements. Household results must be determined from the Marketplace application.

Are Bronze and Catastrophic plans HSA-compatible in 2026?

Yes. IRS Notice 2026-05 states that Bronze and Catastrophic plans are treated as HSA-compatible beginning January 1, 2026. A person must still satisfy the other federal HSA eligibility rules before contributing.

What are the 2026 HSA contribution limits?

For calendar year 2026, the HSA contribution limit is $4,400 for an eligible individual with self-only coverage and $8,750 for an eligible individual with family coverage. The permitted amount can depend on eligibility, coverage type, age, and months of eligibility.

Can I change my health plan outside Open Enrollment?

Possibly. HealthCare.gov lists loss of qualifying coverage, specified household changes, and qualifying residence changes among events that may create a Special Enrollment Period. Timing, prior-coverage conditions, plan choices, and documentation vary by event, and the Marketplace application determines eligibility.
