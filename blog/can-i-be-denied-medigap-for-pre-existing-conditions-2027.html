Medigap Underwriting & GI Rights

Can You Be Denied a Medigap Policy for Pre-Existing Conditions?

Unlike ACA Marketplace plans and Medicare Advantage plans, Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policies do not have a federal annual open enrollment period without health questions. Knowing exactly when you have guaranteed issue rights protects your ability to get Plan G or Plan N.

Direct answer: Inside your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period (starting the month you are 65+ and enrolled in Part B) or during a federal 63-day Guaranteed Issue event, insurers cannot deny you or charge more for health conditions. Outside those protected windows, in Florida and most states, Medigap carriers can require full medical underwriting and deny applications for serious health conditions.

What About the 6-Month Pre-Existing Condition Waiting Period?

Even during your initial 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, an insurer is allowed to delay covering a pre-existing condition for up to 6 months only if you lacked 6 months of prior continuous creditable coverage (with no break longer than 63 days). If you came directly from employer insurance, ACA Marketplace coverage, or VA coverage without a 63-day gap, the insurer must shorten or completely waive that waiting period - and Original Medicare still pays its normal 80% share during any waiting period.

State Birthday & Anniversary Rules: While Florida does not have a Medigap birthday rule, several licensed states (including Maryland, Oklahoma, Louisiana, Virginia, Missouri, Washington, and Indiana) allow existing Medigap policyholders to switch to equal-or-lesser Medigap plans during annual windows without underwriting. 4 Smart Steps Before Applying for Medigap in 2027

  1. Check for a 2027 Non-Renewal Letter First: If your 2026 Medicare Advantage plan is exiting your county Dec 31, 2026, do not enroll in another Advantage plan first if you want Medigap - enrolling in another MA plan forfeits that specific 63-day GI right.
  2. Never Cancel Your Existing Plan Before Written Approval: If you must go through medical underwriting, keep your current coverage active until the Medigap carrier issues an official policy approval.
  3. Verify Medication & Look-Back Lists Across Multiple Carriers: Underwriting knockouts vary widely between insurers (e.g., 2-year vs. 5-year look-backs for stents, cancer remission, or insulin dosages). One decline does not mean every carrier declines.
  4. Pair Medigap With a Standalone 2027 Part D Plan: Remember Medigap covers Parts A and B only; add a 2027 Part D prescription plan ($2,400 out-of-pocket cap) during your enrollment window to avoid Part D penalties.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap anytime without health questions?

In Florida and most states, no. Switching voluntarily from Medicare Advantage to Medigap after your first 12-month Trial Right requires medical underwriting unless you have a federal Guaranteed Issue event (such as your MA plan exiting your county or moving out of its service area).

What health conditions commonly cause a Medigap underwriting denial?

Outside guaranteed issue windows, carriers frequently decline applicants with active cancer treatment, oxygen use, COPD on specific inhalers, insulin-dependent diabetes with complications, dementia, dialysis, or pending surgeries, though rules vary by carrier.

Does Medicare Advantage deny people for pre-existing conditions?

No. Medicare Advantage plans cannot deny enrollment or charge higher premiums for pre-existing health conditions (including End-Stage Renal Disease), as long as you have Parts A and B and live in the plan's service area.

Can I get Medigap without health questions if my 2026 Advantage plan is ending?

Yes. Federal Guaranteed Issue rules give displaced members a window from 60 days before through 63 days after plan termination to buy Medigap Plans A, B, D, G, K, or L (plus C or F if eligible before 2020) with no medical underwriting.
