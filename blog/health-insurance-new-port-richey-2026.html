What Changed for New Port Richey Residents in 2026

For Marketplace enrollees, the enhanced premium tax credits expired December 31, 2025. These credits had been keeping monthly premiums affordable for the majority of ACA enrollees. Florida regulators approved a 34.1% enrollment-weighted average 2026 increase in individual-market rates before subsidies (Florida OIR, Oct. 16, 2025); your own change may be higher or lower. Many New Port Richey residents are seeing monthly costs they hadn't budgeted for.

Pasco County's cost of living has been rising but still sits below Hillsborough and Pinellas counties, meaning more residents may fall in income ranges where losing Medicaid or smaller subsidies can make after-subsidy premiums harder to absorb without a plan review.

The elimination of tax credit repayment caps under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act adds risk for residents with variable income. Seasonal workers, part-time employees, and anyone whose earnings fluctuate through the year should pay close attention to income estimates on their Marketplace application.

If You Lose Medicaid or CHIP in Pasco County

The 2025 federal law adds six-month renewals and work requirements for Medicaid expansion adults starting in 2027. Florida did not expand Medicaid, so those rules generally do not apply to Florida Medicaid. If you do lose Medicaid or CHIP, that loss may open a 60-day Marketplace Special Enrollment Period, subject to Marketplace rules and verification.

If you received a Medicaid termination letter, act within the 60-day window. Subsidy eligibility on the Marketplace depends on your current projected income, household and plan; confirm at HealthCare.gov.

Special Enrollment Periods: Who May Qualify

Events that may open a Special Enrollment Period, subject to Marketplace rules, deadlines, and verification, include:

  • Lost job-based coverage (including through reduced hours)
  • Got married
  • Divorce or legal separation that ends your coverage
  • Had or adopted a child
  • Certain permanent moves that change your plan options (prior coverage usually required)
  • Lost Medicaid or CHIP
  • Aged off a parent's plan at 26
  • COBRA coverage ran out (dropping COBRA or not paying does not qualify)

You generally have 60 days. For most new SEP enrollments, HealthCare.gov verifies eligibility before coverage starts.

Bronze + HSA: A Tax-Advantaged Option for 2026

Every Bronze and Catastrophic Marketplace plan is HSA-compatible for 2026. For New Port Richey residents who are relatively healthy and don't qualify for Silver-tier cost-sharing reductions, a Bronze plan plus an HSA is one option to compare — not a promise of a lower bill. Savings and subsidy eligibility depend on income, household and plan; confirm at HealthCare.gov.

HSA contributions are pre-tax, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. The account rolls over year to year and belongs to you regardless of plan changes.
