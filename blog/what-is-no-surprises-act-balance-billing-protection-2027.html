What Is the No Surprises Act and When Are You Protected From Balance Billing?

One of the biggest fears patients have when a hospital contract changes or an emergency strikes is getting hit with a $15,000 out-of-network surprise bill. Here is exactly what the federal No Surprises Act covers, what it does NOT cover (ground ambulances!), and how to fight an illegal bill.

Direct answer: The federal No Surprises Act bans out-of-network "balance bills" for (1) emergency room care at any hospital or freestanding ER (even if the hospital is out of network), (2) out-of-network doctors (like anesthesiologists, radiologists, or assistant surgeons) working inside an in-network hospital, and (3) out-of-network air ambulances. You owe only your in-network copay, deductible, or coinsurance.

Watch Out for Consent-to-Waive Forms (And Ground Ambulances)

For non-emergency scheduled care (such as choosing an out-of-network surgeon at an in-network hospital), a provider can ask you to sign a written Notice and Consent waiver at least 72 hours beforehand giving up your balance-billing protections. Never sign a balance-billing waiver blindly - and know that emergency doctors, anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, neonatologists, and assistant surgeons are legally prohibited from asking you to waive your rights.

Medicare protection: Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage already prohibit participating providers from balance billing (non-participating Medicare providers are capped at the 15% limiting charge on Original Medicare, which Medigap Plan G covers 100% of).

4 Steps If You Receive an Illegal Out-of-Network Surprise Bill

  1. Do Not Pay Immediately - Wait for Your Explanation of Benefits (EOB): Compare the provider's bill against your insurer's EOB to confirm whether the claim was processed under No Surprises Act in-network rules.
  2. Call Your Insurer & Provider Billing Office Together: Tell the billing department the service is protected under the federal No Surprises Act and ask them to reprocess directly with the insurer through Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR).
  3. File a Federal No Surprises Help Desk Complaint: If the provider refuses to pause collections or remove the balance bill, call the CMS No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059 or submit online at cms.gov/nosurprises.
  4. Verify Hospital & Specialist Networks Before Scheduled Care: Because non-emergency out-of-network visits are not automatically protected, always confirm both the facility NPI and surgeon NPI before booking elective procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the No Surprises Act cover emergency room visits at out-of-network hospitals?

Yes. Under federal law, emergency evaluation and stabilization at any hospital or freestanding emergency department must be billed at your in-network cost-sharing rate without prior authorization.

Are ground ambulances covered by the federal No Surprises Act?

No. Air ambulances are protected under the federal No Surprises Act, but ground ambulances were excluded from the federal statute (though several states have enacted separate ground-ambulance protections).

What if an out-of-network anesthesiologist bills me after surgery at an in-network hospital?

That bill is banned under the No Surprises Act. Anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, and assistant surgeons at in-network facilities cannot balance bill you or ask you to waive your protections.

What is a Good Faith Estimate if I am uninsured or paying cash?

Under the No Surprises Act, uninsured or self-pay patients have the right to a written Good Faith Estimate before scheduled care and can use the federal Patient-Provider Dispute Resolution process if the actual bill is $400 or more above the estimate.
