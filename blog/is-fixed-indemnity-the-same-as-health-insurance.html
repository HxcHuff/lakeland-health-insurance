Is a fixed indemnity plan the same as health insurance?

The word insurance on a fixed indemnity brochure does not make the contract major medical coverage. These policies pay a scheduled amount for listed events, such as a hospital stay or a doctor visit, regardless of the full bill. They are designed as supplements, not replacements, for comprehensive health coverage.

It is not ACA-compliant major medical coverage and does not replace a Marketplace, employer, or Medicare plan. Hospital bills, specialist care, and prescriptions can still be your responsibility beyond the scheduled amount.

What does the policy actually pay?

A fixed indemnity or hospital indemnity contract pays the amount listed for a covered event, such as a daily hospital benefit or a visit benefit. It is not coinsurance against the hospital invoice. If the bill is larger than the schedule, the difference remains. If the event is excluded, the benefit can be zero. See what supplemental coverage is and the existing product analysis at fixed indemnity insurance analysis.

Why is it not ACA coverage?

ACA individual major medical plans must cover essential health benefits, meet actuarial-value metal rules, and follow consumer protections such as covering pre-existing conditions. Fixed indemnity products are excepted benefits. They can use underwriting, waiting periods, and limited schedules. They generally do not satisfy minimum essential coverage. That is a different legal category, not a cheaper metal level.

When do people look at indemnity anyway?

Some households add a supplement on top of major medical to help with cash flow during a hospital stay. That only works if the major medical plan is already in place. Using indemnity alone during a coverage gap leaves the full bill exposed except for the schedule. Compare that risk with Marketplace or COBRA options. See high deductibles and hospital-bill cash flow.

How is this different from short-term medical?

Short-term medical is underwritten medical coverage with its own exclusions and duration limits. Indemnity is a scheduled cash benefit. Neither is a substitute for ACA-compliant major medical insurance. See what short-term insurance does not cover.

This is not comprehensive coverage

Fixed indemnity and similar limited-benefit products are supplemental coverage. They are not a substitute for comprehensive major medical insurance or ACA-compliant coverage. Benefits, exclusions, waiting periods, and eligibility are controlled by the policy.

How do I read a benefit schedule without confusing it for coinsurance?

Look for per-day, per-visit, and per-admission amounts, and for lifetime or annual maximums. Look for waiting periods after the effective date. Look for exclusions for pre-existing conditions. If the schedule does not mention surgery, do not assume surgery is paid.

Ask whether the product is an excepted benefit and whether it is ACA-compliant major medical coverage. The honest answer for fixed indemnity is that it is not comprehensive major medical insurance. Keep that sentence on the same page as any quote.

If you already have a Marketplace or employer plan, decide whether a supplement is worth the extra premium using the schedule, not using a hospital bill you hope it will erase.

Common questions

If the hospital bill is large, does indemnity pay the rest?

No. It pays the scheduled amount for covered events. Amounts above the schedule remain your responsibility unless another plan pays them.

Can I use fixed indemnity instead of a Marketplace plan?

It is not a substitute for ACA major medical coverage. Compare comprehensive coverage first whenever you have an enrollment path.
