How a carrier non-renewal notice creates a rare federal Guaranteed Issue window to return to Original Medicare and enroll in Medicare Supplement Plan G without answering medical questions.

By David Huff, licensed Florida health insurance agent #W371813 · Published October 21, 2026

Direct answer: When a Medicare Advantage carrier discontinues or non-renews your plan in Polk County for 2027, federal law (42 U.S.C. § 1395ss) grants you a Guaranteed Issue (GI) right to buy a Medicare Supplement policy—including Plan G with participating carriers in Florida—without answering health questions or undergoing medical underwriting. If you previously wanted Plan G but were locked out due to pre-existing conditions, your carrier's non-renewal notice is the document that unlocks this window.

Every year during Annual Enrollment (October 15–December 7), thousands of Florida seniors ask the same question: "Can I switch from my Medicare Advantage plan to a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Plan G?"

In most normal years, the answer is tough: unless you are in your initial 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment period when you first turned 65, switching from an Advantage plan to Plan G requires passing medical underwriting. If you have been treated for heart conditions, diabetes with insulin, cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, or scheduled joint replacements, insurance companies can legally deny your application or charge higher rates.

However, for 2027, hundreds of beneficiaries across Lakeland, Winter Haven, and Central Florida received an official letter stating that their Medicare Advantage plan contract is ending on December 31. If you hold that letter, the rules change completely.

How a Plan Non-Renewal Triggers Federal Guaranteed Issue Rights

Under federal Medicare law and Florida insurance guidelines, if your Medicare Advantage plan stops offering coverage in your county or service area, you qualify for a federal Medigap Guaranteed Issue right.

Under this federal protection, insurance companies selling Medigap policies to eligible beneficiaries:

  • Must offer qualifying Medigap policies (including Plan G for individuals first eligible for Medicare on or after January 1, 2020, or Plan A, B, C, F, K, or L depending on your Medicare eligibility date and carrier availability).
  • Cannot deny your application based on your medical history or pre-existing conditions.
  • Cannot place waiting periods on pre-existing health conditions.
  • Cannot charge you a higher rate because of past or present health problems.

This means a plan termination gives you a protected option to return to Original Medicare and apply for a Medicare Supplement policy without medical underwriting.

Why Beneficiaries Compare Plan G When an Advantage Plan Ends

When an Advantage plan non-renews, you can choose another Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare with a Medigap policy. Comparing Plan G alongside Original Medicare offers several structural differences:

  • Nationwide Medicare Provider Access: See any doctor, specialist, or hospital in the United States that accepts Original Medicare assignment (including participating physicians at Lakeland Regional Health, Watson Clinic, Orlando Health, Moffitt Cancer Center, and Mayo Clinic in Jacksonville) without HMO network referrals.
  • Predictable Covered Medical Costs: After you meet the annual Medicare Part B deductible, Plan G pays 100% of Medicare-approved Part A and Part B coinsurance for covered hospital and physician services. (Monthly premiums, separate Part D drug costs, and non-covered services still apply.)
  • Rare Prior Authorization Under Original Medicare: Unlike Medicare Advantage plans that apply plan-level prior authorization to many specialist procedures and imaging studies, Original Medicare requires prior authorization only for a limited set of specific services and equipment.

Key Enrollment Windows

Understand How Your Medigap GI Window and Part D SEP Work Together

1. Medigap Guaranteed Issue Window (60 days before to 63 days after coverage ends): Federal rules allow you to apply for your Guaranteed Issue Medigap policy as early as 60 calendar days before your Medicare Advantage plan ends (around November 2) and up to 63 calendar days after December 31.

2. Non-Renewal Special Enrollment Period (December 8 through the last day of February): Because your plan is non-renewing, Medicare grants a Special Enrollment Period from December 8 through the end of February to enroll in another Medicare Advantage plan or a standalone Part D drug plan. You can also make your selection during standard Annual Enrollment (October 15–December 7). Enrolling before December 31 ensures your new coverage takes effect on January 1, 2027 with no gap.

Important Requirement: Pair Medigap with a Standalone Part D Plan

There is one vital step to remember when moving from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare with a Medigap policy:

Plan G does not include retail prescription drug coverage.

Because Medicare Advantage plans typically bundle medical and Part D drug coverage into one card, returning to Original Medicare means you need to enroll in a standalone Medicare Part D Prescription Drug Plan (PDP). You can enroll in your standalone Part D plan during Annual Enrollment (October 15–December 7) or use your non-renewal Special Enrollment Period (December 8 through the last day of February)—applying before December 31 ensures your drug coverage starts January 1. Going more than 63 consecutive days without creditable drug coverage can trigger a Part D late-enrollment penalty.

4 Steps to Use Your Non-Renewal Guaranteed Issue Right

  1. Keep your official non-renewal letter: The Medigap insurance company requires a copy of the carrier's official termination or non-renewal notice as proof of your Guaranteed Issue eligibility.
  2. Compare Polk County Medigap quotes: Standardized Medigap plans with the same letter offer the same core Medicare-covered medical benefits, while monthly premiums and rating methods vary by insurance company, age, ZIP code, and tobacco status.
  3. Select a standalone Part D plan before your coverage ends: Match your prescription list and dosage strengths against 2027 Florida Part D formularies before December 31 for a January 1 start date.
  4. Submit your Medigap application with proof of non-renewal: Coordinate your Medigap and Part D effective dates for January 1, 2027 to avoid any gap in coverage.

Common questions

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Plan G without answering health questions?

Yes, but only if you have a qualifying federal Guaranteed Issue right. An involuntary non-renewal or termination letter from your Medicare Advantage plan proves that your plan is ending on December 31, granting you a 63-day window to purchase Medigap Plan G without medical underwriting.

What proof do I need to get Guaranteed Issue for Medigap Plan G?

You must provide a copy of your official carrier non-renewal or plan termination notice showing that your specific plan contract will not be offered in your service area next year.

Does Plan G include prescription drug coverage?

No. Medicare Supplement insurance (Medigap) works alongside Original Medicare (Parts A and B) to cover deductibles and coinsurance, but it does not cover retail prescription drugs. To have standalone Medicare Part D coverage active on January 1, you can enroll during Annual Enrollment (October 15–December 7) or use your federal non-renewal Special Enrollment Period (December 8 through the last day of February).

When does the Guaranteed Issue window open and close?

Federal rules permit you to apply for a Guaranteed Issue Medigap policy as early as 60 calendar days before your Medicare Advantage plan ends (around November 2) and up to 63 calendar days after coverage ends. Applying before December 31 helps ensure your new Medigap coverage starts January 1 with no coverage gap.
