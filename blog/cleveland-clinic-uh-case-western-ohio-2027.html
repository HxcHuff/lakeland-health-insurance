The Northeast Ohio Hospital Network Split: Cleveland Clinic vs. UH vs. MetroHealth

Northeast Ohio (Ohio Rating Areas 3 and 11, spanning Cuyahoga, Lake, Geauga, Lorain, Medina, Summit, Portage, and Stark counties) features one of the most concentrated academic medical corridors in North America:

  • Cleveland Clinic Health System: Cleveland Clinic Main Campus, Hillcrest Hospital, Fairview Hospital, Avon Hospital, Marymount, South Pointe, Akron General, and Mercy Hospital (Canton), including the Sydell and Arnold Miller Family Heart, Vascular & Thoracic Institute and Taussig Cancer Institute.
  • University Hospitals (UH) / Case Western Reserve University Affiliate: UH Cleveland Medical Center, UH Seidman Cancer Center, UH Rainbow Babies & Children's Hospital, UH Ahuja, UH St. John, UH Parma, UH Geauga, and UH Portage.
  • The MetroHealth System: MetroHealth Medical Center (Glick Center on West 25th Street, Level I Adult Trauma & Burn Center) and Cuyahoga County community health centers.

On the individual HealthCare.gov Marketplace in Northeast Ohio, carriers—including Medical Mutual of Ohio, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, Oscar Health, Ambetter (Buckeye Health Plan), CareSource, and Molina—rarely include all three systems in the same low-premium HMO tier. Instead, plans are frequently built around system-specific sub-networks (such as Cleveland Clinic-aligned HMO tiers vs. UH-aligned or MetroHealth-aligned networks).

Ohio Continuity of Care & Federal No Surprises Act Protections

  • Federal No Surprises Act Emergency Protection: Under the federal No Surprises Act, emergency medical evaluation and stabilization at MetroHealth Level I Trauma Center, UH Cleveland Medical Center ER, UH Rainbow Babies Pediatric ER, and Cleveland Clinic emergency departments are covered at in-network cost-sharing rates regardless of your plan's elective network tier.
  • Ohio Revised Code § 1751.13 & Federal Section 113 Continuity of Care: If you are receiving active treatment for a serious or complex condition—such as oncology care at Taussig Cancer Institute or UH Seidman Cancer Center, cardiac surgery follow-up, or pregnancy—when a provider contract terminates, Ohio law and federal continuity-of-care rules allow eligible enrollees to apply for up to 90 days of transitional care at in-network cost-sharing rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Northeast Ohio ACA Marketplace plans cover both Cleveland Clinic and University Hospitals in 2027?

Rarely on the same narrow HMO tier. In Cuyahoga, Lake, Lorain, Summit, and Stark counties, HealthCare.gov carriers frequently build separate sub-networks aligned with either Cleveland Clinic, University Hospitals (UH), or MetroHealth, making plan-specific directory verification essential.

Which 2027 Medicare option covers Cleveland Clinic Main Campus and UH Seidman Cancer Center without network splits?

Original Medicare (Parts A and B) paired with a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan is accepted at Cleveland Clinic Main Campus, University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center, and MetroHealth without Medicare Advantage HMO network restrictions.

What happens if an Ohio health plan changes network status with Cleveland Clinic or UH mid-year?

Under Ohio Revised Code Section 1751.13 and federal No Surprises Act Section 113 continuity-of-care rules, patients in active treatment for serious or complex conditions can apply for transitional in-network coverage, and emergency room stabilization is always covered at in-network rates.
