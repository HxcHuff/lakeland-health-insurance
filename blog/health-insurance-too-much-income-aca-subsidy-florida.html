What health insurance options exist if I make too much for ACA subsidies?

If your income is too high for a premium tax credit, you can still compare full-price Marketplace plans and other coverage paths. The right answer depends on doctors, prescriptions, tax household, and risk tolerance.

Updated July 28, 2026 | By David Huff, Licensed Florida Agent #W371813 | NPN 18213932

Direct answer: If you make too much for ACA subsidies, you can still buy a Marketplace plan without the premium tax credit. You can also compare off-Marketplace major medical plans, employer or spouse coverage, and limited alternatives such as short-term medical or supplemental coverage where appropriate.

Do not treat non-ACA options as automatic substitutes for ACA coverage. Many alternatives have underwriting, benefit limits, exclusions, or gaps that must be understood before enrolling.

Options to compare

  • Full-price Marketplace plans: ACA-compliant plans with essential health benefits and ACA consumer protections.
  • Off-Marketplace major medical: Similar carrier options may be available outside HealthCare.gov, but subsidies are only through the Marketplace.
  • Employer or spouse coverage: Sometimes the best value if the employer contribution is meaningful.
  • Short-term medical: Can fit certain temporary gaps but is medically underwritten and is not ACA major medical coverage.
  • Supplemental coverage: May help with defined cash-flow risks, but it does not replace comprehensive major medical coverage.

What to check before leaving ACA coverage

Review pre-existing condition rules, prescription coverage, doctor access, hospital networks, preventive care, maternity coverage if relevant, annual out-of-pocket exposure, and whether the plan is minimum essential coverage.

Higher income does not mean simple coverage

Higher-income households often need a more precise comparison because full-price ACA premiums can be high, but non-ACA options may shift risk back to the household.

FAQ

Can I use the Marketplace without a subsidy?

Yes. You can use HealthCare.gov to buy a Marketplace plan without a premium tax credit if you do not qualify for savings.

Are short-term plans the same as ACA plans?

No. Short-term medical is not ACA major medical coverage. It can have underwriting, exclusions, and benefit limits, so it should be compared carefully.
