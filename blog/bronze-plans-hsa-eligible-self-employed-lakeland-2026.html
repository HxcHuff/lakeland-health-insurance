Bronze Plans Now Work With HSAs — Why That Matters for Self-Employed Workers in Lakeland

By David | Licensed FL Agent #W371813 | May 11, 2026
Home > Blog > Bronze Plans Now Work With HSAs (May 2026)

If you're self-employed in Polk County and your health insurance premium just jumped 30%+, you're not imagining things. Florida's ACA Marketplace rates went up an average of 31.5% this year.

For freelancers, contractors, small business owners, and gig workers buying their own coverage, that's a real hit — but something changed in 2026 that most people haven't heard about, and it could reshape how you think about your plan.

Every Bronze Marketplace Plan Is Now HSA-Eligible

Starting January 1, 2026, all Bronze and Catastrophic plans on the ACA Marketplace qualify as High Deductible Health Plans under IRS rules. That means they can be paired with a Health Savings Account.

This wasn't the case before. Previously, most Marketplace plans didn't meet the HDHP thresholds, so the HSA option was effectively off the table for anyone buying through HealthCare.gov. The Working Families Tax Cuts Act changed that.

What an HSA Actually Does for You

An HSA is a tax-advantaged account that works three ways at once.

1. Tax-Deductible Contributions

If you're self-employed and filing a Schedule C, that's money directly off your taxable income — not just an itemized deduction, but an above-the-line deduction that reduces your AGI. That can also affect your ACA subsidy calculation.

2. Tax-Free Growth

You can invest HSA funds the same way you'd invest a retirement account. Over time, the balance compounds without triggering capital gains.

3. Tax-Free Qualified Withdrawals

Doctor visits, prescriptions, lab work, dental, vision, mental health — all covered. No receipts to submit to an insurance company. You just pay from the account.

2026 Contribution Limits

The IRS set the 2026 HSA limits at $4,400 for individual coverage and $8,750 for family coverage. If you're 55 or older, you can add another $1,000 catch-up contribution.

Those limits went up slightly from 2025 ($4,300 individual / $8,550 family), and the money rolls over year to year. There's no "use it or lose it" like an FSA.

The Bronze + HSA Strategy for Self-Employed Workers

Here's why this matters right now in the context of the 31.5% rate increase.

Bronze plans carry the lowest premiums on the Marketplace. The tradeoff is a higher deductible — but now that the deductible qualifies you for an HSA, that tradeoff comes with a tax benefit attached.

How the Math Works

Take the premium savings from choosing Bronze over Silver or Gold, redirect some of that savings into an HSA, and reduce your federal tax bill in the process. If you're healthy and don't expect heavy medical utilization, the net cost of coverage can drop meaningfully.

This is especially relevant for 1099 workers already managing their own taxes. The HSA deduction stacks with the self-employed health insurance deduction you're already taking. It's one of the few legal ways to get a double tax benefit on healthcare spending.

Who This Works Best For

The Bronze + HSA combo isn't for everyone. It tends to work well for people who:

  • Are generally healthy and don't have ongoing specialist care or expensive prescriptions.
  • Have enough cash flow to absorb the higher deductible if something comes up.
  • Want to build a long-term medical savings cushion (HSA funds never expire).
  • Are looking for every available tax deduction during a high-income year.

When Bronze + HSA Is the Wrong Choice

It's less ideal if you have chronic conditions requiring frequent care. A Silver plan with Cost-Sharing Reductions may deliver better value — especially if your income falls between 100% and 250% of the federal poverty level. Run both scenarios before committing.

One Thing to Watch: Subsidy Interaction

Here's a nuance worth flagging. Your HSA contributions reduce your AGI, and your ACA subsidy is calculated based on your projected income. For some self-employed individuals, contributing to an HSA could actually increase your premium tax credit by lowering your modified adjusted gross income.

What this means for you in Lakeland

This isn't guaranteed — it depends on where you fall on the FPL table and your total household income — but it's worth modeling. A good broker can run both scenarios side-by-side. For a self-employed Polk County household close to a subsidy bracket threshold, the right HSA contribution can move you into a better tax-credit tier.

What to Do Now

If you're in Lakeland, Winter Haven, Bartow, or anywhere in Polk County and you're self-employed with Marketplace coverage, this is worth a 10-minute conversation. Even if you're already on a Bronze plan, you may not have opened an HSA because it wasn't eligible before.

The rules changed. The math changed. The opportunity is sitting there.

Self-Employed Action List (2026)

  1. Confirm your current Marketplace plan tier. If it's already Bronze, it's HSA-eligible as of January 1, 2026.
  2. Open an HSA with a qualified custodian — most major banks and brokerages now offer them with low or no fees.
  3. Model your AGI both ways. Compare your projected ACA subsidy with and without a planned HSA contribution.
  4. Stack the deductions. The HSA deduction is separate from the self-employed health insurance deduction — you can take both.
  5. Book a plan review before Open Enrollment. November fills up fast; getting your strategy locked in early is worth it.
