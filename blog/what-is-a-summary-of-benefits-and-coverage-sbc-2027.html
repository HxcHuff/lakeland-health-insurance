How to Read a 2027 Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC) in 5 Minutes

Marketing brochures highlight $0 primary-care visits while hiding $9,000 hospital deductibles or 40% coinsurance for MRIs and brand drugs. Federal law requires every carrier to give you the standardized SBC document - here is how to spot hidden traps in five minutes.

Direct answer: Every ACA Marketplace and employer health plan must publish a standardized Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC) using identical rows so you can compare plans apples-to-apples. Check six numbers first: (1) embedded vs. aggregate family deductible, (2) whether prescriptions are subject to the deductible, (3) out-of-pocket maximum ($12,000 cap in 2027), (4) out-of-network coverage (usually $0 on HMOs/EPOs), (5) ER/imaging coinsurance, and (6) the exact HIOS Plan ID.

Why Embedded vs. Aggregate Family Deductibles Matter So Much

On a family plan with an embedded deductible (e.g., $4,000 individual / $8,000 family), once one family member incurs $4,000 in covered bills, the plan starts paying its share for that person even if the rest of the family spent $0. On an aggregate (non-embedded) deductible - common on employer HDHPs - a single hospitalized spouse must pay the entire $8,000 family deductible before coinsurance starts.

Also verify the exact 14-character HIOS Plan ID (or CMS H-number/PBP for Medicare) on Page 1 of the SBC before checking your doctor's name in the carrier directory, because carriers routinely operate multiple narrow sub-networks under the same brand name.

3 Fast Steps to Compare Two 2027 Plans Side by Side

  1. Calculate Annual Fixed Cost + Expected Care: Multiply monthly net premium × 12, then add your expected Tier 1-4 drug fills and specialist visits - not just the headline premium.
  2. Check the Worst-Case Ceiling: Add 12 months of net premiums + the plan's Out-of-Pocket Maximum (capped at $12,000 individual / $24,000 family in 2027, or far less on CSR Silver).
  3. Cross-Check Provider & Formulary Links on Page 1: Every SBC prints the exact URL and phone number for that plan's provider directory and drug formulary - never use a generic carrier search without entering the Plan ID.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I find a plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)?

On HealthCare.gov, click 'Plan Details' and scroll to 'Summary of Benefits and Coverage' PDF. For employer plans, HR must provide the SBC before open enrollment or within 7 business days of a request.

What does 'deductible does not apply' mean on an SBC?

It means you pay only the stated copay or coinsurance right away on Day 1 without having to satisfy your annual deductible first.

Do premiums count toward my deductible or out-of-pocket maximum?

No. Monthly premiums, out-of-network charges, and non-covered services never count toward your in-network deductible or annual out-of-pocket maximum.

What is the difference between an HMO, EPO, and PPO on an SBC?

HMOs typically require a primary care referral and cover in-network only; EPOs usually require no referral but still cover in-network only (except emergencies); PPOs cover out-of-network care at higher cost-sharing.
