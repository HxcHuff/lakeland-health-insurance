When people review health insurance, they often start with the monthly premium. That number is easy to compare, and it matters. But one of the biggest areas people overlook is prescription drug cost.

As you prepare for 2027 coverage, now is the time to look closely at your medications, your doctor visits, and any health changes that could affect next year's plan choice. Waiting until the last minute can leave you making a rushed decision without the information you really need.

Simple planning move: Schedule a doctor visit or checkup before next year. That gives you time to see the doctor, complete any tests, get results back, and adjust medications if needed before choosing 2027 coverage.

Why Prescription Costs Matter

Two health insurance plans can look similar on the surface but handle prescriptions very differently. One plan may cover your medication at a lower copay. Another may place the same medication on a higher tier, require prior authorization, or cover it differently at your preferred pharmacy.

If you take regular prescriptions, those differences can add up quickly over the course of a year. That is why prescription costs should be part of the plan review before enrollment, not a surprise after the new year begins.

Schedule a Checkup Before 2027

A doctor visit before the end of the year can give you a clearer picture of what your medication needs may look like going into 2027. Your doctor may want to review your current prescriptions, update dosages, order lab work, or talk through symptoms that could affect your care plan.

If tests are needed, you also want time to get results back. A medication change, new diagnosis, dosage adjustment, or switch to a generic can all affect which health plan is the better fit.

Give Medication Changes Time

Medication changes are not always instant. Sometimes your doctor may want to try a different dosage, monitor side effects, repeat lab work, or see whether a new medication is working before making a final decision.

Preparing early gives you room to:

  • See your doctor before the year-end rush
  • Complete any needed lab work or testing
  • Review test results with your provider
  • Adjust medications if necessary
  • Confirm your updated prescription list
  • Compare 2027 plan options more accurately

Bring an Updated Prescription List

Before reviewing 2027 coverage, write down each medication you take, including the drug name, dosage, how often you take it, and the pharmacy you prefer. If you have recently changed medications, include the new prescription and remove anything you no longer take.

This helps estimate your real annual cost. A plan with a lower premium may not be the lowest-cost option if your prescriptions are expensive under that plan.

Make a Sound Decision

Health insurance decisions are easier when you have the right information in front of you. By scheduling a checkup, reviewing prescriptions, and addressing medication changes before the new year, you put yourself in a better position to choose wisely for 2027.

Do not wait until enrollment season is almost over to find out that your medication list changed or that you still need test results. Prepare now so your 2027 decision is based on what you actually need.

Frequently Asked Questions

What prescription details should I compare before choosing 2027 health coverage?

List each medication's exact name, dosage, frequency, and preferred pharmacy. Then compare formulary status, tier, copay or coinsurance, prior authorization, quantity limits, and preferred-pharmacy pricing for each plan.

Do Marketplace health plans cover prescription drugs?

Marketplace plans cover prescription drugs, but each plan can use a different formulary, cost-sharing structure, pharmacy network, and utilization rules. Verify the exact medication rather than relying on the benefit category alone.

Why should I review prescriptions before enrollment begins?

An earlier review gives you time to complete a checkup, receive test results, confirm medication changes with your clinician, and compare plans using an accurate prescription list.
