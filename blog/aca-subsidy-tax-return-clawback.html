ACA Subsidy Clawback: What Happens When Income Is Reported Wrong

By David Huff | Licensed FL Agent #W371813 | Updated August 12, 2026
Home > Blog > ACA Subsidy Clawback

Review Your Marketplace Application and Tax Records

The income and household information on a Marketplace application affects advance premium tax credit eligibility. If the application does not reflect the consumer's reasonable current estimate, update it promptly and keep a record of the change.

How Incorrect Information Can Appear

An application can become inaccurate after an income or household change, through a data-entry mistake, or through an enrollment completed without the consumer's informed review. An agent must not invent, alter, or optimize eligibility facts. The consumer or properly authorized representative should verify the application before enrollment and retain access to the HealthCare.gov account.

What Happens at Tax Time: Form 8962 Reconciliation

When advance payments of the premium tax credit were paid for Marketplace coverage, the tax filer generally uses IRS Form 8962 to compare those advance payments with the premium tax credit allowed using the final federal tax return information. If advance payments exceed the allowed credit, the difference can reduce a refund or increase the balance due. If advance payments were lower, the tax filer may qualify for an additional credit.

Repayment Rules Changed After 2025

2025 tax year: The IRS instructions list repayment limitations for households below 400% FPL. For single filers, the listed limits are $375, $975, and $1,625 across the three income bands below 400% FPL; other filing statuses use different amounts. At 400% FPL or above, the 2025 limitation does not apply.

Tax years after 2025: IRS guidance states that there is no repayment cap. The full amount by which advance payments exceed the allowed premium tax credit is added to tax liability.

The actual result is tax-return specific. Use the applicable Form 8962 instructions and consult a qualified tax professional when needed.

2025 vs. 2026 Reconciliation Rules

Tax year Repayment treatment Income eligibility rule
2025 Income- and filing-status-based limits may apply below 400% FPL Temporary enhanced-credit rules removed the upper-income limit
2026 No repayment cap for excess advance payments In general, premium tax credit eligibility ends above 400% FPL

Warning Signs That Require Review

Watch for These Warning Signs

  • The application income or household information does not match what you provided.
  • The enrollment was completed without your informed consent or without an authorized representative.
  • You cannot access your own HealthCare.gov account or application.
  • You were not shown the eligibility information before plan selection.
  • Your Form 1095-A shows advance premium tax credit amounts or coverage months you do not recognize.

How to Check Right Now

Verify Your Current Application

  1. Go to HealthCare.gov and log into your account
  2. If you can't log in, use the "Forgot Password" option to reset it
  3. Navigate to your active application
  4. Find the reported household income on the application
  5. Compare it with a reasonable estimate based on current, documented household information
  6. Review household members, tax-filing assumptions, current coverage, and contact information
  7. If information is wrong, correct it through HealthCare.gov and keep the confirmation

What to Do If This Happened to You

  1. Correct current information through HealthCare.gov. A correction affects prospective eligibility; it does not itself determine the final tax result.
  2. Call the Marketplace Call Center at 1-800-318-2596 if you see an enrollment or application change you did not authorize.
  3. Report suspected agent misconduct to the Florida Department of Financial Services through its official consumer-services process.
  4. Consult a qualified tax professional about Form 8962, amended returns, repayment, or payment options.
  5. Preserve relevant records. Keep application notices, confirmations, emails, and other communications without sending sensitive documents through unsecured channels.

The 400% FPL Limit Returned for 2026

The temporary rule allowing otherwise eligible households above 400% FPL to receive a premium tax credit expired after 2025. For 2026, the IRS states that eligible household income is generally at least 100% and no more than 400% FPL. Eligibility also depends on tax filing, access to other minimum essential coverage, Marketplace enrollment, and other rules.

Because the credit amount depends on household-specific information and benchmark-plan premiums, do not rely on a generic dollar example to predict eligibility or repayment.

What a Compliant Enrollment Should Include

A licensed agent should obtain and document required consumer consent, verify eligibility facts directly with the consumer or authorized representative, explain that advance credits are reconciled on the federal tax return, and allow the consumer to review the application before submission. Compensation arrangements never justify changing eligibility information.

How to Protect Yourself Going Forward

  • Always verify your own HealthCare.gov account access. You should know your username and password. Never let an agent lock you out of your own account.
  • Review your Form 1095-A annually. Confirm coverage months, enrollment premiums, benchmark premiums, and advance credit amounts.
  • Use a reasonable household-income estimate. Base it on current, documented information and update material changes.
  • Work with a broker who explains the process transparently. A good agent walks you through the application, shows you the numbers, and explains what happens at tax time.
  • Do not share account credentials. Maintain control of your Marketplace login and use secure channels for required documents.
