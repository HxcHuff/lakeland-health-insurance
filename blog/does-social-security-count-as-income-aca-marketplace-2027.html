Social Security & MAGI Guide (2027)

Does Social Security Count as Income for ACA Marketplace or Medicare?

Early retirees ages 62 to 64 and disabled workers on SSDI get tripped up by Social Security MAGI rules more than almost any other line on a Marketplace application. Entering only the taxable slice or forgetting Box 5 gross benefits can cause a huge Form 8962 repayment surprise.

Direct answer: Yes for ACA Marketplace subsidies: HealthCare.gov counts 100% of Social Security retirement and SSDI benefits (both the taxable and non-taxable portion, entered as the full gross amount on Form SSA-1099 Box 5 before Medicare or tax withholdings). Supplemental Security Income (SSI) does NOT count. For Medicare IRMAA, only the taxable portion of Social Security included in your AGI counts.

Two Surprising Ways Social Security Impacts Ages 62 to 64 Shoppers

  • 1. Escaping Florida's Medicaid Coverage Gap (100% FPL Floor): In non-expansion states like Florida, adults under 65 need at least 100% of the Federal Poverty Level ($15,960 for a single adult in 2027) in projected MAGI to unlock ACA Marketplace subsidies. Because 100% of Social Security counts toward MAGI - even when $0 of it is federally taxed - starting Social Security at age 62 can push an early retiree above $15,960 and unlock substantial Marketplace subsidies until age 65!
  • 2. Avoiding an Uncapped 2026-2027 Form 8962 Tax Shock: Conversely, if a 63-year-old couple takes $36,000 in Social Security plus $55,000 from a 401(k) or part-time work and only enters the taxable fraction of Social Security on HealthCare.gov, their actual Form 8962 MAGI adds back the untaxed Social Security portion - potentially crossing the 400% FPL cliff and triggering 100% repayment of the entire year's ACA subsidies.

3 Quick Steps to Enter Social Security Correctly on HealthCare.gov

  1. Use Gross Monthly Benefit (Before Deductions): Enter your full gross monthly Social Security payment before federal tax withholding or Part B deductions - not the net direct-deposit number hitting your bank account.
  2. Never Enter SSI (Supplemental Security Income): Check your award letter carefully: SSDI and Retirement count 100%, while means-tested SSI is left completely off the ACA income total.
  3. Watch the 24-Month SSDI Medicare Transition: If you receive SSDI, Medicare automatically starts on your 25th month of entitlement, at which point you must end ACA Marketplace subsidies so you do not owe back APTC for overlapping Medicare months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I include non-taxable Social Security on my HealthCare.gov application?

Yes. ACA Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) specifically adds back non-taxable Social Security retirement and SSDI benefits to your Adjusted Gross Income, so you must enter 100% of your gross benefit.

Does SSI count as income for Marketplace health insurance?

No. Supplemental Security Income (SSI) is excluded from ACA Marketplace MAGI and should not be added to your household income estimate.

If my spouse is on Medicare and collects Social Security, does their Social Security count for my ACA plan?

Yes. On a joint tax return, HealthCare.gov counts total household MAGI - including the Medicare spouse's full gross Social Security benefit - even though only the under-65 spouse is applying for an ACA plan.

Does Social Security count toward Medicare IRMAA?

Only the taxable portion of Social Security that flows into your Form 1040 Adjusted Gross Income counts toward Medicare IRMAA; non-taxable Social Security is not added back for IRMAA.
