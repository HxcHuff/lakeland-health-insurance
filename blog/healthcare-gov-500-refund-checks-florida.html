$500 HealthCare.gov Refund Checks in Florida: Who Gets One

October 2, 2026 · Updated as payments begin landing

Quick answer: The Treasury began sending $500 payments on September 30, 2026, to more than 950,000 HealthCare.gov enrollees in 30 states, including about 127,900 people in Florida. Official announcements describe the money going out automatically. A real payment does not require an application, a fee, or a bank-account number shared by phone or text.

Checks and letters are arriving just as Florida shoppers start preparing for 2027 Open Enrollment. That timing makes this a trust question as much as a money question. Some households will receive a legitimate Treasury payment. Others will see copycat calls, texts, and emails that try to collect a Social Security number, a bank account, or a processing fee.

This page explains what public sources say about the payment, who is typically included, and how to stay with official channels. It does not estimate anyone's eligibility or treat the $500 as a substitute for reviewing 2027 coverage.

What is being mailed

On September 30, 2026, CNBC reported that the Treasury started sending $500 payments to more than 950,000 Affordable Care Act Marketplace enrollees. An administration official told CNBC that some families with more than one affected person may receive more than one $500 check or direct deposit. Recipients are also being sent a letter dated September 30.

The White House fact sheet published September 10 describes the payments as $500 per person to nearly 1 million Americans in the 30 states that use the federal exchange. The administration says the money comes from a surplus of HealthCare.gov user fees collected from insurers and passed through in premiums. Florida is on that state list.

The payment is one-time. It does not change a 2027 premium, deductible, provider network, or formulary.

Who is typically receiving one

CNBC, citing an administration official, said primary recipients are people whose income is over 400 percent of the federal poverty line, roughly $63,000 for an individual and $129,000 for a family of four. That group lost premium tax credit help after the enhanced credits expired at the end of 2025. The same official said some people between 100 percent and 400 percent of the poverty line may also receive a payment.

CNBC also reported federal data showing Texas and Florida as the two largest state counts, about 139,000 and 127,900 people. Those figures describe estimated recipients, not every Florida Marketplace enrollee. People in states that run their own marketplace are outside this mailing.

If a household did not buy HealthCare.gov coverage without premium assistance, a message promising a $500 check is a reason to pause, not a reason to reply.

Do you have to apply?

No official announcement describes an application window, a claim form, or a fee. The White House said checks would be sent to eligible Americans beginning in October 2026. CNBC then reported that mailing and deposits began September 30.

That means a real payment should arrive on its own. Nobody needs to call Lakeland Health Insurance, HealthCare.gov, or the Treasury and ask us to "release" a check. If a message says the money will expire unless you verify identity, pay a processing fee, buy a gift card, or click a link, treat it as a copycat.

If you are unsure whether a household is in the recipient group, use HealthCare.gov or the Marketplace Call Center at 1-800-318-2596. Do not use a number from an unexpected text or voicemail.

How to recognize copycat messages

Government-payment mailings attract impersonation attempts. The Federal Trade Commission says government agencies will never call, email, text, or message you on social media to ask for money or personal information. Caller ID can be faked. Official HealthCare.gov pages use a .gov address.

Warning signs include:

  • A request for a Social Security number, Medicare number, or bank account to "unlock" the $500
  • A processing fee, gift card, wire, cryptocurrency, or payment-app demand
  • A link in a text or email that is not healthcare.gov or another .gov site
  • Urgent language that the check will be canceled unless you act immediately
  • A refund offer sent to someone who did not have HealthCare.gov coverage

Compare a paper check with the accompanying letter and look for signs of tampering before depositing it. If something looks wrong, call your bank at the number on your card, not a number in the message. Report suspected impersonation at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If personal information was already shared, start at IdentityTheft.gov.

What this payment does not replace

Open Enrollment for 2027 Marketplace coverage starts November 1. December 15 is the last day to enroll or change for coverage that starts January 1. Open Enrollment ends January 15; enrollments from December 16 through January 15 generally start February 1. Confirm those dates on HealthCare.gov's dates and deadlines page.

A $500 payment does not answer whether a 2027 plan still includes your doctors or prescriptions. If a current Marketplace plan is ending, read the transition letter and compare the matched plan before December 15. Start with what to do when a Marketplace plan is ending in 2027.

Officials have not published a clear public statement on whether the payment is taxable. Keep the letter with tax records and ask a tax professional if that question comes up next spring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to apply for the $500 HealthCare.gov payment?

Official announcements describe the Treasury sending $500 payments to eligible HealthCare.gov enrollees. They do not describe an application, processing fee, or a requirement to share a bank account number by phone or text.

Who is typically receiving a $500 HealthCare.gov payment in Florida?

CNBC reported that about 127,900 Floridians are among more than 950,000 recipients in 30 HealthCare.gov states. Recipients are largely people who bought Marketplace coverage without premium tax credits, often with income above 400 percent of the federal poverty line.

Does the $500 payment lower 2027 Marketplace premiums?

No. The payment is a one-time Treasury disbursement. It does not change 2027 plan premiums, deductibles, networks, or formularies. Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage still starts November 1.

What should I do if someone contacts me about a $500 refund?

Treat unexpected calls, texts, emails, or social messages that ask for money, a fee, a Social Security number, or bank details as impersonation attempts. The FTC says government agencies will not contact you that way to request money or personal information. Use HealthCare.gov or the Marketplace Call Center at 1-800-318-2596.
