Florida ACA Premiums Up 31.5% for 2026

By David Huff | Licensed FL Broker #W371813 | Published May 11, 2026 | Updated July 26, 2026
Home > Blog > Florida ACA Premiums Up 31.5% for 2026

If you live in Polk County and buy your own health insurance, the 2026 ACA market deserves a fresh review. Florida's individual ACA marketplace premiums increased by a weighted average of 31.5% for the 2026 plan year before subsidies.

The source trail matters. Florida's 2026 individual market filings are maintained by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation, and independent premium datasets from KFF show Florida benchmark and metal-tier premiums moving sharply higher for 2026.

Why Florida ACA Premiums Increased

Several pressures hit the individual market at the same time.

First, enhanced premium tax credits expired after 2025. Those temporary credits had reduced net monthly premiums for many Marketplace households, especially in Florida, where ACA enrollment was unusually high. When the enhanced credits ended, many households saw a larger bill even before changing plans.

Second, underlying healthcare costs continued to rise. Hospital contracts, specialty drugs, provider utilization, and claims experience all affect what carriers file for the next plan year.

Third, Marketplace enrollment rules changed. CMS finalized the Marketplace Integrity and Affordability Final Rule, including tighter verification and Special Enrollment Period controls. Program integrity matters, but enrollment changes can also affect the risk pool carriers price against.

What This Means for Lakeland and Polk County

Polk County residents are exposed because many local households buy their own coverage: self-employed workers, small business owners, early retirees not yet eligible for Medicare, and families without affordable employer coverage.

The number on the bill is not the whole decision. A higher statewide average does not mean every Lakeland household saw the same increase. Final cost still depends on ZIP code, household income, age, household size, tobacco status, subsidy eligibility, plan metal level, carrier, network, and prescriptions.

Why the Risk Pool Matters

When healthier members leave coverage, the remaining pool tends to use more care. Carriers price future rates against that claims experience. That is why affordability, enrollment integrity, and continuous coverage all matter in the ACA market.

You Might Still Have Options

You may not have to wait until the next Open Enrollment window to get covered or correct a plan problem.

If you've experienced a qualifying life event in the past 60 days, you may be eligible for a Special Enrollment Period right now. Qualifying events include:

  • Losing job-based coverage
  • Getting married or divorced
  • Having a baby or adopting a child
  • Moving to a new county
  • Losing Medicaid or CHIP eligibility
  • Aging off a parent's plan at 26

Even without a life event, it is worth checking whether your current plan is still the right fit. Some carriers adjusted benefits, drug formularies, and networks for 2026. A plan that worked last year may not be the best fit now.

What to Compare Before You Switch

Do not compare only the monthly premium. Review the total annual cost: premium, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, primary care, specialists, hospitals, prescriptions, and whether your subsidy estimate is current. A lower premium with the wrong network can cost more at the point of care.

Use Current Plan Prices Before Deciding

For an initial check, use the official HealthCare.gov plans and prices tool. It is the right starting point because it uses current Marketplace plan data and asks for the ZIP code where coverage will be used.

Your 2026 ACA Premium Action List

  1. Pull your latest premium statement. Compare it side-by-side with your 2025 statement. A 25%+ jump is your signal to shop.
  2. List any qualifying life events from the past 60 days. Any one of them may open a Special Enrollment Period.
  3. Calculate your total annual cost — not just premium — on your current plan vs. one or two alternatives.
  4. Check your doctors and prescriptions before switching. Network and formulary details matter more when premiums are already higher.
  5. Request a plan review before Open Enrollment so your decisions are ready, not rushed.
