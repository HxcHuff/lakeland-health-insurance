What Counts as Creditable Coverage for Medicare Part B and Part D?

Mixing up Part B creditable coverage rules with Part D rules is the #1 cause of permanent lifetime Medicare penalties. Before delaying Medicare at 65, verify your current coverage against the exact table below.

Direct answer: Medicare uses two completely different rules for Part B vs. Part D. To delay Part B safely, you need group health coverage based on current active employment (yours or your spouse's). Under 42 CFR 407.20, active employment drives Part B Special Enrollment Period eligibility, while the 20-employee rule decides who pays first: at companies with 20+ employees, the group plan pays primary; with fewer than 20 employees, Medicare pays primary at 65, so delaying Part B risks unpaid medical claims if the small-group plan refuses to pay first. COBRA, retiree plans, ACA Marketplace plans, and VA benefits do not qualify as current-employment coverage for Part B. For Part D, employer drug plans meeting CMS actuarial value, VA pharmacy benefits, and TRICARE do count as creditable.

The COBRA Trap & Form CMS-L564 Checklist

The single most expensive mistake seniors make is taking 18 months of COBRA after retiring at 65 and waiting until COBRA ends to sign up for Part B. Because COBRA is not based on current employment, your 8-month Medicare Part B Special Enrollment Period begins when your employment or active group coverage ends, whichever happens first. Waiting 18 months means missing your SEP, facing a coverage gap until General Enrollment, and paying a permanent 10% lifetime Part B penalty (+$20.94/mo on the 2027 $209.40 premium).

When you leave a qualifying 20+ employee active plan, submit two forms together to Social Security: Form CMS-40B (Application for Enrollment in Part B) and Form CMS-L564 (Request for Employment Information, Section B signed by HR).

3 Things to Request From HR Before Turning 65 or Retiring

  1. Exact Company Headcount (20+ vs. Under 20): Confirm whether your employer has 20 or more full- and part-time employees for 20+ weeks (or participates in a multi-employer plan with at least one 20+ employer).
  2. Annual Notice of Creditable Prescription Drug Coverage: Employers must send this letter every October before AEP. Keep every year's copy in a folder to prevent Part D penalty letters later.
  3. Completed Form CMS-L564 Signed by HR: Collect this 30 to 60 days before your last day of work so your Part B starts smoothly on the first of the month you retire with zero gap and zero penalty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does COBRA count as creditable coverage for Medicare Part B?

No. COBRA does not count as creditable coverage to delay Medicare Part B. Your 8-month Part B Special Enrollment Period starts when active employment ends, not when COBRA ends.

Does VA healthcare count as creditable coverage for Medicare?

VA drug benefits count as creditable coverage for Medicare Part D, but VA care does not count as creditable coverage for Medicare Part B. Delaying Part B while relying only on the VA triggers the 10% per year Part B penalty if you enroll later.

What form proves I had creditable employer coverage for Part B?

Form CMS-L564 (Request for Employment Information), with Section B signed by your employer's HR department, submitted alongside Form CMS-40B to Social Security.

Can I keep my ACA Marketplace plan after turning 65 instead of Medicare?

If you qualify for $0-premium Part A at 65, Marketplace premium tax credits end and an ACA plan does not count as creditable coverage to delay Medicare Part B or Part D.
