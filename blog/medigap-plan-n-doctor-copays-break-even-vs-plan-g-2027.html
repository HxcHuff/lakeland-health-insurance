How Many Doctor Trips on Medigap Plan N Is Too Much? (2027 Break-Even Math vs. Plan G)

Seniors comparing Medigap Plan G and Plan N often worry about getting nickel-and-dimed by Plan N's office-visit copays. Running the exact 2027 break-even math shows quickly whether Plan N saves you hundreds every year or whether Plan G makes more sense for your health routine.

Direct answer: Divide your annual premium savings between Plan N and Plan G by $20. If Plan N saves you $40/month ($480/year) compared to Plan G, you would have to pay a full $20 copay at 24 separate doctor visits per year (2 every single month) just to break even - and preventive visits, labs, imaging-only visits, telehealth, and hospital inpatient stays carry $0 copays on Plan N.

Which Visits Actually Charge the $20 Copay (And What About Excess Charges)?

  • When the up-to-$20 Copay Applies: Only after you satisfy the 2027 $292 Part B annual deductible (which both Plan G and Plan N require you to pay) AND only on standard Part B office evaluation visits. Emergency room visits that do not result in inpatient admission charge up to $50 (waived if admitted).
  • Visits That Usually Cost $0 on Plan N: Annual Medicare wellness/preventive exams, standalone bloodwork/lab draws, X-rays/MRIs billed separately, outpatient surgery facility claims, chemotherapy infusions, and inpatient hospital stays (Plan N covers 100% of the 2027 $1,776 Part A deductible and $222/day SNF coinsurance just like Plan G!).
  • How Part B Excess Charges Work: Plan G covers Part B excess charges while Plan N does not. Under federal law, only doctors who opt out of Medicare assignment can bill an excess charge (capped at 15% above the Medicare-approved rate) - and roughly 98% of Medicare-participating doctors accept assignment (plus 8 states ban or strictly limit Part B excess charges). Ask your doctors one question: "Do you accept Medicare assignment?"

Frequently Asked Questions

How much are doctor copays on Medigap Plan N?

After the $292 Part B annual deductible in 2027, Medigap Plan N charges up to $20 per office visit (often less if 20% of the Medicare-approved charge is under $20) and up to $50 for emergency room visits that do not result in an inpatient hospital admission.

Is there an annual cap on Plan N $20 office copays?

No, Plan N does not have a separate annual cap on the $20 office copays, which is why running the annual premium break-even math (yearly savings divided by $20) is the smartest way to compare it against Plan G.

Do you pay a $20 Plan N copay for lab work, urgent care, or physical therapy?

Standard preventive visits, standalone labs, and inpatient hospital stays have $0 copays, while urgent care or specialist office visits billed under office-visit CPT codes can trigger up to $20. Exact billing depends on the provider's CPT code.

Can I switch from Medigap Plan N to Plan G later if my doctor visits increase?

In Florida and most states, upgrading from Plan N to Plan G later requires passing medical underwriting unless you qualify for a federal Guaranteed Issue event or live in a state with a Medigap birthday/anniversary rule. Factor future health changes into your choice upfront.
