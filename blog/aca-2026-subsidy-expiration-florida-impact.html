ACA Subsidy Changes in 2026: What Florida Families Should Review Now

By David | Licensed FL Agent #W371813 | Published April 15, 2026 | Updated August 12, 2026
Home > Blog > ACA 2026 Subsidy Expiration

Current review: The temporary enhanced premium tax credit rules expired after 2025. Standard premium tax credits remain available to eligible households in 2026, but the applicable-percentage table, the 400% FPL upper-income limit, and reconciliation rules differ from 2025. Review the current Marketplace determination before changing coverage.

What changed for 2026

A. The temporary enhanced-credit rules ended

The American Rescue Plan temporarily expanded premium tax credits for 2021 and 2022, and the Inflation Reduction Act extended those changes through 2025. The temporary rules removed the 400% FPL upper-income limit and used a more generous applicable-percentage table.

For 2026, the IRS states that premium tax credit eligibility is generally limited to households with income from 100% through 400% FPL, subject to the other tax and coverage requirements. The 2026 applicable-percentage table ranges from 2.10% below 133% FPL to 9.96% from 300% through 400% FPL.

Standard Premium Tax Credits Still Exist

The credit did not disappear. Eligibility and amount depend on household income, family size, tax filing, access to other coverage, age, location, and the local benchmark Silver premium. Confirm the current Marketplace result rather than relying on a statewide average or prior-year premium.

How the change can affect a household

A household may see a different net premium because the temporary credit formula ended, the available plans or benchmark premium changed, or the household's income, age, location, or family size changed. Those factors can move in different directions, so a national percentage or another customer's premium is not a reliable estimate for a Florida household.

Review These Inputs

  • Expected 2026 household income and tax-filing relationships
  • Eligibility for employer coverage, Medicare, Medicaid, or other minimum essential coverage
  • Current plan premium, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, network, and formulary
  • The Marketplace's current eligibility notice and advance-credit amount

2026 Marketplace verification rules

CMS finalized temporary 2026 program-integrity rules affecting automatic re-enrollment and eligibility verification on the federal platform. Their application depends on the enrollee's circumstances and current Marketplace record.

Key points to verify

  • Automatic re-enrollment with no premium responsibility: On the federal platform, a consumer who is automatically re-enrolled at $0 after advance credits without affirming or updating eligibility information is assigned a $5 monthly premium. CMS states that the $5 amount is removed if the consumer confirms or updates the information and remains eligible for a $0 premium.
  • Income data mismatch: When IRS tax-return data is unavailable, the Marketplace may require other trusted data or documents to resolve the inconsistency.
  • Special enrollment: Do not assume income alone creates a midyear enrollment right. Confirm a current Special Enrollment Period through HealthCare.gov before ending or replacing coverage.

Confirm, Do Not Guess

Log in to HealthCare.gov and review the current eligibility notice, household information, and any document request. A $5 premium is not a universal charge on every $0 plan; the rule concerns the specific automatic re-enrollment circumstances described above.

What Florida families should review now

A. Review Your Current Plan

If you auto-renewed for 2026 without reviewing your options, compare the current plan with the plans actually available to your household. Plan designs, networks, formularies, and net premiums can change each year.

B. Check for Special Enrollment Period Eligibility

While the low-income SEP is gone, you may still qualify for a special enrollment period if you've experienced a qualifying life event:

  • Loss of other health coverage (job-based, Medicaid, etc.)
  • Marriage, divorce, or legal separation
  • Birth or adoption of a child
  • Permanent move to a new coverage area

C. Explore All Coverage Options

The marketplace isn't your only option. Depending on your situation, you may benefit from:

  • Employer-sponsored coverage — verify eligibility, affordability, effective date, network, and total cost
  • Marketplace coverage — compare current plans using the household's confirmed eligibility result
  • Other coverage types — review exclusions, renewal limits, preexisting-condition rules, and whether the coverage is ACA-compliant before replacing comprehensive coverage
  • Medicaid or CHIP — use the current Florida eligibility process for household members who may qualify

D. Work With a Licensed Agent

A licensed agent can help review Marketplace information and compare available plans. The Marketplace and IRS determine subsidy and tax eligibility; an agent should not alter household facts or provide tax advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did ACA subsidies go away completely in 2026?

No. Standard premium tax credits still exist for eligible households. The temporary enhanced rules that removed the 400% FPL upper-income limit and used a more generous contribution table expired after 2025.

How much more will I pay for ACA coverage in 2026?

There is no reliable household answer without a current Marketplace determination. The result depends on household eligibility, location, ages, benchmark premiums, and plan selection.

Can I still get help paying for health insurance in Florida?

Potentially. In general, the 2026 income range is 100% through 400% FPL, but tax-filing, other-coverage, Marketplace-enrollment, and other eligibility rules also apply.

What if I can't afford my 2026 premium?

Review the current Marketplace notice and available plans before ending coverage. A plan change usually requires Open Enrollment or a qualifying Special Enrollment Period, and other coverage types may not provide ACA protections.

Expert Recommendations

Here's what I'm telling every client right now:

  1. Do not end coverage before confirming the replacement. Verify eligibility, effective date, benefits, network, prescriptions, and total cost.
  2. Verify Marketplace eligibility information and respond to current document requests.
  3. Compare available Marketplace plans. Plan availability and pricing can change by year, ZIP code, and household eligibility.
  4. Ask a licensed agent for a plan review. Confirm subsidy eligibility, network fit, prescriptions, and renewal alternatives before changing coverage.
  5. Document any qualifying life events in case you need to make a mid-year plan change through a special enrollment period.
