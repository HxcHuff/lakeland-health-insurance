Federal Medicaid Work Requirements: What Florida Residents Should Know

By David Huff | Licensed FL Agent #W371813 | Published May 5, 2026 | Updated August 12, 2026
Home > Blog > Federal Medicaid Work Requirements

Key Takeaways

  • Federal rules begin January 1, 2027, unless a state implements sooner. CMS defines the affected population rather than applying the rule to every Medicaid recipient.
  • Florida has not adopted the ACA adult Medicaid group. The federal rule therefore does not apply to Florida's current traditional Medicaid coverage groups.
  • Nebraska implemented early on May 1, 2026. Its requirements and renewal schedule apply to Nebraska's expansion population, not Florida Medicaid recipients.
  • Florida DCF decides Medicaid eligibility. Coverage group, household size, income, and other facts must be reviewed together.
  • Losing qualifying coverage may open a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period, subject to timing and documentation requirements.

Nebraska implemented Medicaid work requirements for its expansion population on May 1, 2026. Federal rules require states to begin conditioning eligibility for defined adult populations on qualifying activities on January 1, 2027, unless a state implements sooner.

Florida's current Medicaid program does not include the ACA adult group addressed by the federal rule. Florida residents should continue to use Florida DCF for Medicaid eligibility and HealthCare.gov for Marketplace determinations rather than applying another state's rules to their case.

Do Medicaid Work Requirements Apply in Florida?

Current answer: The requirement does not apply to Florida's current traditional Medicaid coverage groups. CMS says it applies to the ACA adult group and certain comparable section 1115 demonstration populations. Florida has not adopted the adult group, and Florida DCF remains the authority for individual eligibility.

CMS says an affected person can satisfy a month through at least 80 hours of work, community service, a qualifying work program, qualifying education, or a combination of those activities. CMS also recognizes earnings equal to 80 hours at the applicable federal minimum wage, currently $580 per month. Exclusions and hardship rules may apply, and states must first use reliable information available to them before asking for additional documentation.

Florida DCF lists family-related coverage groups such as parents and caretakers, pregnant women, infants, children, and certain people ages 19 and 20. Eligibility depends on the applicable coverage group, household size, current income limits, and other rules. Do not rely on a single statewide income number to decide whether to apply.

What Nebraska's Rollout Looks Like

Nebraska DHHS says its rules took effect May 1, 2026, for new applicants eligible through Medicaid expansion. For existing expansion members, Nebraska begins checking at renewal for coverage periods ending on or after July 31, 2026. Nebraska also publishes exclusions and temporary-hardship rules and says it will try to verify compliance from information it already has before requesting more.

How Florida Residents Should Check Eligibility

Use the official decision path: Florida DCF determines Medicaid eligibility, while HealthCare.gov determines Marketplace eligibility and any premium tax credit. A broker can help compare available coverage but cannot make either eligibility decision.

Start with the Florida DCF coverage-group and household-size guide. If DCF determines that you are not eligible for Medicaid, review the notice and use HealthCare.gov's Medicaid-to-Marketplace instructions for the current enrollment and document deadlines.

Who in Polk County Should Pay Attention Anyway?

Even though Florida hasn't implemented work requirements, this story is relevant if any of these apply to you:

  • You have family in another state. Medicaid implementation is state-specific; use that state's Medicaid agency for the current rule and case status.
  • You're considering a move. A permanent move between Florida and another state may support a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period, subject to prior-coverage, timing, and documentation rules.
  • You're between jobs or self-employed. Review current Marketplace eligibility and plan options. The expected-contribution estimator is educational; HealthCare.gov determines the actual credit.
  • You're approaching 65. Review Medicare enrollment timing separately from Medicaid or Marketplace eligibility.

How to Use a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) in Florida

If you lose qualifying coverage, a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period may be available before or after the loss. The exact window, eligibility rules, and documentation requirements vary by event.

Events that may support a Special Enrollment Period include:

  • Losing Medicaid, CHIP, or other minimum essential coverage
  • Job loss or termination of employer-sponsored coverage
  • Aging off a parent's plan at 26
  • Marriage, divorce, birth, or adoption
  • A permanent move to a new ZIP code or state
  • Other circumstances recognized by HealthCare.gov under the current SEP rules

Practical advice for Lakeland residents

Review the coverage-end date and eligibility notice before choosing a next step. Those facts control the relevant enrollment and document deadlines. This job-loss checklist explains the comparison points.

What to Do Right Now

The Polk County Action List

  1. Confirm your current coverage status. Marketplace, traditional Medicaid, Medicare, employer, or uninsured? Each path has different next steps.
  2. If coverage ended or will end, use the exact end date and eligibility notice to check the current Marketplace deadline.
  3. If DCF says you are ineligible, keep the notice and review HealthCare.gov's current Medicaid-to-Marketplace instructions.
  4. If you have family in another state, use that state's Medicaid agency for the current implementation timeline and case rules.
