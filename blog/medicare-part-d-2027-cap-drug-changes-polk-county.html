What Changed in Medicare Part D for 2027

Over the past two plan years, federal Medicare Part D redesign eliminated the old "Donut Hole" coverage gap and introduced a hard annual cap on covered out-of-pocket prescription costs (indexed from $2,000 in 2025 to $2,100 in 2026, with annual inflation indexing for 2027), alongside the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (which lets beneficiaries spread out-of-pocket drug costs into monthly billing statements rather than paying a lump sum at the pharmacy counter).

In addition, the first 10 Medicare Part D medications selected for CMS Maximum Fair Price negotiations—including widely prescribed blood thinners, heart failure medications, and diabetes drugs such as Eliquis, Jardiance, Xarelto, Januvia, Farxiga, Entresto, Enbrel, Imbruvica, Stelara, and NovoLog/Fiasp—moved under negotiated pricing rules starting in 2026 and continuing into 2027.

Why 2027 Drug Formularies & Tiers Shifted in Florida

Because Part D and Medicare Advantage (MAPD) plans now absorb a much larger share of catastrophic prescription costs after a member reaches the annual out-of-pocket cap, many Florida carriers restructured their 2027 Drug Formularies in three ways:

How the Annual Part D Cap & Prescription Payment Plan Work Together

Two important rules govern how the annual Part D out-of-pocket cap protects you in 2027:

  • Only Covered Formulary Drugs Count Toward the Cap: The annual out-of-pocket cap applies strictly to medications that are on your 2027 plan's formulary (or approved through a formal formulary exception) and filled at an in-network pharmacy. If a medication is dropped from your plan's 2027 formulary and you pay cash or use a discount card, those dollars do not count toward your Part D out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Smoothing January Bills with the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan: If you take expensive Tier 3, Tier 4, or Tier 5 prescriptions that hit a high deductible or coinsurance in January and February, you can opt into your carrier's Medicare Prescription Payment Plan. It does not lower your total drug cost, but it spreads your out-of-pocket responsibility into predictable monthly payments across the remaining months of the calendar year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Medicare Part D out-of-pocket cap cover drugs that are not on my plan's 2027 formulary?

No. The annual Part D out-of-pocket cap only counts spending on prescription drugs that are covered on your specific plan's 2027 formulary (or approved via an official formulary exception) and purchased at an in-network pharmacy. If a drug is excluded from your 2027 formulary, you could pay full retail price unless you switch to a plan that covers it during AEP.

Why did my Tier 3 brand drug change from a flat copay to a percentage coinsurance for 2027?

Many Florida Part D and Medicare Advantage plans shifted Tier 3 (Preferred Brand) and Tier 4 (Non-Preferred) drugs from fixed dollar copays to percentage coinsurance (typically 20% to 25%) for 2027 to offset higher plan liability under the redesigned Part D benefit. Comparing total annual drug cost across plans before December 7 helps identify plans that still keep your specific medications on lower cost-sharing tiers.

Are covered insulins still capped at $35 per month in 2027?

Yes. Under federal law, Medicare Part D and Medicare Advantage plans cannot charge more than $35 for a one-month supply of each covered insulin product on the plan's formulary, with no Part D deductible applied to covered insulin. However, you must still verify that your specific insulin brand and delivery pen or vial are on the 2027 formulary.

What is the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan for 2027?

The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan is a voluntary payment option offered by all Medicare Part D and MAPD plans that lets you pay $0 at the pharmacy counter for covered drugs and instead receive a monthly bill from your plan that spreads your out-of-pocket drug costs across the rest of the calendar year.
