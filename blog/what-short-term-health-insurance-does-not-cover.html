What does short-term health insurance not cover?

Short-term, limited-duration insurance is built for a defined temporary gap. It is not a comprehensive ACA plan. What it omits is often more important than what a brochure highlights. Always read the exclusion list, waiting periods, and benefit maximums in the policy form.

How is this different from the full short-term guide on this site?

The short-term health insurance guide covers costs, limits, duration rules, and when an ACA plan or COBRA is the safer comparison. This page isolates exclusions: what these contracts commonly do not cover. Read both before you treat short-term coverage as a substitute for major medical insurance.

The CMS short-term insurance fact sheet explains that short-term, limited-duration insurance is not subject to all federal protections that apply to comprehensive individual health insurance.

Which gaps show up most often?

Pre-existing condition exclusions are common: care related to a condition you already have may be denied. Essential health benefits required of ACA plans, including maternity, mental health, and many prescriptions, may be missing or tightly limited. Preventive care that an ACA plan would cover may not be covered. Dollar maximums and duration limits can end benefits while treatment is still underway. None of those items is theoretical; they are contract terms you must read.

Does short-term coverage create a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period when it ends?

Short-term policies are not qualifying health coverage, so losing one generally does not open a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period. Confirm the event on HealthCare.gov Special Enrollment guidance. Do not buy a short-term policy as a strategy to manufacture a later Marketplace enrollment without reading those rules.

When should I compare ACA or COBRA instead?

If you have ongoing treatment, expensive prescriptions, a pregnancy, or a doctor you must keep, start with ACA or COBRA. See coverage outside Open Enrollment, COBRA versus Marketplace, and the short-term medical information page.

These products are not comprehensive coverage

Short-term, limited-duration insurance is not a substitute for ACA-compliant major medical coverage. It is not ACA-compliant, may use medical underwriting, and can exclude pre-existing conditions, essential benefits, and other care. Eligibility, exclusions, benefit limits, provider access, and effective dates depend on the specific policy.

What underwriting questions change the exclusion list?

A "yes" on a health question can lead to a declination, a rider, or a pre-existing condition exclusion that lasts for the entire short contract. Two applicants can receive two different outlines of coverage. Never shop from a colleague's policy.

Florida law limits short-term plans to a first term under 12 months and 36 months in total, counting renewals, and requires a signed disclosure listing the essential health benefits the policy does not cover (Fla. Stat. §627.6426). A 2024 federal rule set shorter limits, but in August 2025 federal agencies said they would not prioritize enforcing it while they write a new rule. Read the term and renewal limits in the policy itself. A short contract is not a rolling substitute for ACA coverage year after year without reading those limits.

If you have a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period, compare the ACA plan first. Short-term coverage is not the default bridge.

Common questions

Does short-term insurance have to cover pre-existing conditions?

No. These policies can exclude pre-existing conditions. That is one reason they are not equivalent to ACA major medical coverage.

Is short-term coverage the same as a Marketplace plan?

No. Marketplace plans must cover essential health benefits and follow ACA consumer protections. Short-term policies do not.
