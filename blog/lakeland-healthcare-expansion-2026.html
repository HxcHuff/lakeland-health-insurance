Healthcare Boom: Orlando Health, HCA, and BayCare Are All Expanding in Lakeland

Published: February 2026 | 7 min read

Lakeland's Healthcare Landscape Is Changing Fast

If you've driven around Lakeland lately, you've probably noticed construction cranes and new medical buildings popping up everywhere. That's not a coincidence. With over 1,000 people moving to Florida every single day, healthcare systems are racing to keep up with the demand—and Lakeland is ground zero for this expansion.

For decades, Lakeland residents had two main options: Lakeland Regional Health and Watson Clinic. That's changing dramatically in 2026. Four major hospital systems are now competing for your business, which means more choices, more facilities, and—if you pick the right insurance plan—potentially better access to care.

But here's the catch: not every new facility is in every insurance network. Let's break down what's coming and what it means for your coverage.

Orlando Health: The Big New Player

Orlando Health has made the biggest splash in Lakeland's healthcare scene. After acquiring Watson Clinic, they're now building out a major presence in Polk County.

Orlando Health Watson Clinic Lakeland Highlands Hospital

Orlando Health Watson Clinic Lakeland Highlands Hospital

Lakeland Highlands area (near Watson Clinic Highlands)

Opening Summer 2026

A brand-new seven-story, 302-bed hospital being built by Swedish construction firm Skanska. This $109 million facility will bring full hospital services to the Highlands corridor—an area that's been underserved for years.

Orlando Health Freestanding ER at Lakeland Park Center

Orlando Health ER - Lakeland Park Center

Expected 2026

A new freestanding emergency room that will provide 24/7 emergency care without the wait times of a full hospital ER. Located near the I-4 corridor for easy access from North Lakeland and Auburndale.

What This Means for Watson Clinic Patients

If you currently see Watson Clinic doctors, the Orlando Health integration is mostly good news—your doctors now have hospital privileges at the new Highlands Hospital. However, network tiers are shifting. Some plans that had Watson physicians as "Tier 1" may now classify them differently. Always verify your tier status before enrolling in a 2026 plan.

HCA Florida: Urgent Care and Emergency Expansion

HCA Healthcare isn't building a full hospital in Lakeland (yet), but they've aggressively expanded their emergency and urgent care footprint. If you have an HCA-affiliated plan or use their facilities in Tampa or Brandon, this expansion gives you more local options.

Freestanding ER vs. Urgent Care: Know the Difference

Freestanding ERs (like HCA Florida North Lakeland Emergency) charge emergency room prices—even though they look similar to urgent care centers. If you have a non-emergency issue like a cold, sprain, or minor cut, always go to an urgent care facility instead. The cost difference can be thousands of dollars.

BayCare: Coming to South Lakeland

BayCare, the large Tampa Bay-area health system, is also expanding into Polk County with a new freestanding ER.

BayCare Freestanding ER

BayCare's first facility in Lakeland proper. This freestanding ER will serve South Lakeland and the Highland City area with 24/7 emergency services.

What This Means for Your Insurance

More healthcare options sounds great—until you realize that each of these systems has different network contracts with different insurance carriers. Here's the reality:

  • Orlando Health facilities are typically in-network with Florida Blue, Aetna, Cigna, and most major carriers—but not all plan types
  • HCA facilities have strong network participation with UnitedHealthcare and some Aetna plans, but may be out-of-network for certain Florida Blue HMOs
  • BayCare facilities are well-covered by most PPO plans but may have limited HMO participation in Polk County (this is new territory for them)
  • Lakeland Regional Health remains the most broadly covered facility—if you want the widest network access, plans that include LRH are your safest bet

The Network Verification Rule

Before you enroll in any 2026 health plan, call your insurance carrier and ask specifically: "Is [facility name] in-network for emergency services AND for follow-up care?" Freestanding ERs sometimes have different network status than their affiliated hospitals. Get it in writing.

Which Facilities Should You Use?

For True Emergencies (Chest Pain, Stroke, Severe Trauma)

Go to the nearest ER. In a true emergency, federal law (EMTALA) requires any ER to treat you regardless of insurance. Worry about the bill later—your life comes first.

For Urgent But Non-Emergency Issues

Use an urgent care center like MD Now, not a freestanding ER. You'll save hundreds or thousands of dollars. Common urgent care visits include:

  • Flu, cold, or COVID symptoms
  • Minor cuts requiring stitches
  • Sprains and minor fractures
  • UTIs and minor infections
  • Sports physicals and routine screenings

For Scheduled Care and Specialists

This is where network selection matters most. If you want access to Watson Clinic specialists, make sure your plan includes Orlando Health. If you prefer Lakeland Regional's specialists, verify LRH network participation. Don't assume—verify.

The Bottom Line

Lakeland's healthcare expansion is great news for residents—more options, more competition, and potentially shorter wait times. But it also means you need to be smarter about your insurance choices.

The worst mistake you can make is picking a plan based on premium alone, then discovering that the shiny new hospital down the street isn't in your network. A $150/month savings means nothing when you're facing a $15,000 out-of-network bill.
