My Marketplace Plan Is Ending in 2027. What Now?

October 2, 2026 · Carrier-neutral transition checklist

Quick answer: A plan-ending notice means the current 2026 Marketplace plan will not continue as-is. HealthCare.gov will usually match you to another plan. Doctors, hospitals, and prescriptions do not automatically follow that match. Update the application and enroll by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1.

Insurers change or discontinue Marketplace plans every year. In 2027, some Florida households will see a letter that the current plan is ending, that the company is leaving a county, or that the nearest replacement is a different product. The steps are the same no matter which company sent the notice.

This page stays carrier-neutral on purpose. The decision is not "keep the brand." The decision is whether the matched 2027 plan still fits your doctors, medications, income estimate, and first-premium deadline.

What the letters mean

HealthCare.gov says that by November 1 you should get two letters: one from the current insurance company and one from the Marketplace. Together they tell you:

  • Whether the current plan is available next year
  • Which plan, if any, you are matched with if you take no action by December 15
  • Changes in coverage or savings
  • Whether the Marketplace needs documents and how to send them

If the company letter is missing, contact the current plan. If the Marketplace letter is missing, use the Marketplace Call Center. Do not wait until mid-December to discover that a notice never arrived.

If the company is not offering the current plan or a similar plan, HealthCare.gov says you will be matched with an alternate plan. When you update the application and are asked whether you are losing coverage, include that current coverage ends December 31.

What happens if you do nothing

Automatic re-enrollment is designed to prevent a January gap. If you have Marketplace coverage in December and do not choose a plan or stop coverage by December 15, the Marketplace may re-enroll you in:

  • The same plan, if it is still offered
  • A different plan from the same company
  • A comparable plan from a different company, if the current company is not offering similar plans

That match is a safety net, not a recommendation. Plan costs change every year. A different company can use a different network, formulary, referral rule, and premium. Coverage still does not start until the first premium is paid.

If you do not want Marketplace coverage for 2027, HealthCare.gov says to stop re-enrollment no later than December 15 from the current-year application. Waiting until January can leave a January premium in place.

Doctors and prescriptions do not automatically carry over

A matched plan can look similar on metal level and still change the care you use. Before you accept the match, confirm:

  • Each primary doctor and specialist against the exact 2027 plan ID, not only the company name
  • The hospital or outpatient facility you would actually use
  • Each medication by name, dose, tier, prior authorization, and preferred pharmacy

Directories can lag. Call the provider billing office and the pharmacy benefit line on the 2027 ID card or plan documents. A lower premium is not a better fit if one ongoing prescription moves to a higher tier or a specialist is out of network.

Use the provider and prescription check to list each doctor, drug, dose, and pharmacy. Then compare that list against the matched plan and at least one alternative. The annual reminder is in the 2027 prescription-cost checklist.

The December 15 deadline

For plan year 2027, HealthCare.gov Open Enrollment starts November 1. December 15 is the last day to enroll or change for coverage that starts January 1. Open Enrollment ends January 15; enrollments from December 16 through January 15 generally start February 1. Confirm those dates on the HealthCare.gov dates and deadlines page.

December 15 matters twice. It is the last day to replace an unwanted auto-match for January 1 coverage. It is also the last comfortable day to update expected 2027 income so the premium tax credit is based on next year, not last year's leftover estimate.

If the matched plan starts January 1 and you later change plans by January 15, the replacement generally starts February 1. That can leave a one-month stretch on a network or formulary you did not choose.

A short transition checklist

  1. Read both letters and write down the matched 2027 plan name and ID.
  2. Update expected 2027 household income and household size on HealthCare.gov.
  3. Confirm each doctor, facility, and prescription on that exact plan ID.
  4. Compare at least one other available plan using the same list.
  5. Enroll by December 15 and follow the date on the first-premium bill.
  6. Save the confirmation and the new ID card before January appointments.

If a $500 HealthCare.gov payment also arrives this fall, treat it as a separate Treasury mailing. It does not enroll anyone or lock in a 2027 plan. See who is receiving the $500 checks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Marketplace plan-ending letter mean?

By November 1 you should receive one letter from your current insurance company and one from the Marketplace. Together they say whether the current plan is available next year, which plan you are matched with if you take no action by December 15, and whether the Marketplace needs documents.

Do my doctors and prescriptions automatically carry over?

No. A matched or auto-enrolled plan can use a different network and formulary. Confirm each doctor, facility, and medication against the exact 2027 plan ID before you accept the match.

What happens if I do nothing by December 15?

If you have Marketplace coverage in December and do not choose a plan or stop coverage by December 15, HealthCare.gov may automatically re-enroll you. That can be the same plan, a different plan from the same company, or a comparable plan from another company. Coverage still requires the first premium.

When do I need to enroll for January 1, 2027 coverage?

Enroll or change by December 15 and pay the first premium for coverage that starts January 1. Open Enrollment continues through January 15, but enrollments from December 16 through January 15 generally start February 1.
