What Are Prior Authorization and Step Therapy (And How Do 2027 CMS Rules Speed Them Up)?

Getting a pharmacy rejection or surgical delay right before treatment is one of the most frustrating parts of modern health insurance. Understanding how prior authorization, step therapy exceptions, and CMS's faster turnaround deadlines work puts you back in control.

Direct answer: Prior authorization requires your doctor to get insurer approval before a procedure, scan, or specialty drug is covered; step therapy requires trying a lower-cost medication first. Under CMS's Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (operational 2026-2027), Medicare Advantage and federally facilitated Marketplace insurers must issue urgent decisions within 72 hours, standard decisions within 7 calendar days, and provide a specific denial reason.

How 2027 Medicare Advantage vs. Original Medicare + Medigap Differ on Prior Auth

One of the biggest practical differences between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare + Medigap is utilization management. Original Medicare requires prior authorization for only a narrow list of services (such as certain durable medical equipment, repetitive ambulance transport, and select hospital outpatient items), and Medigap automatically pays its secondary share whenever Original Medicare approves a claim.

By contrast, Medicare Advantage plans commonly apply prior authorization to inpatient hospital admissions, skilled nursing stays, MRIs/CTs, outpatient surgeries, and Part B infused drugs - though CMS rules now require MA plans to base medical necessity on traditional Medicare criteria, honor existing courses of treatment for at least a 90-day transition when switching plans, and meet the strict 72-hour / 7-day decision deadlines.

4 Steps When Your Prescription or Procedure Is Held Up

  1. Ask for the Exact Denial Code & Written Reason: Under CMS rules, insurers must state the specific clinical reason for any denial so your doctor knows exactly what lab or chart note is missing.
  2. Request an Expedited (72-Hour) Review If Delay Risks Your Health: Your prescribing doctor can mark a prior authorization or formulary exception as urgent/expedited when waiting 7 days could jeopardize your health.
  3. Ask for a 30-Day Transition Fill on Medicare Part D: New Part D enrollees (or members whose drug changed tiers on January 1) are generally entitled to a one-time 30-day transition supply within the first 90 days while your doctor files an exception.
  4. Audit PA & Step Therapy Flags Before Picking Your 2027 Plan: Every formulary lists PA, ST, and QL icons next to each drug - checking these before enrolling prevents January pharmacy surprises.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can Medicare Advantage or ACA insurers take on prior authorization in 2027?

Under CMS's Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule, impacted payers (including Medicare Advantage and HealthCare.gov QHP issuers) must respond to urgent prior authorization requests within 72 hours and standard requests within 7 calendar days.

What is a step therapy exception?

A step therapy exception is a written request from your prescribing doctor asking your plan to skip a required lower-cost medication because you already tried and failed it, have a documented allergy, or it is medically contraindicated.

Does Original Medicare with Medigap require prior authorization for most surgeries?

Original Medicare requires prior authorization for far fewer services than Medicare Advantage, and when Original Medicare approves a medically necessary service, Medigap pays its standardized secondary share without separate carrier authorization.

Do non-formulary drugs count toward the 2027 $2,400 Medicare Part D cap?

No - only covered Part D formulary drugs (or drugs approved through an official formulary exception) count toward the $2,400 annual out-of-pocket cap in 2027.
