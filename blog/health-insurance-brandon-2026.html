What Changed for Brandon Residents in 2026

The 2026 Marketplace uses the current premium tax credit rules and applicable-percentage table. The amount a household pays after advance credits depends on the Marketplace determination and the selected plan; it should not be inferred from a statewide percentage.

Florida OIR publishes 2026 individual-market premium tables, carrier availability by county, and sample scenarios. Those tables are market context, not a quote. Compare the exact Brandon plans returned for the household, including the gross premium, applied tax credit, network, prescriptions, deductible, and maximum out-of-pocket exposure.

IRS guidance also states that for tax years after 2025 there is no repayment cap for excess advance premium tax credit. Report income and household changes to the Marketplace and review the final Form 1095-A before filing Form 8962.

Moving From Medicaid or CHIP to Marketplace Coverage

Florida DCF determines Medicaid eligibility. If DCF sends a termination or ineligibility notice, keep it and confirm the exact coverage-end date before changing coverage.

Current HealthCare.gov instructions generally allow 90 days after Medicaid or CHIP coverage ends to select a Marketplace plan. The application result and eligibility notice determine the case-specific deadline and any required documents.

Special Enrollment Periods Available Now

You may qualify to enroll in or change a Marketplace plan after events such as:

  • Lost employer-sponsored health insurance
  • Got married or divorced
  • Had a baby or adopted a child
  • Moved to a new ZIP code or county and met the applicable prior-coverage rule
  • Lost Medicaid or CHIP coverage
  • A dependent turned 26 and lost coverage
  • COBRA coverage ended

Do not assume every event uses the same window or allows every household member to change plan categories. Apply through the Marketplace and follow the eligibility notice and document instructions.

HSA-Eligible Bronze Plans: A New Option for Brandon Families

HealthCare.gov and the IRS state that for 2026, Bronze and Catastrophic individual-market plans are treated as HSA-compatible. A person must still meet the other HSA eligibility rules, including restrictions involving other disqualifying coverage.

HSA compatibility is not a recommendation to choose Bronze. Compare the premium, providers, prescriptions, deductible, services covered before the deductible, and maximum out-of-pocket exposure. If cost-sharing reductions apply, they are available only through an eligible Silver plan.
