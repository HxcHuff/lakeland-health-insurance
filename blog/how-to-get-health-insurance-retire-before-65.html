How Do I Get Health Insurance If I Retire Before 65?

Medicare generally starts at 65 for people who qualify based on age. If you retire earlier, you need another source of major medical coverage until then. The usual comparison is COBRA or retiree coverage, a Marketplace plan, a spouse's employer plan, and, in limited cases, other products that are not ACA-compliant.

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026.

By David Huff, licensed Florida health insurance agent #W371813 · Updated September 30, 2026

Direct answer: Retiring before 65 does not start Medicare. You need coverage from your retirement date until the month Medicare begins. The usual options are:

  • COBRA, generally up to 18 months,
  • an employer retiree plan, if one is offered,
  • a spouse's job-based plan, or
  • a Marketplace plan, if losing your job coverage qualifies you for a Special Enrollment Period (subject to eligibility).

Marketplace savings depend on your retirement income for the year, not on the fact that you retired. For 2027 coverage, household income above 400% of the poverty line ($63,840 for one person, $86,560 for a couple) gets no premium tax credit. How you draw retirement income can decide whether you qualify.

How retirement income affects Marketplace savings

The Marketplace uses modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) for the coverage year.

Income sourceCounts toward MAGI?
Pension paymentsYes
Traditional IRA and 401(k) withdrawalsYes (the taxable amount)
Roth IRA conversionsYes (the converted amount is taxable)
Qualified Roth IRA withdrawalsNo
Social Security benefitsYes, including the non-taxable part
Capital gains and dividends from a taxable brokerage accountYes
Cash from savings already taxedNo
Tax-exempt interestYes

Because credits stop at 400% of the poverty line, one large withdrawal or Roth conversion can cost a full year's credit. Drawing more from savings and qualified Roth money, and less from traditional accounts, can keep MAGI inside the range. At the low end, you need at least 100% of the poverty line ($15,960 for one person, $21,640 for a couple) to get a credit. Have a tax professional plan the withdrawals. An agent can show you the coverage effect of each income level.

For 2027, the expected contribution toward the benchmark Silver plan ranges from 2.15% to 10.22% of income. The ACA lets insurers charge older adults up to three times what they charge younger adults, so at the same income a 62-year-old usually gets a larger credit than a 30-year-old. Check actual quotes on HealthCare.gov once 2027 prices post on November 1.

Timing the switch without a gap

  • Get the coverage-end date in writing. Employer coverage often ends on the last day of the month.
  • Pick a Marketplace plan up to 60 days before that date, and the new plan can start the next day, on the first of the month.
  • If you elect COBRA, mark the date it ends. When it runs out, you may qualify for a new Special Enrollment Period, subject to eligibility.

Plan the Medicare handoff at 65

  • Your Initial Enrollment Period is 7 months: 3 months before your 65th-birthday month, that month, and 3 months after. Sign up early in that window so Medicare starts the month you turn 65.
  • Marketplace credits end for any month you are eligible for premium-free Part A. Drop the Marketplace plan when Medicare starts.
  • COBRA and retiree coverage do not let you delay Part B without a penalty. Only coverage from current employment does. Medicare.gov says not to wait until COBRA ends to sign up.
  • A younger spouse can keep a Marketplace plan after you move to Medicare. Their credit is then based on the household's income.

Common questions

Does retiring at 62 start Medicare?

No. Age-based Medicare begins at 65 for people who qualify. If you retire earlier, you need COBRA, a retiree plan, a spouse's plan or a Marketplace plan until Medicare starts.

Does retirement income count for Marketplace subsidies?

Yes. Pensions, taxable IRA and 401(k) withdrawals, Roth conversions, capital gains and all Social Security benefits count toward MAGI. Qualified Roth IRA withdrawals and spending from already-taxed savings do not.

Is there an income limit for early retirees on the Marketplace?

Yes. For 2027 coverage, premium tax credits stop above 400% of the federal poverty line, which is $63,840 for one person and $86,560 for a couple.

Can I keep COBRA and delay Medicare Part B at 65?

No. COBRA is not coverage based on current employment, so it does not let you delay Part B without risking a late-enrollment penalty. Medicare.gov says not to wait until COBRA ends to sign up.
