Does Medicare cover dental, vision, and hearing?

Original Medicare is medical and hospital insurance, not a routine dental, vision, or hearing plan. Some services tied to a covered medical treatment can be covered. For routine cleanings, glasses, or hearing aids, people often look at Advantage extras, separate policies, or paying out of pocket.

By David Huff, licensed Florida health agent #W371813 | NPN 18213932

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026.

Direct answer: Original Medicare generally does not cover routine dental, vision, or hearing care. Medicare.gov states that in most cases you pay all costs for services such as cleanings, fillings, dentures, routine eye exams, glasses, and hearing aids. Some Medicare Advantage plans may offer extra dental, vision, or hearing benefits. Those extras vary by plan. Medigap does not add routine dental, vision, or hearing the way a stand-alone policy might.

What does Original Medicare say about dental?

Exceptions are tied to covered medical treatment, such as certain jaw surgery or an eye exam related to a disease.

Medicare.gov dental coverage says that in most cases Medicare does not cover routine cleanings, fillings, extractions, dentures, or implants. It may cover certain dental services connected to a covered inpatient stay or to specific medical treatments, such as exams related to organ transplants or some cancer treatments. Those exceptions are medical, not a routine dental plan.

What about vision and hearing?

Medicare.gov eye exams and hearing aids pages describe limited medical situations, such as some exams related to specific diseases, and state that routine vision care and hearing aids are generally not covered. Do not treat a cataract-related benefit as a glasses plan. Confirm any service with Medicare.gov and the provider before you assume coverage.

Can Medicare Advantage fill these gaps?

Some Medicare Advantage plans may include extra dental, vision, or hearing benefits. Coverage varies by plan. Networks, waiting periods, annual maximums, and which dentists or audiologists participate are plan-specific. Original Medicare plus Medigap does not automatically include those extras. Compare the extra-benefit brochure for the exact plan ID, not a company name. See Advantage versus Supplement and dental and vision information.

Are stand-alone dental policies the same as Medicare?

No. A separate dental or hearing policy is not Part A or Part B. It has its own underwriting, waiting periods, and maximums. See the dental insurance guide for how those contracts usually work. They do not replace major medical Medicare coverage.

Common questions

Does Original Medicare pay for dentures or hearing aids?

Generally no. Medicare.gov says that in most cases you pay all costs for those items. Limited medical exceptions can apply and should be confirmed on Medicare.gov.

If an Advantage plan advertises dental, is every dentist covered?

No. Extra benefits and dental networks vary by plan. Confirm the dentist, waiting periods, and annual maximums in the plan documents.

What to do next

List the dental, vision, and hearing services you actually use. Then check Original Medicare rules on Medicare.gov and, if you are comparing Advantage, the extra-benefit section of that plan.
