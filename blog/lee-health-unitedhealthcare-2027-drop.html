Lee Health Dropping UnitedHealthcare in 2027: Patient Guide

What the impending January 1, 2027 contract termination means for approximately 50,000 Southwest Florida patients, hospital access across Lee County, and 2027 Open Enrollment options.

Text David for Lee Health Plan Check Call (863) 640-3102

The Southwest Florida Contract Dispute Explained

Lee Health, the dominant public healthcare system serving Lee County and surrounding Southwest Florida communities, has issued formal notice terminating its network agreements with UnitedHealthcare effective January 1, 2027. This impasse affects approximately 50,000 patients enrolled in UnitedHealthcare commercial and Medicare Advantage products.

Lee Health leadership cited persistent challenges regarding claims adjudication, extensive pre-authorization reviews, and care delivery administrative burdens as core reasons for the contract termination. For residents in Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Estero, Bonita Springs, and Lehigh Acres, this development coincides directly with Medicare Annual Enrollment and ACA Marketplace Open Enrollment.

Lee Health Facilities and Services Impacted

The contract termination encompasses major acute-care hospital facilities, specialized campuses, and hundreds of outpatient clinical providers across Southwest Florida:

  • Lee Memorial Hospital (Fort Myers)
  • HealthPark Medical Center (South Fort Myers)
  • Gulf Coast Medical Center (Fort Myers)
  • Cape Coral Hospital (Cape Coral)
  • Golisano Children's Hospital of Southwest Florida
  • Lee Physician Group outpatient practices, diagnostic laboratories, and imaging centers.

Emergency Care vs. Non-Emergency Medical Services

Patients with UnitedHealthcare coverage should recognize the distinction between emergency and elective care rules:

Care Type 2027 Network Status Patient Financial Protection
Emergency Room Services Protected by Law Covered at in-network cost-sharing under the federal No Surprises Act at all Lee Health hospital emergency departments.
Elective Inpatient Surgeries Out-of-Network Subject to out-of-network coinsurance and deductibles, or non-covered under HMO plan rules.
Lee Physician Group Clinics Out-of-Network Routine office consultations, physicals, and chronic disease visits will no longer be processed at in-network rates.

Open Enrollment Action Steps for Impacted Patients

  1. Audit Current Policies: Check whether your insurance card displays UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage, commercial employer coverage, or an individual exchange product.
  2. Review Alternative Carriers: Evaluate carriers that maintain long-term in-network agreements with Lee Health, including Florida Blue and other contracted regional plans.
  3. Continuity of Care Application: Patients undergoing active treatment (such as chemotherapy, high-risk pregnancy, or scheduled major surgery) may qualify for a temporary 90-day continuity of care transition period at in-network rates.
  4. Switch Plans Before December 7 / December 15: Make plan changes during the fall open enrollment windows to ensure continuous in-network care starting January 1, 2027.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Lee Health's contract with UnitedHealthcare end?

Lee Health announced that its contracts with UnitedHealthcare for Medicare Advantage and commercial plans will terminate effective January 1, 2027, unless a new agreement is reached before that date.

Can I still receive emergency care at Lee Health hospitals under UnitedHealthcare in 2027?

Yes. Under federal law, emergency room evaluation and stabilization at any hospital facility, including Lee Memorial, Gulf Coast Medical, and Cape Coral Hospital, must be covered at in-network cost-sharing rates regardless of network contract status.

What can patients do during Open Enrollment to maintain in-network access to Lee Health?

Impacted policyholders can review alternative insurance carriers that maintain active contracts with Lee Health, such as Florida Blue or other participating Medicare Advantage and commercial exchange plans, during fall Open Enrollment.
