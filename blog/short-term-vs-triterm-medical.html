Short-Term Medical Rules in Florida

Short-term medical insurance is temporary, underwritten, non-ACA coverage. The published federal definition, current enforcement posture, Florida law, and the actual policy offered must all be reviewed before application.

Why the Current Rule Check Matters

The 2024 federal final rule limits a new short-term policy to no more than three months initially and four months total. On August 7, 2025, the federal Departments said they would not prioritize enforcement of that definition while considering future rulemaking. The statement did not itself amend or repeal the rule.

Florida law separately defines short-term coverage as less than twelve months initially and no more than thirty-six months including renewals or extensions, and it requires written purchaser disclosures. Because these authorities differ, an applicant must verify the exact policy offered rather than infer availability or duration from a product name.

Practical rule: check ACA, employer, continuation, and public coverage first. If a temporary policy is still being considered, verify underwriting, exclusions, policy duration, benefit limits, and replacement coverage before applying.

How the Three Lanes Compare

ACA Marketplace

Comprehensive individual-market coverage with ACA consumer protections, subject to enrollment eligibility and plan terms.

Short-Term Medical

Temporary non-ACA coverage that may use underwriting, exclude conditions or services, and impose benefit limits.

Historical product labels

Names such as TriTerm do not prove current Florida availability, duration, policy form, network, or benefits. Current carrier documentation is required.

When Short-Term Medical May Be Reviewed

A temporary policy may be worth evaluating when the coverage gap is brief, the replacement date is known, comprehensive options have been checked, and the applicant can absorb uncovered charges.

  • Potential use: a defined transition between comprehensive plans.
  • Weakness: underwriting, exclusions, and narrower benefits can matter quickly.
  • Risk: pre-existing conditions and ongoing prescriptions may not be handled the way an ACA shopper expects.

How to Handle TriTerm References

VERIFICATION REQUIRED: treat TriTerm and other historical product labels as unconfirmed until the current carrier quote, Florida-approved policy form, requested effective date, contract term, underwriting rules, network, and benefits are available for review.

An older brochure can explain what a prior product described; it cannot establish what an applicant can buy now.

Supplemental Coverage Is a Separate Decision

Fixed-indemnity coverage is not major medical insurance and does not correct every limitation in a short-term policy. Review any supplemental product on its own policy terms and avoid treating combined products as equivalent to comprehensive coverage.

Questions to Answer Before Applying

  • Do you qualify for an ACA subsidy, and how much would the net premium actually be?
  • Do you have any pre-existing conditions, pending symptoms, upcoming care, or recurring prescriptions?
  • How long do you need coverage, and what is your next enrollment window?
  • What happens if you develop a condition during the policy period?
  • Are you comfortable with the deductible, exclusions, and benefit maximums?
  • Have any supplemental products been evaluated separately instead of treated as replacement major medical coverage?

Bottom Line

Short-term medical insurance is a temporary, underwritten option—not a default replacement for comprehensive coverage. Start with comprehensive alternatives, then verify the exact temporary policy and the date the next coverage begins.

FAQ

Is Short-Term Medical real insurance?

It is insurance, but it is not ACA Marketplace coverage. That distinction matters because ACA consumer protections and short-term policy rules are not the same.

Is a TriTerm-labeled product currently available?

VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Confirm the current quote, Florida-approved policy form, effective date, term, and underwriting requirements.

Should I choose short-term coverage just because the premium is lower?

No. Lower premium only matters after you understand underwriting, exclusions, benefit limits, prescriptions, and the risk of a denied or limited claim.
