1. Confirm your enrollment window

Your first Medicare decision is identifying the correct Initial Enrollment Period or Special Enrollment Period. The Initial Enrollment Period is the 7-month window around your 65th birthday: 3 months before your birthday month, your birthday month, and 3 months after. Coverage generally starts the first of the month after you enroll during that window, subject to Medicare rules. If you are turning 65 and do not have other creditable coverage, missing the applicable window can create delayed coverage or late-enrollment penalties. In Lakeland and Polk County, start comparing doctors, prescriptions, and pharmacies before your birthday month so the review is not rushed.

Medicare Annual Enrollment (Oct 15–Dec 7, 2026) is for people who already have Medicare and want to change Medicare Advantage or Part D for January 1, 2027. It is not the same window as turning 65. If that is your situation, use the Polk County AEP checklist instead.

  • Part A: Hospital coverage. Many people qualify for premium-free Part A.

  • Part D: Prescription coverage. Skipping it without creditable drug coverage can create a penalty. See Medicare Part D plans in Lakeland.

2. Decide which Medicare path you want to compare

Most people compare two broad paths:

Medicare Advantage

A private plan that replaces Original Medicare for how you receive benefits. It often includes Part D and extras like dental, vision, or hearing, but uses networks and copays.

Original Medicare + Supplement + Part D

Original Medicare with a Medicare Supplement can offer broader provider flexibility and more predictable medical costs, but usually has higher monthly premiums and requires a separate Part D plan.

3. Check your doctors before you fall in love with a plan

Do not choose a plan based only on premium or extra benefits. Check your primary doctor, specialists, hospital preference, and referral patterns before you enroll.

4. Run your prescriptions through the plan

Prescription costs can vary by plan, tier, quantity limit, prior authorization rule, and pharmacy. Have your medication names, dosage, and pharmacy ready before you compare. If limited income may lower drug costs, also review Extra Help and Florida Medicare Savings Programs.

5. Look at total yearly risk

A Medicare Advantage plan with a low advertised premium can still have copays and a maximum out-of-pocket limit. A Medicare Supplement can cost more monthly but reduce surprises on medical bills. The right answer depends on your care pattern and budget.

When the numbers look close, request a side-by-side review of premium, drug costs, and worst-case out-of-pocket before you enroll.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Medicare Initial Enrollment Period when I turn 65?

The Initial Enrollment Period generally lasts seven months: the three months before the month you turn 65, your birthday month, and the three months after.

What information should I gather before comparing Medicare plans?

Prepare your doctors, specialists, prescriptions, dosages, preferred pharmacy, current coverage, travel needs, and a realistic healthcare budget.

Should I compare Medicare Advantage with Medicare Supplement?

Yes. Compare provider access, referrals, prescription coverage, premiums, copays, maximum out-of-pocket exposure, Medigap underwriting requirements, and travel needs before choosing a path.
