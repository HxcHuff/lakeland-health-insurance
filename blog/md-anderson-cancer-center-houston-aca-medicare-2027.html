Why MD Anderson Cancer Center Network Access Requires Careful Verification in 2027

The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center in the Texas Medical Center (along with Houston-area outpatient locations in West Houston, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, and League City) is an NCI-designated comprehensive cancer center that draws patients from Harris County and every region of Texas.

Because virtually all individual ACA Marketplace plans sold on HealthCare.gov in Texas use narrow HMO or EPO networks rather than broad employer PPO networks, specialty oncology centers like MD Anderson are excluded from many individual exchange tiers. Even when a carrier's employer PPO plans include MD Anderson, the same carrier's individual Marketplace HMO sub-network—such as certain Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Texas Blue Advantage HMO tiers or narrow regional HMOs—may have different contract rules or require single-case gap exceptions.

Texas Continuity of Care & Federal No Surprises Act Protections

Oncology patients navigating network changes in Houston and across Texas should know two statutory safeguards:

  • Texas Continuity of Care (Texas Insurance Code Chapters 843 & 1301) & Federal No Surprises Act Section 113: If you are in active cancer treatment (such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, or post-surgical oncology care) and your physician or hospital leaves your plan's network during the policy year, Texas law and federal continuity-of-care rules allow eligible patients to request transitional in-network coverage (up to 90 days under federal rules, or up to 90 days/special circumstance periods under Texas HMO/EPO rules) so treatment is not abruptly interrupted.
  • Federal No Surprises Act Emergency Room Protection: Under the federal No Surprises Act, emergency medical evaluation and stabilization at any hospital emergency department in the Texas Medical Center are covered at in-network cost-sharing rates without out-of-network balance billing. Scheduled oncology consultations, diagnostic workups, and elective surgeries are non-emergency services and require active in-network status or an approved out-of-network referral/exception.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does UT MD Anderson Cancer Center accept Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas Blue Advantage HMO in 2027?

Individual ACA Marketplace HMO plans in Texas, including many Blue Advantage HMO and narrow-network exchange products, have historically restricted or varied in participation at UT MD Anderson Cancer Center; patients must verify the exact 2027 carrier network ID and facility contract with MD Anderson Patient Access before enrolling.

Which 2027 Medicare option provides the broadest access to MD Anderson Cancer Center in Houston?

Original Medicare (Parts A and B) paired with a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy, such as Plan G or Plan N, is accepted at UT MD Anderson Cancer Center without Medicare Advantage HMO network exclusions or carrier prior authorization delays for Medicare-covered oncology services.

What protections apply if my Texas health plan drops MD Anderson during active cancer treatment?

Under Texas Insurance Code Chapter 843 and Chapter 1301 continuity-of-care rules and federal No Surprises Act Section 113 protections, patients undergoing active oncology treatment when a provider contract terminates can apply for up to 90 days of transitional care at in-network cost-sharing rates.
