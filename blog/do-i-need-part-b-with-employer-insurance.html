Do I need Part B if I have insurance through my employer?

Employer insurance at 65 does not always replace Part B. Some group plans pay as if Medicare were in place. Others allow a delay while you or a spouse still work. The safe step is to ask the employer how the plan coordinates with Medicare, in writing, before you delay Part B.

By David Huff, licensed Florida health agent #W371813 | NPN 18213932

Last reviewed: September 29, 2026.

Direct answer: You may be able to delay Part B if you have group health insurance based on current employment, as Medicare.gov describes. That is not automatic. Ask the employer whether you must enroll in Part B for the group plan to pay correctly. COBRA, retiree coverage, and Marketplace plans are not the current-employment test. Delaying without a qualifying Special Enrollment Period can create a late enrollment penalty.

What question should I ask human resources?

Ask whether the plan is a group health plan based on current employment, whether it is a large-group plan if you have a disability, and whether claims will be denied or reduced if you are Medicare-eligible but not enrolled in Part B. Medicare.gov's working past 65 page tells you to ask the employer that provides the insurance. Get the answer in writing.

When is the answer usually to enroll in Part B now?

Retiree coverage, COBRA, Marketplace coverage, and many small or individual policies are not reasons to delay. Medicare.gov says if you have insurance that is not from a job, rules vary and some private coverage pays less, or does not pay, once you are eligible for Medicare. If you are already retired and on COBRA, sign up for Medicare when you first qualify rather than waiting for COBRA to end.

How does this interact with Part D and Medigap?

Creditable employer drug coverage can delay Part D without a penalty if you do not have a 63-day gap. Keep the annual creditable-coverage notice. Medigap federal Open Enrollment is tied to Part B enrollment at 65 or older, and it is not part of Medicare Annual Enrollment. If you delay Part B for years, you also delay that Medigap window. See Advantage versus Supplement.

What happens to dependents if I enroll in Medicare?

Your Medicare decision can change who remains on the employer plan. Some employers drop the employee from the group plan once Medicare is primary, which can affect a younger spouse. Ask whether dependents can stay and what they pay. That answer belongs in the same written request as the Part B question.

If a spouse will need Marketplace coverage after leaving the group plan, that spouse may have a Special Enrollment Period. See coverage outside Open Enrollment. Do not leave that person uninsured while you complete Medicare paperwork.

Lakeland and Polk County employers vary. There is no single local rule. Confirm with the administrator, then with Social Security.

How do I ask the employer the right question?

Ask, in writing: Is this a group health plan based on current employment? Does the plan require Medicare Part A or Part B at 65? How does claims payment change if I enroll in Medicare? Can dependents remain if I enroll or if I delay? Request the creditable coverage notice for drugs.

Take that letter to Social Security if you delay Part B. Verbal hallway advice is hard to use later. See Medicare.gov working past 65.

If the employer is unsure, that is a reason not to delay. You can enroll during the Initial Enrollment Period and revisit Advantage or Medigap later. Those later choices still follow their own windows.
