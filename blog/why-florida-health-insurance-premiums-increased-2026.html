Why Florida ACA Premiums Rose 34.1% on Average in 2026

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026. Historical 2026 details below are left unchanged.

Published March 4, 2026 • Lakeland Health Insurance

If you’re shopping for health insurance in Florida this year and noticed higher premiums, you’re not imagining things. Many Floridians are seeing noticeable price increases for 2026 coverage.

Several major factors are driving these changes.

Florida regulators approved a 34.1% enrollment-weighted average 2026 increase in individual-market rates before subsidies, from a $648 to an $867 weighted-average monthly premium per person (Florida OIR, Oct. 16, 2025); your own change may be higher or lower. A Lakeland ZIP can differ from the statewide average, so compare the plan ID, subsidy estimate, and total annual cost—not only the headline percentage.

If you missed Open Enrollment, a qualifying life event may qualify you for a Special Enrollment Period, subject to eligibility. Confirm the event and dates before you assume you can enroll mid-year. For ongoing Lakeland Marketplace help, see ACA health insurance in Lakeland.

The Expiration of Enhanced ACA Subsidies

The biggest reason premiums feel higher in 2026 is the expiration of the enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies that were introduced during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Those expanded subsidies helped millions of Americans reduce their monthly health insurance premiums. With those temporary financial protections ending, many consumers are now responsible for a larger share of their plan costs.

After 2025 there is also no cap on repaying excess advance premium tax credits at tax time, so report income changes promptly.

For households that previously paid very little each month, the difference can be significant.

Healthcare Costs Continue to Rise

Insurance premiums are also increasing because healthcare costs themselves are rising.

Hospitals, pharmaceutical companies, and medical providers continue to raise prices for services, procedures, and prescription drugs. Insurance companies must adjust premiums each year to account for these higher costs.

Florida is also experiencing rapid population growth, which places additional demand on healthcare providers throughout the state.

More People Are Enrolling in Marketplace Plans

Florida has become one of the largest ACA Marketplace states in the country. Millions of residents rely on Marketplace coverage instead of employer-provided health insurance.

As enrollment grows, insurance carriers must carefully manage risk across their member populations. When more people with high medical costs enroll in plans, premiums can increase for the entire pool.

The Good News: Many People Still Qualify for Subsidies

Even though enhanced subsidies have ended, many Florida residents still qualify for financial assistance through the Marketplace.

Depending on household income and family size, tax credits may significantly reduce monthly premiums. Depending on income and household size, some people may still qualify for low-cost plans after tax credits.

The key is understanding your eligibility and choosing the right plan.

What to Review Before You Switch Plans

A premium increase does not automatically mean your current plan is bad. It means the plan needs to be rechecked against the rest of the math. A cheaper plan can cost more over the year if it drops your doctor, changes your prescriptions, or increases your deductible exposure.

Before switching, review:

  • Your household income estimate and tax household size.
  • Primary care, specialists, hospitals, and urgent care locations you actually use.
  • Prescription formularies, tiers, prior authorizations, and pharmacy rules.
  • Deductible, maximum out-of-pocket, copays, coinsurance, and referral requirements.

Why the Lowest Premium Can Backfire

Low-premium plans can be useful, but only when the network and cost-sharing fit the household. If you need ongoing care, the difference between a slightly higher premium and a worse deductible or specialist setup can matter more than the headline monthly price.

The better comparison is total annual exposure: premium plus expected care plus the risk if something goes wrong. That is the number worth reviewing before you commit.

The Bottom Line

While health insurance premiums have increased in 2026, many Floridians still have access to affordable coverage options through the Marketplace.

Shopping carefully and reviewing plan options each year can make a major difference in what you pay.

Working with a licensed insurance professional can also help you check which subsidies and benefits you may qualify for.
