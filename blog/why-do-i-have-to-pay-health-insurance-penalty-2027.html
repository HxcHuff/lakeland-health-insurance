Why Do I Have to Pay a Health Insurance Penalty?

Most people who ask why they owe a health insurance penalty are actually seeing either a lifetime Medicare late-enrollment surcharge on their Social Security statement or an unexpected IRS Form 8962 tax balance after receiving Marketplace subsidies. Here is how all four work in 2027 and how to stop or prevent them.

Direct answer: There is no federal tax penalty for being uninsured under the ACA (it has been $0 since 2019 in Florida and 45 other states). If you are being charged a health insurance penalty in 2027, it almost always comes from one of four specific rules: (1) the Medicare Part B late penalty (10% per 12-month delay), (2) the Medicare Part D late penalty (1% per month of the $41.33 base premium), (3) a state individual mandate (CA, MA, NJ, RI, or DC), or (4) an IRS Form 8962 ACA subsidy repayment because your actual income exceeded your estimate.

Why Many People Mistake IRS Form 8962 Repayment for a Penalty

If your tax preparer told you that you owe money back because of HealthCare.gov, that charge is not an uninsured fine: it is reconciliation of Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC) on IRS Form 8962. Starting with 2026 tax returns (filed in early 2027) and continuing for 2027 coverage, federal law eliminated the old sliding-scale repayment caps, meaning households that underestimate their annual MAGI must repay 100% of excess advance subsidies, and crossing the 400% FPL cliff triggers full repayment of the year's credit.

On the Medicare side, the most common surprise comes from delaying Part B while covered by COBRA, retiree insurance, VA care, or an employer with fewer than 20 employees. None of those count as creditable Part B coverage under CMS rules, which is why submitting Form CMS-L564 from an active 20+ employee group plan is essential.

Can You Appeal or Remove an Existing Penalty?

  1. Medicare Part B Equitable Relief or Employer Proof: If Social Security missed your employer coverage dates, file Form CMS-L564 signed by HR along with a Reconsideration request (Form SSA-561) within 60 days of the notice.
  2. Medicare Part D Reconsideration: If you actually had creditable drug coverage (such as VA, TRICARE, or an employer drug plan), submit the Part D LEP Reconsideration Request form with your creditable coverage letter.
  3. Extra Help (LIS) Automatic Waiver: Beneficiaries who qualify for Medicare Extra Help have their Part D late enrollment penalty permanently waived while eligible.
  4. Preventing 2027 ACA Repayment Surprises: Update HealthCare.gov within 30 days whenever income rises, or choose to take a smaller monthly APTC slice up front and claim any remaining credit at tax time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there still a federal tax penalty for not having health insurance in 2027?

No. The federal ACA individual shared responsibility payment remains $0 in 2027 across Florida and 45 states. Only California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island, and Washington, D.C. enforce state-level uninsured tax penalties.

Why am I paying a Medicare Part B penalty when I had COBRA?

Medicare does not count COBRA or retiree insurance as creditable coverage for delaying Part B. Only group health coverage tied to current active employment (yours or your spouse's) at a 20+ employee company lets you delay Part B without penalty.

How much is the 2027 Medicare Part D penalty for going 12 months uncovered?

For 2027, 1% of the $41.33 national base beneficiary premium multiplied by 12 months equals $4.96, rounded to the nearest ten cents: $5.00 per month added to your Part D premium.

Are ACA subsidy repayment caps still in effect for 2026 and 2027 taxes?

No. Federal legislation eliminated the historical statutory repayment caps starting with the 2026 tax year (filed in 2027), so any excess advance premium tax credit must be repaid in full on Form 8962.
