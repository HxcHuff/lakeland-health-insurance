Medicare Part D $2,400 Cap in 2027: How it Works in Florida

Published November 2, 2026 · By David Huff, Licensed Florida Agent #W371813

Key Takeaway for Central Florida Beneficiaries:

The $2,400 out-of-pocket prescription cap under the Inflation Reduction Act permanently replaces the Medicare coverage gap ("donut hole"). Once your total spending on covered formulary drugs reaches $2,400 in plan year 2027, your cost-sharing drops to $0 for the rest of the year. However, formularies, tiers, and pharmacy networks are changing significantly—so reviewing your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) before December 7 is critical.

1. What Changed Under the Inflation Reduction Act?

For years, Medicare beneficiaries taking brand-name or specialty medications faced the dreaded "donut hole"—a coverage gap where out-of-pocket costs spiked until reaching the catastrophic threshold. In 2024 and 2025, federal reforms eliminated coinsurance in the catastrophic phase. Now, for plan year 2027, the cap is fixed at an absolute maximum of $2,400 out-of-pocket for covered medications.

This protection applies equally whether you receive prescription coverage through a stand-alone Part D plan paired with Medigap or an integrated Medicare Advantage (MAPD) plan from carriers like Humana, UnitedHealthcare, or BayCarePlus.

2. How the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (M3P) Works

While the $2,400 cap provides financial certainty, beneficiaries taking high-cost medications often reached their limit within the first two or three months of the year, creating front-loaded cash flow strain. To address this, CMS introduced the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (M3P).

  • Voluntary Enrollment: You can opt in through your Part D or Medicare Advantage carrier at any time.
  • $0 at the Pharmacy Counter: Participating pharmacies charge $0 at pickup for covered drugs.
  • Monthly Installments: The plan bills you in calculated monthly installments throughout the remaining months of the year.
  • No Interest or Fees: There is no interest or financing charge for utilizing the program.

3. Watchouts: Formulary Shifts, Tiers, and Preferred Pharmacies

Because insurance carriers bear a greater share of high-cost catastrophic drug claims under the new law, many carriers have adjusted their formularies to manage expenses. Watch for the following when checking your 2027 plan documents:

  • Tier Placement: A drug that was Tier 2 (low generic copay) last year might be reclassified to Tier 3 (preferred brand) or require coinsurance.
  • Prior Authorization & Step Therapy: Check whether newly added utilization management rules apply to your medications.
  • Preferred vs. Standard Pharmacy Status: Filling at a standard retail counter vs. a plan-preferred pharmacy (such as Publix, CVS, or Walgreens depending on carrier) can mean double or triple the copay.

4. Your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) Checklist

Every September, existing Medicare enrollees receive an ANOC from their insurance carrier detailing adjustments for the upcoming calendar year. Before the December 7 AEP deadline, complete these three steps:

  1. Check Section 1 (Changes to Benefits and Costs): Look specifically at your Part D deductible and tier copays.
  2. Run Your Drug List on Medicare Plan Finder: Compare all available plans in your ZIP code using your exact dosages and preferred pharmacy.
  3. Confirm Doctor Alignment: If switching Medicare Advantage plans to optimize drug costs, verify that your physicians at Watson Clinic, Lakeland Regional Health, or BayCare remain in-network using our Provider Check Tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the $2,400 Part D cap include my monthly plan premium?

No. The $2,400 maximum out-of-pocket cap applies strictly to covered prescription copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles at the pharmacy. Monthly plan premiums for Part D or Medicare Advantage MAPD plans are paid separately and do not count toward the $2,400 threshold.

What is the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (M3P)?

The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan is a voluntary program created by the Inflation Reduction Act allowing Medicare Part D enrollees to spread their out-of-pocket pharmacy costs across monthly installments throughout the calendar year instead of paying large sums at the pharmacy counter in January or February.

Does the $2,400 cap apply to both Medicare Advantage (MAPD) and stand-alone Part D plans?

Yes. The $2,400 annual out-of-pocket limit applies to all CMS-approved Part D prescription drug coverage, including Medicare Advantage plans that include prescription benefits (MAPD) and stand-alone Prescription Drug Plans (PDPs) that pair with Original Medicare or Medigap.
