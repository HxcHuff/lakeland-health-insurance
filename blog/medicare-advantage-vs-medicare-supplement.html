1. Doctor and Hospital Access: Watson Clinic, LRH, and BayCare

The single biggest everyday difference between an Advantage plan and a Supplement in Lakeland is provider network flexibility. The federal source for plan structure is Medicare.gov's coverage comparison guide.

With a Medicare Supplement: There are no commercial HMO or PPO plan networks. Original Medicare is your primary payer. If a physician or hospital accepts Original Medicare—which participating physicians at Watson Clinic, Lakeland Regional Health, Bond Clinic, and BayCare Winter Haven generally do—they accept your Medigap policy. You do not need network referrals to see a specialist, and Original Medicare requires prior authorization only for a limited set of specific services and equipment.

With a Medicare Advantage Plan: You use the carrier’s contracted network (HMO or PPO):

  • Some local HMO networks contract with specific provider groups while excluding others, or require higher out-of-pocket costs if you see out-of-network doctors on a PPO.
  • If your primary care doctor is at Watson Clinic, but a preferred cardiologist is affiliated with Lakeland Regional Health, an Advantage plan may not include both in the same network tier.
  • If you seek specialized care at Moffitt Cancer Center in Tampa or UF Health Shands in Gainesville, an HMO plan generally requires network participation and prior authorization for non-emergency care. With Original Medicare and a Supplement, you can visit any facility nationwide that accepts Original Medicare assignment. 2. Upfront Premiums vs. Out-of-Pocket Risk in Polk County

Cost structure is why many Central Florida seniors compare Medicare Advantage and Medigap carefully. Many Polk County Medicare Advantage plans offer a $0 monthly plan premium (you still pay your standard Medicare Part B premium), and some offer a partial Part B premium giveback reduction.

However, a $0 plan premium does not mean $0 healthcare costs. When you use care on an Advantage plan, you pay the plan's copays and coinsurance as you go—for specialist visits, outpatient surgery, diagnostic imaging, and hospital stays—up to the plan’s annual Maximum Out-of-Pocket (MOOP) limit for covered Part A and Part B services.

With a Medicare Supplement like Plan G or Plan N, you pay a separate monthly Medigap premium (which varies by insurer, age, ZIP code, gender, tobacco status, and rating method) plus a separate standalone Part D drug plan premium. On Plan G, after you meet the annual Medicare Part B deductible, the policy pays 100% of Medicare-covered Part A and Part B coinsurance for the rest of the year. On Plan N, premiums are typically lower in exchange for copays of up to $20 for some office visits and up to $50 for emergency room visits that do not result in an inpatient admission (plus any Part B excess charges if a provider does not accept Medicare assignment). 3. The Florida Underwriting Rule (What to Know Before Your 6-Month Window Ends)

Timing is critical when choosing between Medicare Advantage and Medigap in Florida:

When you are 65 or older and enrolled in Medicare Part B, federal and Florida rules grant a one-time 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period (and federal law also provides specific 12-month "trial right" protections for certain first-time Medicare Advantage enrollees). During your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, insurance companies selling Medicare Supplements in Florida must accept your application regardless of your health history or pre-existing conditions.

However, if you remain on a Medicare Advantage plan past your initial enrollment and trial-right windows, and years later develop a health condition and want to apply for a Medicare Supplement:

  • In Florida, Medigap insurers can require medical underwriting unless you have a qualifying Guaranteed Issue right.
  • They can review your medical and prescription history on the application.
  • They can legally charge higher premiums, apply pre-existing condition waiting periods where permitted, or decline the application.

You can change Medicare Advantage plans every fall during the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 to December 7) without medical underwriting. Switching from Advantage to a Medigap policy in Florida after your initial windows close requires either passing medical underwriting or qualifying for a special Guaranteed Issue trigger—such as your Medicare Advantage plan non-renewing for 2027. See switching from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare.

Which Option Makes Sense for You?

Medicare Advantage is typically worth comparing if:

  • Your monthly budget prioritizes a low or $0 monthly plan premium beyond Part B.
  • Your primary doctors, specialists, and preferred hospitals (Watson Clinic, LRH, BayCare) are verified in the specific plan’s 2027 provider directory.
  • You want bundled Part D drug coverage and supplemental benefits like routine dental, vision, or hearing allowances (see dental, vision, and hearing coverage).
  • You receive most of your routine care locally within your plan’s service area.

A Medicare Supplement is typically worth comparing if:

  • You prefer predictable monthly premiums and minimal out-of-pocket coinsurance when receiving Medicare-covered care.
  • You travel frequently outside Florida or split time in another state.
  • You want direct access to physicians and hospitals nationwide that accept Original Medicare without HMO network referrals.
  • You are in your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, have a federal Guaranteed Issue right, or can qualify through medical underwriting.

Common questions

Can I have Medicare Advantage and Medigap at the same time?

Generally no. Medigap works with Original Medicare. If you join Advantage, you typically cannot use a Medigap policy with that Advantage plan.

Can I see doctors at Watson Clinic or Lakeland Regional Health on Medicare Advantage?

It depends on the plan. Medicare Advantage plans use contracted provider networks (HMO or PPO). Some Polk County plans contract with Watson Clinic while excluding others, or require out-of-network copays. A Medicare Supplement allows you to see any physician or hospital that takes Original Medicare nationwide without network restrictions.

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage back to a Supplement later in Florida?

In Florida, your guaranteed right to buy any Medigap plan occurs during your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period when you turn 65 and enroll in Part B. If you choose Medicare Advantage and try to switch to a Supplement years later, private insurance companies can put you through medical underwriting, charge higher premiums, or deny coverage entirely.

Which has an out-of-pocket limit, Medicare Advantage or Medigap?

Every Medicare Advantage plan has a mandatory yearly limit on in-network Part A and Part B costs set within federal CMS maximums. Original Medicare has no out-of-pocket cap on its own, while a Medigap policy covers most or all Medicare-approved cost sharing depending on the plan letter (for example, Plan G covers 100% of covered Part A and Part B coinsurance after the annual Part B deductible, while Plan N applies modest office and emergency room copays).
