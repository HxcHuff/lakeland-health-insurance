Frequently Asked Questions: Kettering Health, Miami Valley Hospital & OhioHealth (2027)

Do Dayton and Miami Valley health plans cover both Kettering Health and Miami Valley Hospital (Premier Health) in 2027?

On Original Medicare paired with a Medigap supplement (such as Plan G or Plan N), patients can visit any facility or physician accepting Medicare assignment, including both Kettering Health and Premier Health (Miami Valley Hospital). On 2027 HealthCare.gov ACA Marketplace HMO plans and certain Medicare Advantage plans in Montgomery, Greene, Warren, Miami, and Butler counties, carriers frequently split their sub-networks between Kettering Health (and Kettering Physician Network) and Premier Health (and Premier Physician Network), requiring a plan-ID and NPI directory check before enrolling.

How do I verify if Kettering Physician Network (KPN) or Premier Physician Network (PPN) is in-network for my 2027 Ohio plan?

Always check both the hospital facility (such as Kettering Health Main Campus, Soin Medical Center, Miami Valley Hospital, or Miami Valley Hospital South) and your physician's 10-digit National Provider Identifier (NPI) under the exact 2027 carrier network name on the insurer's directory.

Which 2027 carriers can Ohio residents compare for Kettering Health, Miami Valley Hospital, and OhioHealth?

Depending on your Ohio county and ZIP code, licensed Ohio agent David Huff (NPN #18213932) compares 2027 Medicare options from Anthem, Humana, Devoted Health, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, Medical Mutual, Wellcare, and Cigna, as well as under-65 ACA Marketplace plans from Anthem BCBS, Ambetter from Buckeye Health Plan, Medical Mutual, Molina, Oscar, and UnitedHealthcare.

Are OhioHealth and OSU Wexner Medical Center on the same 2027 Columbus ACA Marketplace networks?

Not always. In Franklin, Delaware, Licking, Fairfield, and Union counties (Rating Area 7), narrow HMO Marketplace plans often align with either OhioHealth (Riverside Methodist, Grant Medical Center, Dublin Methodist) or The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center and The James Cancer Hospital, making side-by-side directory verification essential.

What if my Ohio hospital or specialist leaves my plan's network mid-year?

Under the federal No Surprises Act (Section 113) and Ohio Revised Code Section 1751.13 continuity-of-care rules, patients in active treatment for serious or complex conditions, pregnancy, or scheduled surgery may request transitional in-network coverage, and emergency services are covered at in-network cost-sharing regardless of hospital network status.
