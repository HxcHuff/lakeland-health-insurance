Do You Have to Renew Medicare Every Year? How 2027 Auto-Renewal Works for Original Medicare, Advantage, Part D & Medigap

Wondering if you have to re-apply or renew your Medicare coverage every fall? The short answer is no—your coverage renews automatically on January 1—but what changes inside your plan on autopilot can affect your doctors, prescriptions, and out-of-pocket costs.

Direct Answer: Does Medicare Automatically Renew Every Year?

Direct answer: No, you do not have to re-enroll or renew Medicare every year. Once you are enrolled in Original Medicare (Part A and Part B), a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy, a Medicare Advantage (Part C) plan, or a Medicare Part D prescription drug plan, your coverage automatically renews each year on January 1 as long as you continue paying any required monthly premiums and your private plan continues operating in your service area.

However, automatic renewal only keeps your enrollment active—it does not freeze your private plan's doctor directory, hospital contracts, drug formulary, or copays from year to year.

2027 Auto-Renewal Rules by Medicare Coverage Type (Parts A & B, Medigap, Advantage & Part D)

Each part of Medicare follows different rules on January 1, 2027:

3 Exceptions When Your Medicare Plan Does Not Renew Automatically

There are three situations where a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan will not roll over automatically on January 1:

  1. Plan Non-Renewal Notice (Contract Exit): If your Medicare Advantage or Part D insurer leaves your county or terminates your specific plan contract for 2027, you receive an official non-renewal notice in October. You then have a Special Enrollment Period (and in Florida, a guaranteed-issue right to buy Medigap Plan G or select a new Advantage plan).
  2. Moving Outside Your Plan's Service Area: Medicare Advantage and Part D plans are county- and ZIP-specific. Moving to a new county or state triggers a Special Enrollment Period to select a plan in your new service area (see our Moving to Florida with Medicare guide).
  3. Non-Payment of Premiums or Loss of Part B / Medicaid Status: Failing to pay Part B or plan premiums, or losing Dual-Eligible (Medicaid + Medicare D-SNP) status in Florida MyACCESS, requires action to maintain matching coverage.

Why You Should Still Review Your 2027 ANOC Letter Before December 7

Even though your Medicare Advantage or Part D plan renews automatically, letting it roll into 2027 unreviewed can create expensive surprises in January:

  • Hospital & Clinic Contract Changes: In Polk County, Watson Clinic announced it will not accept Humana in 2027, and Lee Health in Southwest Florida exited UnitedHealthcare for 2027. If you let an affected plan auto-renew, your longtime doctors may be out-of-network on January 1.
  • 2027 Part D $2,400 Cap & Tier Reclassifications: For 2027, the federal Part D out-of-pocket cap for covered prescriptions is $2,400 (up from $2,100 in 2026), and several carriers moved Tier 3 brand-name drugs from flat copays to percentage coinsurance.
  • Dental & OTC Allowance Reductions: Many 2027 Florida Medicare Advantage plans adjusted dental maximums and Over-the-Counter (OTC) card allowances.

During the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 – December 7), you can compare your current plan's 2027 benefits against other options in your ZIP code for a January 1, 2027 effective date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you have to renew Medicare Part A and Part B every year?

No. Original Medicare (Part A and Part B) renews automatically every year as long as your Part B premium is paid (often deducted automatically from Social Security benefits). You never need to re-apply each fall.

Do Medicare Advantage and Part D plans automatically renew for 2027?

Yes, unless your insurer sends a non-renewal notice stating that your specific plan contract is ending on December 31, 2026. However, your plan's doctor network, drug formulary, copays, and extra benefits can change on January 1 even when enrollment auto-renews.

Do I have to renew my Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy every year?

No. Standardized Medigap policies (such as Plan G and Plan N) are guaranteed renewable for life. As long as you pay your monthly premium on time, the insurance company cannot cancel your policy or change your standardized benefits.

What happens if I do nothing during Medicare Open Enrollment (Oct 15 – Dec 7)?

If you take no action between October 15 and December 7 and your plan is continuing in 2027, you will remain enrolled in your current plan on January 1, 2027 with its updated 2027 premiums, network directory, and drug formulary.
