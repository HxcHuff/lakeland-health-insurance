Short answer

Lakeland Regional Health lists labor and delivery suites, a Level III NICU, a Mother-Baby Unit, a pediatric floor with a pediatric ICU, a 33-bed children's emergency department, and an OB emergency department, both open 24/7.

About Carol Jenkins Barnett Pavilion

Look up Carol Jenkins Barnett Pavilion and the specific specialist in your plan's provider directory for the coming year, then call the office with the plan ID. Pediatric specialists can be in a different network than your local doctors.

The Carol Jenkins Barnett Pavilion for Women and Children (1324 Lakeland Hills Blvd) houses the George W. Jenkins Children's Emergency Department (33 beds, 24/7) and a 24/7 Obstetrics (OB) Emergency Department.

How to check pediatric care in a family plan

Yes. Pediatric services, including children's dental and vision care, are essential health benefits in Marketplace plans. Specialist networks vary by plan, so check the pediatric providers you use in the plan's directory.

LRH lists 17 private labor/delivery/recovery suites, 4 C-section suites, a Level III NICU (30 private rooms), a 32-bed Mother-Baby Unit, and a 30-bed pediatric floor with a 12-bed PICU; Nemours surgeons and intensivists care for pediatric surgical patients.

Marketplace plans must cover essential health benefits including emergency services, hospitalization, pregnancy/maternity/newborn care, mental health and substance use disorder services, and pediatric services.

Network status can differ by plan; check the plan's provider directory before enrolling. Use the exact plan ID and coverage year, then call the facility billing office and write down the date and the person you spoke with.

Use the 60-day life-change window, deductible vs. out-of-pocket maximum to gather names, locations, and prescriptions before you compare plans.

Having a baby: the 60-day window

Possibly. Having a baby may qualify your household for a Special Enrollment Period, subject to eligibility, if it happened in the past 60 days; coverage can start the day of the birth. Report the birth to the Marketplace promptly.

Having a baby, adopting, or a foster placement may qualify a household for a Special Enrollment Period, subject to eligibility, if it happened in the past 60 days; coverage can start the day of the event.

What to verify before you enroll

  • Confirm the exact plan ID and year with the billing office
  • Check prescriptions and pharmacy separately from the facility listing
  • Read urgent-care versus emergency-room cost sharing in the Summary of Benefits

Additional verified details
