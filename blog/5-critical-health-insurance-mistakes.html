5 Health Insurance Mistakes to Avoid

Review common health insurance mistakes with educational guidance from a licensed Florida health agent.

Review deductible affordability, provider networks, prescriptions, enrollment timing, and uninsured exposure before choosing coverage.

1. Choosing a Deductible You Could Not Comfortably Meet

A lower premium can shift more cost to the deductible, copays, coinsurance, and annual out-of-pocket limit. Compare plans using both the monthly premium and the amount you could owe during a year with significant care.

  • Review the individual and family deductible.
  • Confirm which services apply before the deductible.
  • Compare the annual in-network out-of-pocket limit.
  • Set aside a realistic reserve for early-year care.

2. Assuming a Doctor or Facility Is In Network

Provider participation can differ by exact plan, not just by carrier name. Verify the doctor, facility, laboratory, and other involved clinicians against the exact plan ID and plan year before enrolling or scheduling non-emergency care.

Keep a record of the directory result and confirm directly with the provider when practical. Directory information is a useful starting point, not a guarantee of claim payment.

3. Skipping the Prescription Review

Check every medication by exact name, dosage, form, and frequency. Review the formulary tier, preferred pharmacy rules, prior authorization, step therapy, quantity limits, and estimated cost under each plan.

A plan with a lower premium may cost more overall if a recurring prescription is excluded or placed on a higher tier.

4. Missing an Enrollment or Documentation Deadline

Open Enrollment, Special Enrollment Periods, Medicare enrollment periods, and employer-coverage transitions follow different rules. Confirm the applicable deadline, requested documents, and effective date using the current official source for the coverage type.

Do not cancel existing coverage until the replacement plan is approved and its effective date is confirmed.

5. Going Without Coverage Without Measuring the Risk

Uninsured exposure can include negotiated-rate differences, emergency and hospital charges, prescription costs, and limited access to routine care. If comprehensive coverage appears unaffordable, compare every available path before deciding, including current Marketplace eligibility, employer or COBRA options, and other coverage for which you may qualify.

Short-term and supplemental products have important exclusions and limitations and do not replace comprehensive major-medical coverage.

A Better Plan-Comparison Checklist

  1. Confirm eligibility and enrollment timing.
  2. Compare annual premium and potential cost sharing.
  3. Verify providers, facilities, prescriptions, and pharmacies.
  4. Read exclusions, prior-authorization rules, and plan limitations.
  5. Confirm the effective date before ending current coverage.
Coverage, eligibility, premiums, provider networks, formularies, and benefits vary by plan, ZIP code, county, and plan year. Verify current plan documents before enrolling or changing coverage.
