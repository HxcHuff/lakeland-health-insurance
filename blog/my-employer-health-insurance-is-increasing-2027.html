My Employer Health Insurance Is Increasing for 2027: What Can I Do?

Employer payroll deductions and deductibles are jumping across the board for 2027 renewals. Before you waive your job's health plan out of frustration, run the exact 10.22% IRS math first, because dropping an "affordable" employer plan blocks Marketplace tax credits.

Direct answer: Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-26, an employer plan is considered unaffordable for 2027 if your required payroll share for the lowest-cost plan exceeds 10.22% of your household MAGI (up from 9.96% in 2026). If your self-only premium crosses 10.22%, you may unlock Marketplace tax credits; if only the family tier crosses 10.22%, your spouse and kids can move to the ACA Marketplace while you stay on the job plan.

The 3 Costly Mistakes Employees Make When Job Premiums Spike

  1. Testing the Wrong Plan Tier: The IRS affordability test uses the lowest-cost minimum-value plan your employer offers, not the richer PPO or buy-up option you currently carry.
  2. Forgetting the Separate Family Test: Even when your self-only payroll deduction is well under 10.22% (say $180/month), adding a spouse and children often jumps to $950-$1,400/month. Under the fixed federal family affordability rule, your spouse and kids are tested on the full family payroll share and can qualify for ACA subsidies separately while you stay on employee-only coverage.
  3. Dropping Job Coverage Mid-Year Voluntarily: Voluntarily cancelling employer coverage outside your employer's open enrollment window does not trigger a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period. Only an unaffordable renewal increase at plan-year renewal, loss of employer contribution, or Open Enrollment (Nov 1 to Jan 15) lets you switch cleanly.

4 Ways to Cut Your 2027 Total Healthcare Spend When Work Rates Jump

  1. Split the Household (Employee on Work Plan, Dependents on ACA / KidCare): Often saves families $400 to $900+ per month when the employer subsidizes employees heavily but barely subsidizes spouses or children.
  2. Check Employer HDHP + Pre-Tax HSA ($4,500 / $9,000 in 2027): If your employer contributes seed money to an HSA and the HDHP premium is much lower, the pre-tax math can beat a high-premium PPO.
  3. Ask HR About ICHRA or QSEHRA Reimbursement: Small and mid-size employers can reimburse individual Marketplace premiums tax-exempt through a CHOICE Arrangement (ICHRA) instead of absorbing double-digit group renewals.
  4. Compare True After-Tax Cost Before Leaving Work Coverage: Employer payroll deductions are usually pre-tax (saving federal income + 7.65% FICA tax), whereas unsubsidized Marketplace plans are paid after-tax unless self-employed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the IRS employer health insurance affordability percentage for 2027?

Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-26, employer coverage is affordable for plan years starting in 2027 if the employee's required contribution for the lowest-cost plan does not exceed 10.22% of household MAGI (up from 9.96% in 2026).

Can I get HealthCare.gov subsidies if my employer plan goes up?

Only if the lowest-cost employer plan offered to you (or your family, for family coverage) exceeds 10.22% of your household MAGI in 2027, or fails the 60% minimum value standard, and your household meets ACA income and tax-filing rules.

Does a mid-year employer premium increase trigger a Special Enrollment Period?

If your employer's non-calendar plan year renews and becomes newly unaffordable above 10.22%, or if your employer stops contributing entirely, that can trigger an ACA SEP. Voluntarily dropping an affordable plan does not.

What if my job plan is affordable for me, but adding my spouse costs $900 a month?

You can keep the affordable employee-only job plan while your spouse and children apply for subsidized Marketplace coverage (or Florida KidCare for eligible children) if the full family payroll deduction exceeds 10.22% of household MAGI.
