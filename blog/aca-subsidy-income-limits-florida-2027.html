How 2027 ACA Income Limits Work in Florida

For the 2027 ACA coverage year, eligibility for Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC) on HealthCare.gov is determined using the 2026 HHS Federal Poverty Guidelines (FPL) (the guidelines in effect when Open Enrollment begins under 26 CFR § 1.36B-1(h)) and the updated IRS contribution table in IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-26.

Because Florida has not expanded Medicaid under the ACA, the primary Marketplace subsidy window for Florida residents runs from 100% of the Federal Poverty Level up to 400% FPL, with extra Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR) available between 100% and 250% FPL when you select a Silver plan.

2027 IRS Expected Contribution Percentages (Rev. Proc. 2026-26)

Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-26, households between 100% and 400% FPL are expected to contribute a sliding percentage of their household income toward the county's Second-Lowest-Cost Silver Plan (benchmark plan):

  • Up to 133% FPL: 2.15% of household income toward the benchmark Silver plan.
  • 133% to 150% FPL: 3.23% to 4.30% of household income.
  • 150% to 200% FPL: 4.30% to 6.78% of household income.
  • 200% to 250% FPL: 6.78% to 8.66% of household income.
  • 250% to 300% FPL: 8.66% to 10.22% of household income.
  • 300% to 400% FPL: Capped at 10.22% of household income toward the benchmark Silver plan.

Your monthly Advance Premium Tax Credit equals the Polk County benchmark Silver premium for your age minus your IRS expected monthly contribution. You can apply that tax credit to any Bronze, Silver, or Gold plan sold on HealthCare.gov.

Navigating the 400% FPL Cliff & Above-the-Line Deductions

Because the temporary ARPA/IRA enhanced subsidy rules expired after 2025, crossing 400% FPL ($63,840 for a single adult or $86,560 for a married couple in 2027) creates a hard eligibility cliff where premium tax credits drop to $0. This is especially critical for pre-Medicare 55-to-64-year-olds in Lakeland and Winter Haven whose unsubsidized benchmark premiums are highest.

If your household is near the 400% FPL threshold or near a Silver CSR cutoff (150%, 200%, or 250% FPL), review legitimate above-the-line tax deductions with your CPA or tax advisor—such as traditional IRA contributions, HSA contributions (when paired with an HSA-eligible Bronze HDHP), SEP-IRA/Solo 401(k) contributions for 1099 workers, and self-employed health insurance deductions—which directly reduce Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum income to qualify for an ACA subsidy in Florida for 2027?

Under the 400% Federal Poverty Level rule for 2027 coverage (using the 2026 HHS poverty guidelines), the maximum projected 2027 MAGI for premium tax credit eligibility is $63,840 for a single person, $86,560 for a household of two, $109,280 for a household of three, and $132,000 for a family of four.

What is the minimum income to qualify for a Marketplace subsidy in Florida for 2027?

Because Florida did not expand Medicaid, adults generally must project household income of at least 100% of the Federal Poverty Level to qualify for Advance Premium Tax Credits on HealthCare.gov—which is $15,960 for one person, $21,640 for two people, $27,320 for three people, and $33,000 for a family of four for 2027 coverage.

What counts as income (MAGI) on a 2027 Florida HealthCare.gov application?

Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) includes Adjusted Gross Income from Form 1040 (wages, salaries, net Schedule C self-employment profit, taxable retirement distributions, unemployment, and capital gains) plus untaxed foreign income, tax-exempt interest, and the non-taxable portion of Social Security benefits.

Why should households under 250% FPL look closely at Silver plans for 2027?

Households between 100% and 250% FPL qualify for federal Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR) that lower deductibles, copays, and out-of-pocket maximums—especially between 100% and 200% FPL (94% and 87% actuarial value tiers). CSR benefits only attach to Silver-tier Marketplace plans, never Bronze or Gold plans.
