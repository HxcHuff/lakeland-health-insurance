Why Health Insurance Costs Went Up in Tampa for 2026

The enhanced premium tax credits in place since the American Rescue Plan in 2021 expired on December 31, 2025. Those credits were reducing what Marketplace enrollees paid out of pocket each month — in many cases by hundreds of dollars.

Without that federal subsidy boost, KFF estimated that subsidized enrollees' average premium payments would more than double (about +114%) if they kept the same plan once enhanced credits expired; KFF later reported actual 2026 average net premium payments rose about 58% among all enrollees as many enrollees switched to lower-premium plans (KFF projection; KFF May 19, 2026). Florida has more ACA Marketplace enrollees than any other state, and Hillsborough County — which includes Tampa, Town 'n' Country, Temple Terrace, and the greater Tampa Bay urban core — has one of the highest concentrations.

On top of the subsidy expiration, Florida regulators approved a 34.1% enrollment-weighted average 2026 increase in individual-market rates before subsidies (Florida OIR, Oct. 16, 2025); your own change may be higher or lower. Hospital cost increases, specialty-medication utilization, and broader healthcare inflation across the Tampa Bay region also contributed.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act — What Tampa Residents Need to Know

Federal legislation signed in 2025 brought additional changes. For Marketplace enrollees in Tampa, the most relevant change is that repayment caps on premium tax credits have been eliminated. If your income fluctuates throughout the year — common for gig workers, rideshare drivers, service industry workers, and self-employed residents throughout Tampa — you could owe back a significant portion of your tax credit at filing time if your actual income exceeds your estimate.

If you or a family member loses CHIP or other minimum essential coverage, that loss may open a 60-day Special Enrollment Period, subject to Marketplace rules and verification.

You Might Qualify for a Special Enrollment Period Right Now

Events that may open a Special Enrollment Period, subject to Marketplace rules, deadlines, and verification, include:

  • Lost job-based coverage (including through reduced hours)
  • Got married
  • Divorce or legal separation that ends your coverage
  • Had or adopted a child
  • Certain permanent moves that change your plan options (prior coverage usually required)
  • Lost CHIP or other minimum essential coverage
  • Aged off a parent's plan at 26
  • COBRA coverage ran out (dropping COBRA or not paying does not qualify)

You generally have 60 days. For most new SEP enrollments, HealthCare.gov verifies eligibility before coverage starts.

New for 2026: Bronze Plans and Health Savings Accounts

For the first time, every Bronze-tier and Catastrophic plan on the Marketplace is HSA-eligible for 2026. This is a meaningful change for Tampa residents who are self-employed, run a small business, or earn too much to qualify for Silver-tier cost-sharing reductions.

An HSA lets you contribute pre-tax dollars, grow them tax-free, and withdraw tax-free for qualified medical expenses, subject to IRS rules. Pairing an HSA with a Bronze plan is one option to compare — not a promise of a lower bill. Savings and subsidy eligibility depend on income, household and plan; confirm at HealthCare.gov.

FAQ — Health Insurance in Tampa, FL

How much did ACA health insurance go up in Tampa for 2026?

The enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025. Florida regulators approved a 34.1% average 2026 increase in individual-market rates before subsidies (Florida OIR), and the expiration of enhanced tax credits raised what many enrollees pay after subsidies.

Possibly. A qualifying life event such as loss of other coverage, marriage, a birth or adoption, or certain moves may open a 60-day Special Enrollment Period, subject to Marketplace rules, deadlines, and verification.
