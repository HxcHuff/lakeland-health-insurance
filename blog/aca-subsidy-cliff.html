ACA Subsidy Cliff in 2026: Eligibility and Tax Reconciliation

By David | Licensed FL Agent #W371813 | Published December 15, 2025 | Updated August 12, 2026
Home > Blog > ACA Subsidy Cliff

Current review: The temporary enhanced premium tax credit rules ended after 2025. For 2026, household income is generally limited to no more than 400% of the federal poverty line for premium tax credit eligibility, and excess advance credits are no longer subject to a repayment cap. Eligibility and reconciliation remain household- and tax-return-specific.

I. What the 2026 Income Limit Means

The IRS states that, in general, a tax household may qualify for the premium tax credit when household income is at least 100% and no more than 400% FPL. Other requirements also apply, including Marketplace enrollment, tax-filing rules, and lack of eligibility for certain other minimum essential coverage.

The temporary 2021–2025 rule allowed some otherwise eligible households above 400% FPL to receive a credit. That rule expired after 2025. A household above the current limit generally cannot claim a 2026 premium tax credit, but a generic dollar example cannot determine a specific household's result.

II. How the 2026 Credit Is Calculated

The premium tax credit depends on the household's applicable percentage, the premium for the second-lowest-cost Silver plan available to the tax family, and the months and people eligible for the credit. IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-25 sets the 2026 applicable-percentage table from 2.10% below 133% FPL to 9.96% from 300% through 400% FPL.

Do Not Treat the FPL Boundary as a Quote

FPL dollar amounts vary by family size and coverage year, while benchmark premiums vary by age and location. Confirm the current Marketplace eligibility result instead of relying on a prior-year article, a rounded threshold, or another household's premium.

III. Reconciliation: 2025 and 2026 Are Different

Advance premium tax credits are reconciled on IRS Form 8962. The final tax calculation compares advance payments made for the coverage with the premium tax credit allowed using the tax return information.

Repayment Rules by Tax Year

Tax year IRS treatment of excess advance credit
2025 Below 400% FPL, repayment limits may apply based on household income and filing status. The single-filer table lists $375, $975, and $1,625 across its three bands. At 400% FPL or above, there is no 2025 repayment limitation.
2026 There is no repayment cap. The full amount by which advance payments exceed the allowed credit is added to tax liability.

If advance payments were less than the allowed credit, the tax filer may claim the difference. If the tax return shows a balance that cannot be paid in full, IRS payment options may be available. A licensed insurance agent cannot determine federal tax liability.

IV. Estimating Marketplace Household Income

Marketplace household income uses a specific modified adjusted gross income definition. It starts with federal adjusted gross income and includes certain additional amounts described by HealthCare.gov and IRS guidance. Household composition and tax-filing relationships also matter.

Do Not Change Facts to Reach an Eligibility Result

Use a reasonable estimate based on current, documented information. Do not omit income, alter household members, or change tax-filing assumptions to obtain a credit. Questions about retirement contributions, capital gains, business deductions, or other tax planning belong with a qualified tax professional.

V. Practical Review Controls

  • Verify consumer consent. The consumer or properly authorized representative should review the eligibility information before submission.
  • Use current records. Consider current pay, self-employment information, expected household changes, and other known income sources.
  • Report material changes. Update the Marketplace when income or household information changes so prospective advance credits can be recalculated.
  • Retain confirmations. Keep eligibility notices, application-change confirmations, and Forms 1095-A without sending sensitive records through unsecured channels.
  • Separate insurance and tax advice. An agent can explain Marketplace plan choices; a qualified tax professional should address MAGI treatment and Form 8962.

VI. Primary Sources

  • IRS: Questions and Answers on the Premium Tax Credit
  • IRS: 2025 Instructions for Form 8962
  • IRS Fact Sheet 2025-10
  • IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-25
  • HealthCare.gov: What to Include as Income
  • HealthCare.gov: Report Income and Household Changes

Professional Takeaway

For 2026, verify the current household information, review the Marketplace eligibility determination, and update material changes. Do not use a prior-year FPL figure or a hypothetical subsidy amount as a substitute for the current application and federal tax guidance.
