Does Medicare Cover Nursing Homes or Assisted Living in 2027?

One of the biggest financial shocks families face is learning at hospital discharge that Medicare does not pay for assisted living or long-term nursing home room and board. Here are the exact 2027 CMS Skilled Nursing Facility rules, the "observation status" trap, and how Medigap Plan G/N protects Days 21 to 100.

Direct answer: No for long-term custodial care or assisted living. Original Medicare covers only short-term Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF) rehab after a qualifying 3-day inpatient hospital stay: in 2027, Days 1 to 20 cost $0/day, Days 21 to 100 cost $222/day coinsurance (up to $17,760 out of pocket without Medigap), and Days 101+ are 100% your responsibility.

The Hospital "Observation Status" Trap That Costs Families Thousands

To unlock Original Medicare's 100-day Skilled Nursing Facility benefit, you must have a medically necessary 3-consecutive-day inpatient hospital stay (not counting the day of discharge). Even if you sleep in a hospital bed for three nights, if the hospital billed your stay as outpatient observation instead of formally admitting you as an inpatient, Original Medicare will not cover your Skilled Nursing rehab afterward.

What about Medicare Advantage? Many Medicare Advantage plans waive the 3-day hospital stay rule, but require prior authorization before SNF transfer and charge daily copays (exact amounts vary by plan, up to your plan's annual MOOP) and periodic medical-necessity reviews to continue rehab days.

4 Questions Every Family Should Ask Before Hospital Discharge

  1. Ask on Day 1: "Am I Admitted Inpatient or Under Observation?" Hospitals must give Medicare patients the MOON notice (Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice) after 24 hours of observation - ask the attending physician immediately if inpatient criteria are met.
  2. Confirm the Rehab Facility Is Medicare-Certified (and In-Network for MA): Original Medicare + Medigap works at any Medicare-certified SNF nationwide; Medicare Advantage requires verifying network status and securing prior authorization.
  3. Know Your Hospital & SNF Discharge Appeal Rights: Every Medicare patient has the right to request a fast-track appeal through the Beneficiary and Family Centered Care Quality Improvement Organization (BFCC-QIO) before being discharged prematurely.
  4. Plan Separately for Long-Term Custodial Care: Because neither Medicare nor Medigap pays for assisted living or long-term nursing home custodial stays, families evaluate personal savings, standalone long-term care / hybrid life policies, VA Aid & Attendance, or state Medicaid planning well before a crisis.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is Medicare's Skilled Nursing Facility coinsurance in 2027?

Under the official 2027 CMS rates, Original Medicare covers Days 1 to 20 at $0 per day and charges $222 per day for Days 21 through 100 per benefit period (up from $217/day in 2026).

Does Medicare pay for assisted living facilities or memory care?

No. Medicare does not pay for assisted living room and board, memory care rent, or long-term custodial help with activities of daily living like bathing and dressing, though Part B still covers medically necessary doctor visits and outpatient therapy while living there.

Does Medigap Plan G cover Skilled Nursing Facility stays?

Yes, for Medicare-approved skilled nursing stays: Medigap Plan G and Plan N pay 100% of the 2027 $222/day coinsurance for Days 21 through 100 per benefit period. Neither covers custodial care or days past Day 100.

Why did Medicare deny my nursing home rehab after 3 nights in the hospital?

The most common reason is outpatient observation status: nights spent under observation do not count toward Original Medicare's 3-day inpatient hospital rule required for SNF Part A coverage.
