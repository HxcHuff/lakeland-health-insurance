How Two Plans From the Same Insurance Company Can Have Completely Different Hospitals

When you sort 2027 plans on HealthCare.gov from lowest monthly premium to highest, the top two or three results often show the exact same insurance carrier logo with slightly different words in the plan title—such as Value, Select, Core, Signature, myBlue, or BlueOptions.

Many families naturally click the one that saves $2 to $15 a month, assuming every plan from that insurance company uses the same doctor and hospital list. In 2027 across Polk County, Tampa Bay, and Greater Orlando, that assumption is the #1 cause of surprise non-emergency hospital denials. One sub-network may cover Lakeland Regional Health while excluding Orlando Health Lakeland Highlands Hospital, or cover HCA Florida while excluding AdventHealth or BayCare.

How to Spot a Narrow Sub-Network Before You Click Enroll

  1. Check the Exact Network Name—Not Just the Carrier Name: Look at the sub-network label underneath the plan title and verify both your primary hospital and your doctor’s 10-digit NPI on that specific sub-network.
  2. Don’t Let HealthCare.gov Auto-Renew You Blindly: If your 2026 plan changed sub-network contracts or if you are being cross-walked after Cigna or Molina exited the Florida Marketplace, HealthCare.gov matches you by metal tier and price—not by which hospital you use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the same insurance company offer two 2027 Silver plans with different hospital networks in the same Florida ZIP code?

Carriers file multiple HMO and EPO sub-networks in the same county so they can offer a lower-priced narrow-network option alongside a broader network option. Even when the monthly premiums differ by only a few dollars, one sub-network may exclude major local hospitals or multi-specialty clinics.

What happens if I need a scheduled surgery or MRI at a hospital that is out of network on my narrow HMO plan?

On an HMO or EPO plan without out-of-network benefits, non-emergency scheduled procedures, imaging, and inpatient admissions at an out-of-network hospital are generally not covered and do not count toward your in-network deductible or out-of-pocket maximum, unless a formal network gap exception or continuity-of-care transition is approved in advance.

Are emergency room visits still covered if I accidentally picked a narrow sub-network plan?

Yes. Under the federal No Surprises Act, emergency room evaluation and stabilization at any hospital emergency department must be processed at in-network cost-sharing levels regardless of which sub-network you selected.
