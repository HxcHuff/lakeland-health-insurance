Can I keep my doctor if I switch Medicare plans?

Maybe. The answer depends on whether you use Original Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medigap, or Part D, and whether your doctor accepts the exact plan you are considering.

Direct answer: If you switch to or between Medicare Advantage plans, you must verify your doctor, hospital, specialists, prescriptions, and pharmacy under the exact plan ID. Original Medicare generally gives broader access to doctors and hospitals that accept Medicare, while Medicare Advantage plans commonly use networks.

Do not rely only on a provider directory search. Confirm with the plan and the provider's billing office before you enroll.

What to check before switching

  • The exact plan name and plan ID, not just the carrier name.
  • Your primary doctor, specialists, hospital, and preferred pharmacy.
  • Whether referrals, prior authorization, or out-of-network rules apply.
  • All prescriptions by name, dosage, quantity, and preferred pharmacy.
  • Whether your doctor is accepting new patients under that plan.

Why carrier name is not enough

A carrier may offer several Medicare Advantage plans in the same county. A doctor can participate with one plan and not another. Provider participation can also differ between HMO, PPO, and special needs plan structures.

Networks can change

Medicare Advantage provider networks can change during the year. That is why annual plan reviews should include doctor, hospital, pharmacy, and prescription checks instead of only premium and extra benefits.

FAQ

Can I keep my doctor if I switch Medicare plans?

It depends on the coverage path and exact plan. Medicare Advantage uses plan networks; Original Medicare generally allows any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare.

Can Medicare Advantage provider networks change?

Yes. Networks can change, so verify doctor access before enrolling and review it again each year.
