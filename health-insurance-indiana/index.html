Questions Indiana shoppers ask

Where do Indiana residents sign up for Marketplace coverage in 2027?

Indiana uses HealthCare.gov, the federal Marketplace. You can apply on HealthCare.gov, with help from a licensed agent registered for the plan year, or through an authorized enrollment site. Premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions apply only to plans bought through the Marketplace.

What are the 2027 open enrollment dates in Indiana?

HealthCare.gov Open Enrollment for 2027 runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. December 15, 2026 is the deadline for coverage starting January 1, 2027; selections from December 16 through January 15 generally begin February 1.

Can an agent in Lakeland, Florida help me with an Indiana plan?

Yes, if the agent holds an active Indiana insurance license and current HealthCare.gov registration. I hold an Indiana nonresident license #3529184. Reviews happen by phone and screen share from Lakeland, Florida. You keep your HealthCare.gov login and authorize every change.

What does a plan review cost?

There is no separate agent fee for the plan-review assistance described on this site. If you enroll in a plan, the insurer may pay me a commission. I compare plans from the insurers I'm appointed with in Indiana, which may not include every insurer on HealthCare.gov.

How do the Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP) and HealthCare.gov fit together?

Indiana expanded Medicaid under the Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP) for eligible adults ages 19 to 64 with household incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level. You can apply year-round via fssabenefits.in.gov, and your state Medicaid agency makes the final eligibility decision. Above 138% up to 400% FPL, HealthCare.gov determines Marketplace premium tax credit eligibility.

Why should Indiana shoppers check IU Health, Franciscan, or Ascension networks for 2027?

Hospital participation varies by specific 2027 Marketplace plan ID and HMO/EPO/PPO network tier. Before letting a 2026 policy auto-renew, check each doctor, outpatient clinic, prescription tier, and your projected 2027 income—especially because excess advance premium tax credits no longer have a federal repayment cap after 2025.
