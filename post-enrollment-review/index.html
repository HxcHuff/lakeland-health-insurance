Service-first

Not every service request is a sales opportunity. David will first identify the service issue, the responsible carrier or Marketplace channel, and the practical next step.

A service review does not imply that a different plan or additional product is needed.

What helps

Current plan, effective date, invoice or ID-card status, provider or prescription issue, and any Marketplace document request.

Do not send full medical records, payment-card data, or unnecessary personal identifiers. David can explain the minimum information needed for the specific issue.

First coverage checks

Confirm that the initial premium was received, the effective date is correct, ID cards are available, and the selected primary-care provider is recorded when the plan requires one. Keep enrollment confirmation and carrier documents together.

Provider and prescription use

Reconfirm the exact provider location and prescription formulary before non-urgent care. Use the provider and prescription workflow for a focused check, or the current-client review for later annual changes.

If a carrier record conflicts with the Marketplace or enrollment confirmation, keep copies of both and contact the responsible service channel. Record confirmation numbers, document dates, and the next required action. A submitted application is not the same as effectuated coverage.

For claim, authorization, billing, or urgent access questions, use the member-service number on the current ID card and follow the plan's formal process. David can help organize the coverage question, but the carrier and plan documents control benefits and claim decisions.

Common requests

  • ID cards or first premium.
  • Provider or prescription confirmation.
  • Effective date and Marketplace documents.
  • Dental, vision, supplemental, or life-insurance review when appropriate.

Urgent care-access issues should be handled by calling directly.
