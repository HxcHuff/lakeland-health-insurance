Campaign Strategy & Execution Plan

Lakeland Health Insurance | Lead Generation & Pixel Training | April 2026

1. Campaign Overview

Primary Objective
Lead generation + pixel training across three audience segments to build historical conversion data and retargeting audiences.
Campaign Duration
6-8 Weeks
Three sequential phases with built-in optimization cycles.
Total Budget
$30-150/day
Phased allocation with scaling as winners emerge from Phase 1 testing.
Target Segments
ACA/Subsidy (self-employed, gig workers, families) | Medicare (63-67, adult children) | Uninsured/Gap (25-64)
Campaign Positioning
This campaign uses educational content (blog posts) as the conversion driver. Each segment receives dedicated messaging that addresses their primary pain point: missed subsidies (ACA), complexity/mistakes (Medicare), or affordability barriers (Uninsured). Success depends on high-quality content, precise audience targeting, and rapid iteration on messaging.

2. Phase Breakdown & Timeline

1
Content A/B Test
Weeks 1-2 | Budget: $30-50/day
Goal: Identify which blog content resonates most with each segment and validate messaging angles before scaling.
  • Audience: Broad audience per segment (minimal exclusions)
  • Ad Creative: 2 variants per segment (6 total ad creatives)
  • Landing Experience: Direct to blog post with /get-help/ CTA in blog
  • Metrics Focus: CTR, Time on Page (TOS), Scroll Depth
  • Duration: Run for 10-14 days with daily budget cap to spread impressions evenly
  • Success Criteria: CTR > 2%, CPC < $1.50, TOS > 90 seconds
Output: Identify top-performing creative variant per segment + blog post engagement insights.
2
Funnel Flow Test
Weeks 3-4 | Budget: $50-80/day
Goal: Test three funnel flows to identify optimal path to lead capture and maximize conversion rates.
  • Winning Creative: Use top 2 blog posts from Phase 1 (best CTR/TOS per segment cluster)
  • Three Funnel Variants:
    • Flow A (Blog > CTA in Blog): Direct to blog, /get-help/ CTA, forms on /get-help/ page
    • Flow B (Blog > Dedicated LP > Form): Blog → dedicated landing page → form capture
    • Flow C (Direct to Form): Ad → Dedicated form page, minimal intermediate steps
  • Audience Refinement: Use Phase 1 pixel data to retarget warm audiences + expand lookalike
  • Metrics Focus: CVR (lead submission rate), CPL (cost per lead)
  • Duration: 10-14 days, equal budget split across flows
  • Success Criteria: CVR > 3%, CPL < $25 on winning flow
Output: Winning funnel flow per segment + optimized landing page for Phase 3 scaling.
3
Scale Winner + Retarget + Lookalike
Weeks 5+ | Budget: $100-150/day
Goal: Maximize lead volume from winning creative + funnel while building robust retargeting and lookalike audiences for long-term growth.
  • Scale Primary Campaign: Winner from Phase 2 funnel at 2-3x daily budget
  • Retargeting Campaigns: 4 custom audiences (see Retargeting Strategy section)
    • Website visitors (no conversion)
    • Blog readers (engaged but no form submission)
    • Form abandoners
    • Low-intent viewers
  • Lookalike Expansion: 1% and 5% lookalike audiences seeded from converted leads
  • Metrics Focus: CPL (target < $20 on primary), ROAS on retargeting
  • Duration: Ongoing, continuous optimization
Output: Repeatable, profitable lead generation funnel with trained pixel audiences.

4. UTM Taxonomy & Tracking Structure

UTM Parameter Definitions

utm_source
Value: facebook
Purpose: Identifies paid Facebook as the traffic source. Allows distinction from organic social, referrals, or direct traffic.
utm_medium
Value: paid
Purpose: Classifies traffic as paid advertising (vs. organic, email, etc.). Used for ROI calculations and channel attribution.
utm_campaign
Value: blog_ab_p1_apr2026
Purpose: Identifies the campaign cohort, test phase, and month. Breakdown: blog_ab = blog A/B test, p1 = Phase 1, apr2026 = April 2026.
utm_content
Value: Variant + segment identifier. E.g., aca_subsidy_v1, medicare_mistakes_v2, uninsured_er_v1
Purpose: Tracks which ad creative variant performed best within each segment. Critical for Phase 1 testing comparisons.
utm_term
Value: Audience/interest identifier. E.g., aca_interest_fl, turning_65_fl, uninsured_fl
Purpose: Allows grouping of traffic by audience segment for cohort analysis and lookalike modeling.

7. Retargeting Strategy

Retargeting Campaign Structure

Campaign 1: Website Visitor Awareness (Broad Retargeting)
Audience: All site visitors, last 30 days (exclude converts)
Creative: Carousel ads showcasing segment benefits (e.g., "3 Ways We Help Self-Employed Floridians Save on Health Insurance")
Budget: $10-15/day
Objective: Re-engage passive visitors, build familiarity, drive return to blog/LP
CPL Target: $35-45 (lower conversion rate, broad audience)
Campaign 2: Blog Reader Warm Retargeting
Audience: Blog readers (ViewContent event), last 14 days per segment
Creative: Segment-specific CTAs (e.g., "Ready for a Free Medicare Review?" for Medicare readers)
Budget: $20-25/day (split across 3 segment campaigns)
Objective: Convert warm, engaged readers to form submission
CPL Target: $15-20 (highest intent segment)
Campaign 3: Form Abandoner Recovery
Audience: Form viewers/interactors without conversion, last 7 days
Creative: Reassurance + urgency ("Your Free Consultation Takes 2 Minutes—No Obligation")
Budget: $5-10/day
Objective: Recover lost conversions from high-intent users
CPL Target: $10-15 (very high intent; should convert at 5-10% rate)
Campaign 4: Lookalike Prospecting (Phase 3+)
Audience: 1% lookalike (primary) and 5% lookalike (secondary) seeded from converted leads (last 90 days)
Creative: Winning primary campaign ad creative from Phase 2
Budget: $30-40/day (Phase 3+)
Objective: Acquire new high-quality prospects similar to best customers
CPL Target: $25-30 (lookalike quality degrades with expansion; 1% < 5%)

Retargeting Frequency & Ad Rotation

Frequency Capping
Set frequency cap at 3-5 impressions per user per day to avoid ad fatigue. Retargeting audiences are smaller (500-2,000), so capping ensures sustainable reach and prevents rising CPL from saturation.
Seasonal & Performance Adjustments
ACA: Increase retargeting budget Oct-Dec (open enrollment peak). Reduce or pause Jun-Aug (low volume).
Medicare: High volume Oct-Dec (AEP) and Jan-Mar (SEP eligibility). Steady May-Sep.
Uninsured: Higher post-life-event (job loss, divorce, move). Less seasonal than ACA/Medicare.

8. Compliance & Regulatory Requirements

ACA / Subsidy Messaging

Key Compliance Points
No Guarantee of Savings: All ACA ads must include a disclaimer stating "Actual subsidy amounts depend on household income, family size, and current year Federal Poverty Level guidelines. Check Healthcare.gov or consult with a broker for personalized estimates."
Accurate Income Thresholds: Current 2026 guidelines: 100%-400% FPL qualifies for APTC (Advanced Premium Tax Credit). For a family of 4 in 2026, 400% FPL ≈ $118,000. Our ads state "<$125K" which is conservative and compliant.
Subsidy Percentages: Claims of "50-80% savings" or "$700+/month subsidies" must be supported by actual plan data in your market. These figures are typical for lower-income households but not guaranteed. Include "typical" or "based on 2025 data" language.
Marketplace Neutrality: No steering toward specific plans or carriers. Reference "plans from all major carriers" and direct to Healthcare.gov or neutral comparison tools.

Medicare Messaging

CMS Marketing Guidelines (42 CFR 422.2260)
No Unsolicited Contact: Do not make unsolicited calls or in-person visits to beneficiaries. This campaign uses passive digital advertising (Facebook), which is compliant.
No Misleading Comparisons: Ads must not compare Medicare Advantage unfavorably to Original Medicare or vice versa. Our messaging focuses on "three common mistakes" (neutral) rather than "Original Medicare is bad."
Accurate Part B Penalty Statement: "One wrong move during enrollment and you pay a penalty for the rest of your life" refers to the 10% per-12-month late enrollment penalty on Part B premiums. This is accurate; include link to Medicare.gov for details.
Licensed Agent Requirement: All lead follow-up must be conducted by licensed insurance agents. Display agent license prominently in follow-up materials.
Annual Attestation: Ensure all ads and landing pages are reviewed and attested by compliance team before launch. Update annually.

State of Florida Compliance (DOI)

Florida Department of Insurance (DOI) Requirements
Agent License Display: All customer-facing materials must display responsible agent/agency FL License #. Include on landing pages, email signatures, and follow-up documents.
Privacy & Consent: Collect explicit opt-in for email/SMS prior to contact. Form should include checkbox: "I consent to be contacted by phone, text, or email about my insurance options."
No Deceptive Practices: Ads and landing pages must not misrepresent coverage, benefits, or obligations. All claims must be substantiated and accurate.
Prohibition on High-Pressure Sales: Marketing must not use high-pressure tactics (countdown timers, fake urgency beyond genuine enrollment windows, misleading stock indicators). This campaign uses educational, low-pressure positioning.

General Advertising Standards

FTC Endorsement & Testimonial Guides
No Fake Reviews: Do not create or use fake customer testimonials. Any testimonials must be from real customers with documented consent.
Disclosure of Incentives: If you offer incentives for leads/referrals, clearly disclose "In exchange for a referral, we offer..." in marketing materials.
Clear Disclaimers: All material limitations, exclusions, and disclaimers should be clearly visible (not hidden in fine print or requiring scroll).

Implementation Checklist

□ Obtain legal review of all ad copy and landing page messaging before launch
□ Display FL Insurance Agent License # on all customer-facing pages
□ Include explicit consent checkbox for contact (email, phone, SMS)
□ Add ACA subsidy disclaimer: "Actual subsidies vary by income and family size..."
□ Link Medicare content to Medicare.gov for official information
□ Ensure ER cost data is cited from reputable source (hospital billing data, health plan claims)
□ Train all agents/brokers on policy regarding lead follow-up timing and pressure
□ Document all campaign changes and approvals in compliance folder
