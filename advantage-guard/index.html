AdvantageGuard Hospital Indemnity

AdvantageGuard pays limited, fixed cash benefits tied to covered hospital stays. It is not a Medicare Supplement, not Medicare Advantage, not major medical, and not Minimum Essential Coverage.

What AdvantageGuard is

AdvantageGuard is hospital indemnity insurance underwritten by Golden Rule Insurance Company, a UnitedHealthcare Individual & Family company. Current materials describe it as fixed-benefit coverage for hospital stays: if a covered confinement or listed event occurs, the policy may pay a set cash amount.

That cash can help with deductibles, copays, travel, or other costs that remain after a hospital stay. It does not turn the policy into major medical insurance, a Medicare Supplement, or a Medicare Advantage plan.

Optional ancillary riders may be available at application, such as benefits related to cancer, wellness, emergency-room or urgent-care visits, skilled nursing, outpatient surgery, diagnostics, ambulance, or certain outpatient provider-administered prescription drugs. Rider availability, waiting periods, and amounts vary. Confirm the current Florida offering before treating any rider as part of a plan.

How the hospital benefit pays

You do not need a full dollar schedule to understand the design.

1

A covered hospital event occurs

The stay or listed service must be covered under the policy that is issued.

2

The policy pays the scheduled amount

The payment is a stated cash benefit, subject to day limits and other policy rules.

3

You still handle the medical bill

If the hospital charge is higher than the benefit, you may owe the difference.

Current product materials describe a range of daily confinement amounts and maximum-day choices that are selected when applying. Any dollar range discussed in a review is illustrative only and is subject to current Florida terms and the issued policy. It is not a quote.

Who it may fit

AdvantageGuard may be worth comparing if you are between ages 60 and 90, already have Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Marketplace, employer, or other comprehensive coverage, and want a separate cash benefit if a covered hospital stay happens.

It is a weaker fit if you are looking for a Medicare Supplement, a Medicare Advantage plan, ACA-compliant major medical coverage, or a policy for dependent children. The primary insured must be 60 or older. A spouse may also be eligible under current materials; confirm household eligibility on the current Florida application.

How hospital indemnity differs from Medigap and Medicare Advantage

Medicare Supplement or Medicare Advantage

  • Medigap helps with cost sharing on services Medicare already covers.
  • Medicare Advantage is a Medicare health plan that delivers Part A and Part B benefits, and often Part D.
  • Those products are Medicare options. They are not hospital indemnity policies.

AdvantageGuard hospital indemnity

  • Pays a stated cash amount for a covered hospital stay or other listed event.
  • Does not replace Medicare, Medigap, Medicare Advantage, or ACA coverage.
  • Does not make the policy comprehensive, and it is not Minimum Essential Coverage.

If you are comparing Medicare choices, start with the Medicare overview. If you are comparing scheduled medical-service benefits rather than hospital-stay cash benefits, see Health ProtectorGuard.

What to verify before applying

  • Florida availability. Current product materials list Florida, but the offering still must be confirmed for your ZIP code and the current application.
  • Benefit choices. Daily confinement amounts, maximum days, and any riders are selected at application. Review the current schedule rather than assuming a dollar amount from this page.
  • Underwriting path. Current materials say coverage may be guaranteed issue or simplified issue, depending on the benefits chosen.
  • Waiting periods. Many standard benefits are described as having no waiting period. Some riders, including cancer, wellness, and outpatient provider-administered prescription-drug benefits, can have a waiting period.
  • Pre-existing-condition rules. Current materials describe a limitation that can apply after coverage begins. The issued policy controls the lookback and the limitation period.
  • How it sits beside coverage you already have. Confirm that you are adding a limited cash benefit, not replacing Medicare, Medigap, Medicare Advantage, or other comprehensive coverage.
  • Exclusions and Florida form rules. The policy has exclusions and state variations. Current Florida materials use form HI-GRI-FL. The issued policy controls.

This page does not publish premiums, county-level availability, or network rules. Those items must be confirmed from the current Florida offering. Current materials emphasize scheduled cash benefits for covered events rather than a medical-provider network.

Frequently asked questions

Is AdvantageGuard a Medicare Supplement or Medicare Advantage plan?

No. AdvantageGuard is a limited-benefit hospital indemnity policy. Current carrier materials say it is not a Medicare Supplement policy and does not fully supplement Medicare or a private Medicare Advantage plan.

Is AdvantageGuard major medical or Minimum Essential Coverage?

No. It is a supplement to health insurance and is not a substitute for the Minimum Essential Coverage required by the ACA. It should not replace comprehensive health insurance.

Who may apply for AdvantageGuard?

Current product materials list issue ages 60 through 90 and require the primary insured to be age 60 or older. A spouse may also be eligible. Dependent children are not eligible. Florida availability still must be verified.

Will AdvantageGuard pay my full hospital bill?

Not necessarily. The policy pays a stated cash amount for a covered hospital stay or other listed event. If the medical bill is higher than the benefit, you may still owe the difference.

Is there a waiting period?

Current product materials say there is no waiting period for many standard plan benefits. Waiting periods can apply to some optional riders, including cancer, wellness, and outpatient provider-administered prescription-drug benefits. The issued policy controls.
