Why 2027 Marketplace reviews include a phone or email verification step

Following the CMS Marketplace anti-fraud actions announced in September 2026, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) added stronger identity and consumer-authorization safeguards on the Federal Marketplace.

These rules protect your household from unauthorized agent switches. They mean that even when you ask a trusted, licensed agent like David Huff to review or update your 2027 application, the system requires you to actively confirm that permission on your own phone or email before any application change can take place.

Frequently asked questions

Why did I receive a one-time link or code during my Marketplace review?

CMS added electronic consumer authorization so an agent or broker cannot access or update a HealthCare.gov application without your active approval. When you work with a licensed agent, a one-time link or security code is sent to your phone or email and typically expires after 15 minutes.

Does the Marketplace identity check affect my credit score?

No. The Marketplace uses identity-verification questions (often powered by Experian identity proofing) to confirm you are the person requesting access. It is an identity check, not a credit application, and it does not affect your credit score.

Should I ever share my HealthCare.gov password with an agent?

No. Never share your HealthCare.gov username or password with anyone. You keep your own login credentials, and you only approve an agent through the official authorization step during a scheduled review.

Do I need to enter my Social Security Number on this website to request a review?

No. You never need to enter a Social Security Number on this website to request a plan comparison or schedule a call with David Huff. CMS requires verifiable applicant information on the official Marketplace application itself when you decide to apply.
